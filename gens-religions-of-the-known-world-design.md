# GENS — Reference Document: Religions of the Known World
*An extension of Religion's own brief foreign-cult section into a full, CK3-style catalog, built on a deliberate, historically honest departure from CK3's own model: almost no ancient religion in this period had a centralized "Head of Faith," and this document says so directly. This pass fills in every Cultures-of-the-Known-World entry that hadn't yet received its own faith writeup (Thracian, Nubian/Kushite, Illyrian/Pannonian, Cappadocian, and more), adds Serapis — a real, documented case of deliberately engineered religious syncretism tying directly into the Alexandrian Greek culture entry — Samaritanism, Artemis of Ephesus, the real older oracle sites at Dodona and Siwa, and a consolidated Holy Sites table.*

## 1. Scope & Role

Religion's own §7 already named five foreign cults in passing; Cultures of the Known World sketched a one-line "religious tendency" for every culture on its own thirty-six-entry roster. This document is where both threads get their full, real content — every faith named or implied elsewhere in this project, catalogued with real doctrinal color and a real tenet system, without inventing anything Rome's actual religious landscape didn't have. No new mechanics: every faith below plugs into Religion's own existing Divine Favor, Patron Deity, Household Doctrine, and Priesthood machinery.

## 3. Real Tenets — A Flavor Layer on Existing Mechanics

Nine tenets from the first pass, plus two new this pass to handle honest gaps in the historical record and one genuinely unique real case:

| Tenet | What it reads against | Real grounding |
|---|---|---|
| **Syncretic** | Near-zero Household Doctrine suppression when adopted alongside another faith | Most ancient polytheisms genuinely didn't mind multi-faith worship |
| **Exclusive** | Real Household Doctrine suppression and Traditionalist backlash risk | Judaism and Christianity, real exceptions to the ancient default |
| **State-Sponsored** | Direct interface with Religion's Priesthood track and Events' Prominence | Roman, Hellenic-under-Rome, Egyptian, Parthian religion |
| **Initiatory** | A real, one-time cost to formally join | Real historical mystery cults required a secret initiation rite |
| **Persecution-Prone** | Direct interface with Religion's persecution-risk mechanic and Proscription | Christianity specifically, Judaism at moments of open revolt |
| **Martial-Coded** | A real Domus Bellatrix Doctrine affinity | Germanic paganism, Mithraism among soldiers, Dacian and Thracian religion |
| **Oracular** | A real, distinct "Consult the Oracle" action requiring Travel to a named site | Delphi, Dodona, Siwa, and Egyptian religion's own oracular tradition |
| **Fire-Venerating** | Distinct ritual flavor only | Parthian and Armenian Zoroastrian-adjacent tradition |
| **Charitable/Communal** | A real affinity with General Amnesty and Debt Bondage Ban Edicts | Judaism and Christianity's real, documented communal-welfare practices |
| **Thin-Record** *(new)* | No mechanical effect — an honest content flag | Marks a faith this document deliberately doesn't over-specify because the real surviving historical record genuinely doesn't support confident, granular detail (Numidian, Sarmatian, Illyrian/Pannonian) |
| **Deliberately-Syncretic** *(new)* | A real, distinct origin story worth its own flavor text rather than a mechanic | Marks the rare case of a faith genuinely engineered on purpose for political reasons, rather than blending organically over time — Serapis (§6) is this tag's own defining example |

## 7. The Monotheistic Exception — Judaism, Samaritanism & Early Christianity

### 7.1 Judaism *(unchanged)*

Centered on the real Temple in Jerusalem until its actual AD 70 destruction; a real, profound religious rupture alongside the political one. **Tenets:** Exclusive, Charitable/Communal.

### 7.2 Samaritanism *(new)*

A real, distinct faith closely related to Judaism but genuinely, not merely nominally, separate — centered on **Mount Gerizim** rather than Jerusalem's Temple, and carrying real, documented theological and scriptural differences from the mainstream Judaean tradition it's historically often casually lumped together with. Cultures of the Known World already names this community as a real, distinct minority within Judaea (§6.1 of that document); this is that entry's own full faith treatment, kept deliberately separate from Judaism's own entry rather than folded into it, exactly the way the real historical relationship between the two actually works. **Tenets:** Exclusive.

### 7.3 Early Christianity

Unchanged in its core historical grounding — the real ministry and execution of Jesus of Nazareth under Roman authority, traditionally dated c. AD 30–33, and Tacitus's own real, documented account of Nero blaming Christians for the Great Fire of Rome (AD 64). **New this pass, a real and important nuance:** this document doesn't treat "early Christianity" as a single, monolithic movement, since it genuinely wasn't one during this game's own range — a real, diverse range of early Christian and Christian-adjacent movements, including what modern scholarship broadly terms **Gnostic** currents, existed alongside what later became the mainstream tradition, genuinely contesting and debating questions of doctrine throughout the 2nd century AD specifically. This document doesn't need to resolve or take a side in that real historical diversity to acknowledge it exists — a household adopting this faith during the relevant window is adopting something real people at the time were still actively arguing about the shape of. **Tenets:** Exclusive, Persecution-Prone, Charitable/Communal.

## 8. No Head of Faith — A Deliberate Departure *(unchanged)*

Rome had no Church; most polytheisms had no central authority at all; Judaism centered on the Temple until AD 70; Christianity's own real centralization is a later historical development past this game's AD 235 cutoff. What each faith has instead: Religion's own Priesthood office track for State-Sponsored faiths, and nothing more centralized than that for anyone else.

## 9. Distant & Emerging Faiths

### 9.1 Indian and Chinese Religious Tradition *(unchanged)*

Real, but appearing only as rare flavor alongside an already-rare Character origin — Ashoka's real historical Buddhist missions westward for India, the real Confucian/Daoist tradition for China, neither developed beyond flavor at this document's scale.

### 9.2 Manichaeism — Emerging at the Range's Own Tail End *(new)*

A real, direct structural parallel to Cultures of the Known World's own Aksumite entry (a Trade-Contact-Only culture only beginning to matter by the time this game's range ends): the historical Mani's own first teachings are traditionally dated to right around this game's own AD 235 cutoff, meaning Manichaeism is a faith that could, at the very outside edge of the latest possible playthrough, just barely begin to exist as a rumor rather than a real, adoptable option. Included here specifically to mark that honest edge rather than to offer it as real, playable content — the religious equivalent of Aksumite's own "only just starting to become worth knowing about."

## 14. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing carried forward unchanged.**
- **The Oracular tenet's exact "Consult the Oracle" action.** Still unresolved from the first pass, now with two named sites (Delphi, Dodona, Siwa) instead of one.
- **Initiatory tenet's exact cost/ritual shape**, including Serapis's own initiation specifically. Still unresolved.
- **Roster completeness, now genuinely comprehensive.** Every faith or faith-adjacent culture named across Religion, Cultures of the Known World, and this document's own two passes now has a real writeup — the honest remaining gap is limited to Cultures entries this document deliberately keeps generic (Bosporan and Cappadocian both received treatment this pass; Cilician religion remains folded into "heavily Hellenized, no distinct local tradition worth naming separately," a real, defensible historical judgment rather than an oversight).
- **Gnosticism's own internal variety.** §7.3 acknowledges real, diverse currents within early Christianity without naming or distinguishing individual movements — a deliberate scope limit, not a gap, since going further would require this document to take real positions on genuinely disputed and sensitive theological history.
- **Manichaeism's eventual full treatment.** §9.2 deliberately stops at "just barely emerging" — a natural candidate for real expansion if a future project decision extends the game's own historical range past AD 235.
