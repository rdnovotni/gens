# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Gallic Frontier
*Final polish and balance pass. Fills in the Starting Regions framework's Region Profile schema (§4) for a single, deliberately unified region rather than a split pair: per direction, Gaul stays one region so its identity as the frontier region stays undiluted, leaning toward the genuinely raw, Rhine-adjacent character of the Three Gauls rather than toward the already-settled south, consistent with this project's default Pax Romana start. This document is also the first to put the Reputation Duality mechanic (Politics & Patronage §2.1) through its full paces — this is the region that mechanic was written for. This pass corrects one cross-document inconsistency — an overlapping Templated Background claim on the "veteran given a land grant" archetype between this document and the Italian Heartland's own Campania half (§12) — and is intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Why This Region Stays Unified

Gaul has a real internal split almost as sharp as the one that justified dividing Latium from Campania: **Gallia Narbonensis**, the old southern province, was so thoroughly Romanized by this game's own era that Romans themselves called it simply *Provincia* — "the Province" — and its own city of Massilia already carries a real, cross-referenced identity in this project as "the Athens of Gaul" (Education & Culture §5.1). Set against that is the newer, rawer **Three Gauls** (Lugdunensis, Belgica, Aquitania) — genuinely more recently pacified, genuinely Rhine-adjacent, and genuinely the closer match to the core doc's own promise for this region: "cheap land, room for raw settlement expansion, higher military/security risk, lower starting prestige, more social mobility."

Per direction, this document keeps Gaul as **one selectable region** rather than splitting it the way Italy and Iberia/North Africa were split. Two real reasons support that call beyond direction alone: first, this project's default starting era is Pax Romana, at which point Narbonensis's own "frontier" character has genuinely faded — a split would produce one region (Narbonensis) that barely earns the name "frontier" at all, undercutting the very identity the core doc assigns this region. Second, "the Gallic Frontier" is explicitly named as *the* primary Reputation Duality setting — that mechanic needs one clear, unambiguous home rather than being awkwardly split across two documents. This document's own Gazetteer (§9) still gives Narbonensis's real cities a place in the wider picture — Massilia and Narbo Martius are real, reachable parts of this region's own geography — but the region's center of gravity, its Home Anchor (§11), and its mechanical identity all lean toward the genuine frontier north.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Genuinely the most terrain-diverse region on the launch roster, spanning both a Mediterranean coast (the Narbonensis south) and a real, distinct Atlantic coast (Aquitania) — two different maritime characters within one region, unlike anywhere else in the game. Dense Forest terrain, particularly toward the Rhine and the real Ardennes region, gives this region the strongest Timber identity in the game. Real hill country inland (the Massif Central) carries genuine mineral deposits — the actual historical basis for this region's own signature Tin good (§7). Multiple major real rivers — the Rhône, Garonne, Loire, and the Rhine itself along the region's own northeastern edge — give River-gated buildings (Clay Pits, water-powered Grain Mills, Glassworks) unusually broad availability here compared to a single-river region like Latium.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* The core doc's own promise holds directly: land here is genuinely cheap, and genuinely abundant, especially away from the handful of real urban centers named in the Gazetteer (§9) — the closest thing on the launch roster to Latium's own economic mirror image. Markets are real but thin outside Narbo Martius, Massilia, and Lugdunum; a household settling in the true frontier interior is trading market depth for land it could never afford in Italy. Security is a real, ongoing cost here in a way it simply isn't in either Italian region — Frontier Security Posture (Policies & Edicts §2.12) isn't background flavor in this region, it's a standing budget line every Gallic Frontier household has to actually set.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

The most genuinely mixed Legal Status population on the launch roster so far — a real, deliberate contrast with both Italian regions. Narbonensis's old cities carry long-settled Roman citizenship; the Three Gauls' own more recently pacified towns hold a real mix of citizen, Latin Rights, and Peregrine status; and the rural interior, away from any city at all, is realistically the most likely place on the entire roster to find native Gauls who haven't been meaningfully drawn into the Roman legal system at all. This is this region's own honest legal texture, not an oversight to smooth over.

Politics & Patronage's cursus honorum is real but genuinely harder to reach here than from either Italian region — the core doc's own "lower starting prestige" plays out exactly as a longer, less certain path to being noticed by Rome. That difficulty cuts the other way too, though: a magistracy actually won from this far out carries real weight precisely because it wasn't easy, and "more social mobility" is this region's own compensating promise — a *novus homo* story that would struggle against Latium's own entrenched competition (Politics & Patronage §6) has real room to breathe out here.

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — The Two Axes of the Frontier

This region runs on two genuinely distinct outward-facing relationships, and keeping them clearly separate is this document's single most important structural decision.

### 5.1 The External Frontier Neighbor — Germanic Tribes Beyond the Rhine

Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples needs a real neighbor to anchor to, and this document names **Germanic tribes across the Rhine** as that default — the single most historically resonant choice available, carrying the real weight of Teutoburg and a genuinely permanent frontier that never fully closed the way Iberia's or Gaul's own internal conquest eventually did. This is an *external* relationship: treaties, raiding, hostage-taking, and the full Diplomacy toolkit that document already built (§6–9 of that document) all play out against this neighbor specifically, anchored at Mogontiacum (§9).

### 5.2 Local Standing — The Region's Own Gallic Population

**Reputation Duality's** own local-standing axis (Politics & Patronage §2.1) tracks something different and internal: favor with this region's *own* Gallic population — conquered, administratively Provincial, but still culturally and often legally distinct from Rome, per §4's own texture. This is not the same relationship as §5.1's external Germanic neighbor, and this document deliberately keeps the two separate rather than collapsing them into one generic "frontier unrest" number: a household can hold excellent standing with Rome, poor standing with its own local Gallic population, and a real, live treaty with the Germanic tribes across the river, all three moving independently. §15 works through a concrete example of exactly this kind of divergence.

### 5.3 A Real Standing Military Presence — Unlike Either Italian Region

Worth stating directly as a contrast: the Italian Heartland document went out of its way to note that Pax Romana legions are frontier-stationed, not posted in Italy. Here, that's reversed — this region genuinely borders the actual military frontier, and Augusta Treverorum and Mogontiacum (§9) are real, historically attested logistics and garrison hubs supporting the Rhine legions. Military & Combat's recruitment pool here skews toward real auxiliary service — Gallic and neighboring Germanic peoples were, historically, prized and extensively used auxiliary troops — a genuine, distinct military-flavor identity from either Italian region's own patronage-based officer track or naval-service track.

## 6. Religious & Cultural Defaults — Suppression, Not Just Syncretism

Per Cultures of the Known World, Gallic culture carries a Druidic religious tradition — but this region's religious texture isn't simply "a foreign cult Rome tolerates and slowly syncretizes," the pattern Campania's own Temple of Isis illustrates. Rome's real historical relationship with Druidic practice specifically was suppression: early Imperial authorities genuinely moved against Druidic religious practice, a real, documented exception to Rome's usual live-and-let-live approach to local religion elsewhere in the empire. This gives Gallic Frontier a religious tension no other launch region carries: not absorption, but active pressure on a native tradition the local population hasn't abandoned.

This connects directly and concretely to §5.2's local-standing axis: a household's own Rites Budget (Policies & Edicts §2.3) and Provincial Administration Posture (§2.10 of that document) choices — tolerating quiet native religious practice versus actively enforcing suppression — should read as one of the single sharpest local-standing swings available in this region, sharper than an ordinary tax or labor policy choice would produce elsewhere. A household that looks the other way on Druidic practice gains real local trust at real risk with Rome; a household that enforces suppression faithfully gains standing with Rome at a real, felt local cost. This is Reputation Duality's own clearest, most concrete lever in the entire region.

## 7. Regional Goods & Trade

Tin (Massif Central), Furs/Pelts, Butter, and Beer are this region's established signature goods (Resources & Goods §5/§7) — Beer specifically as the real, deliberate cultural counterpart to Wine, giving this region its own distinct beverage identity rather than a lesser copy of an Italian vineyard. Per direction, this document adds a second, later-developing good rather than choosing between them: **Gallic wine**, genuinely real and genuinely significant — Narbonensis and Aquitania (Burdigala specifically) developed real, substantial viticulture that eventually rivaled Italian wine in the wider Roman market. This gives the region's own economic identity a real internal range, from Beer's frontier-and-interior identity to a genuinely competitive southern wine trade — and, per §14, a real historical flashpoint (Domitian's Vine Edict) where that Gallic wine success actually collided with Italian economic interests.

## 8. Population & Culture Distribution

| Culture | Presence |
|---|---|
| Gallic | Dominant |
| Roman/Latin (colonists, veterans, administrators, merchants) | Common — a real, growing, but still genuinely minority presence outside the named cities |
| Germanic (traders, hostages per Diplomacy §6, occasional settlers) | Rare, but a real, plausible presence given the Rhine's own permeability as a trade and contact zone, not just a hard military line |
| Any other culture (Mediterranean traders reaching Massilia and Narbo Martius specifically) | Rare, individual-level |

This is the most Gallic-majority population profile on the launch roster by a wide margin — a deliberate, honest contrast with both Italian regions' own Roman-majority tables, and the single clearest illustration in this project so far of what "recently conquered, still finding its own equilibrium" actually looks like at the population level.

## 10. Rival Seeding

Four houses, deliberately spanning the full range of §4's own Legal Status texture — from a family barely inside the Roman system at all to one already reaching for the Senate.

- **Gens Iulia Turona** *(seated at Lugdunum)* — a Romanized native Gallic aristocratic house: descended from old pre-conquest Gallic nobility, enfranchised within recent living memory and carrying an imperial-sponsor nomen (a real, well-documented pattern — newly citizened provincials often took the name of whichever emperor's reign granted their family citizenship) alongside a native-rooted cognomen. Straddles both of §5's axes directly: genuinely respected among the region's own Gallic population for its native lineage, and genuinely ambitious for further standing with Rome — a household that embodies Reputation Duality's own tension in a single rival rather than splitting it across two.
- **Gens Fulvia** *(seated at Augusta Treverorum)* — a military-colonist family descended from veteran land-grant settlers, with real, ongoing ties to the Rhine garrison towns. A natural Frontier Security Posture-aligned rival, and a plausible source of real competition for auxiliary-recruitment prestige per §5.3.
- **Gens Cassia** *(seated at Burdigala)* — a river-and-wine trade house built on Aquitania's own real viticulture success (§7), with real trade reach up the Garonne and out along the Atlantic coast; a natural commercial rival distinct from Gens Fulvia's military-flavored identity and Gens Iulia Turona's political one.
- **The household of Cintugnatus** *(seated in the rural interior, near the player's own Home Anchor)* — deliberately given a native Gallic name rather than a Roman one, reflecting a real, still-largely-Peregrine local family that has not meaningfully Romanized its own naming convention or civic identity. Holds real, quiet influence over the region's own surviving Druidic-adjacent religious practice (§6) and real weight with the local Gallic population specifically — the single most concrete rival-house illustration of §5.2's own local-standing axis, and a household whose favor is much easier to win through tolerance than through any amount of Roman-facing prestige.

---

## 11. Home Anchor

**In the countryside near Augusta Treverorum**, along the Moselle — close enough to a real, attested logistics and garrison hub to make §5.3's own standing military presence and §5.1's Germanic frontier genuinely felt from turn one, but not literally inside a legionary fortress. This is this region's own equivalent of Campania's Vesuvius-adjacent Home Anchor choice: leaning into the region's defining tension rather than settling somewhere safer and less characterful.

---

## 12. Templated Background Flavor

A Gallic Frontier start is the **primary** natural home for Templated Backgrounds' **"veteran given a land grant"** archetype (Core §5.2) — real, historically common legionary veteran colonization is the classic, textbook version of this archetype, and Gens Fulvia (§10) exists partly to populate that archetype's own social world at Augusta Treverorum, a real garrison-town setting that makes a veteran-background household's presence there feel earned. This is the archetype's stronger, more classic expression; Campania's own naval-veteran variant of the same archetype (Starting Regions: Italian Heartland §4.11) remains a real, distinct, secondary reading of it rather than a competing primary claim. The **"provincial notable"** archetype fits even more precisely here than anywhere else on the roster: Gens Iulia Turona's own straddling of native lineage and Roman ambition is close to that archetype's own definition in miniature, giving a future Start Modes pass a strong, concrete default to point to.

## 14. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 14.1 Vercingetorix's Revolt (52 BC, era-conditional)

A real, historically significant Gallic uprising against Roman conquest, predating this game's own default Pax Romana start and effectively closed history by that point — flagged here, consistent with the Italian Heartland document's own Social War treatment, as a genuine option for a playthrough whose start date sits earlier in the timeline, rather than a mechanic live in every playthrough.

### 14.2 The Batavian Revolt (AD 69–70)

Already named in Cultures of the Known World's own Batavian entry as a real, dateable episode erupting during the chaos of the Year of the Four Emperors — and, unlike Vercingetorix's revolt, genuinely live within this game's own default Pax Romana range. A particularly sharp illustration of §5.3's own real military-recruitment texture: the Batavians were simultaneously prized, trusted Roman auxiliaries and, at this real moment, a genuine revolt source — the same honest contradiction Cultures already flags, now given a concrete Events-system Divergence hook.

### 14.3 Domitian's Vine Edict (AD 92, era-conditional)

A real, historically attested imperial policy: a genuine attempt to restrict new provincial vineyard planting, driven by real Italian wine-growing interests threatened by Gallic wine's own growing success (§7). Distinct in character from the other two hooks — an economic and political threat rather than a war — and era-conditional in the other direction from §14.1: only live for a playthrough whose timeline actually reaches this specific date, rather than being closed history by default. A natural Economy & Finance and Trade Openness (Policies & Edicts §2.7) flashpoint for a Burdigala-adjacent, wine-invested household specifically.

## 15. Reputation Duality Applied — A Worked Example

A concrete illustration of §5.2's own divergence, since this is the mechanic's flagship region:

A household pursuing an **Exploitative** Provincial Administration Posture (Policies & Edicts §2.10) — maximizing Treasury and standing-with-Rome extraction — alongside an **Aggressive** Recruitment Intensity (§2.5 of that document) to fill out its own Estate Force will see real, steady gains in Dignitas-with-Rome and real, steady erosion of local standing, per §5.2. If that same household also enforces Druidic suppression faithfully (§6) rather than looking away, that local erosion accelerates sharply rather than gradually. The household is, at this point, a real, live illustration of the two axes pulling in opposite directions: welcomed at Rome, resented at home — a genuinely playable, historically honest position, not a temporary imbalance the system nudges back toward center.

The reverse is equally real: an **Assimilationist** Posture, a **Compassionate** Marital Diplomacy Posture (§2.11), and a quiet tolerance of local religious practice will build strong, durable local standing — including, plausibly, real goodwill from a rival like the household of Cintugnatus (§10) — while Rome itself reads that same household as unambitious, or worse, insufficiently invested in extracting the province's own value. Both paths are real, viable identities this region supports; neither is simply the "correct" way to play a Gallic Frontier household, consistent with Design Pillar #1.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** fulfills the Region Profile schema (§4) for the Gallic Frontier, and extends §6's own Distance Tier table with a further set of pairings (§13 above).
- **Politics & Patronage:** this document is Reputation Duality's (§2.1) own flagship implementation — §5.2 and §15 give that mechanic its first full region-level treatment.
- **Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples:** §5.1 anchors that document's entire treaty/raiding/hostage-taking toolkit to a named external neighbor (Germanic tribes) and a named Gazetteer location (Mogontiacum) for the first time.
- **Policies & Edicts:** Provincial Administration Posture (§2.10) and Frontier Security Posture (§2.12) both find their clearest region-level illustration here (§3, §6, §15); this document names Gallic Frontier as the natural home for the **Domus Provincialis** Household Doctrine referenced in that document's own Foederati Pact hook.
- **Military & Combat:** §5.3's auxiliary-recruitment identity, anchored at Augusta Treverorum and Mogontiacum, gives that system a genuine third recruitment flavor alongside the Italian regions' patronage-officer and naval tracks.
- **Religion:** §6's suppression-rather-than-syncretism framing gives that system a genuinely distinct texture from Campania's own tolerant foreign-cult model, and a direct, concrete Rites Budget/local-standing interaction.
- **Resources & Goods:** §7 specifies both halves of that document's existing Gallic tags (Tin, Furs/Pelts, Butter, Beer) and adds Gallic wine as a new, historically grounded regional good.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§10), deliberately spanning the region's own full Legal Status range from Peregrine to Senate-aspirant.
- **Events:** §14's three Timeline Hooks — one closed-history, one live-in-range, one forward-conditional — give that system a genuinely wider spread of era-conditionality than the Italian Heartland document's own two-hook pair offered.
- **Education & Culture:** Massilia's own already-established role (§5.1 of that document) is inherited directly rather than redefined.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — the Reputation Duality divergence rate in §15, Frontier Security Posture's own cost curve, and every Distance Tier in §13 are left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether an earlier-era Start Mode ever actually opens the Vercingetorix window.** §14.1 flags it the same way the Italian Heartland document flagged the Social War — a real, live option only if a future era-range decision makes it reachable.
- **The household of Cintugnatus's own long-term arc.** §10 leaves this house's own relationship to Roman legal status open — whether it should eventually face pressure (or opportunity) to formally enfranchise over the course of a long playthrough isn't resolved here, and reads as a natural Familia or Legal & Court question rather than this document's own to answer.
- **Domus Provincialis's own full mechanical shape.** §16 names Gallic Frontier as this Doctrine's natural home, but the Doctrine's own Affinity sources and capstone effect remain Policies & Edicts' own territory to finish defining.
- **Whether Narbonensis ever warrants its own future document regardless.** §1 makes the case for keeping Gaul unified now; a much later pass, once every launch region is finished, could still reasonably revisit whether Narbonensis's own calmer, Italy-adjacent identity deserves a lighter, optional second document — deliberately left open rather than foreclosed.
