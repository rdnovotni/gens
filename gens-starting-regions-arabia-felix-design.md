# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Arabia Felix
*The eleventh extensible-slate region, and the first to carry an explicit, clearly-labeled alternate-history layer rather than folding a stretch quietly into the main text. Real history offers less to build a "Roman settlement" premise on here than anywhere else on this roster: Rome never held any part of South Arabia, not even briefly the way Mesopotamia was briefly a real province. The one real Roman military venture here — Aelius Gallus's expedition (26–24 BC) — was a real, documented failure. This document builds its honest default around real, substantial trade contact instead (the same real merchant-and-native-household structure Nubia's own document already established), and then, per direction, adds a second, clearly separated layer: an explicit counterfactual for players who want an actual Roman colonial premise here, built on the one real historical point where it could plausibly have gone differently.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Real Trade, and One Clearly Labeled What-If

South Arabia — the real ancient kingdoms of Saba (Sheba), Himyar, Qataban, and Hadramawt, collectively the region Rome itself named *Arabia Felix*, "Fortunate Arabia," for its real, famous incense-trade wealth — was never Roman territory at any point across this game's own range. Unlike Nubia (Starting Regions: Nubia), which fought Rome once and settled into a real, mature peace, South Arabia's one real encounter with Rome was a one-sided military failure that never got as far as a negotiated peace at all: Aelius Gallus's real expedition (§15.1) never captured a single South Arabian city, and Rome never tried again in force.

This document handles that honestly in two clearly separated layers. §§2–15 build this region's real, defensible default exactly the way Nubia's own document did — genuine trade contact, a genuinely independent kingdom, no Roman administration ever. §16 then adds, explicitly and separately, the one thing direction asked for that the real history alone can't support: a clearly labeled counterfactual in which Aelius Gallus's real historical failure instead succeeds, opening an actual Roman colonial premise. Nothing in §§2–15 depends on §16, and nothing in §16 pretends to be anything other than what it is.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Real, genuinely mountainous terrain in the Yemeni highlands, catching real seasonal monsoon rainfall that made this region markedly more fertile than the surrounding Arabian desert — the actual historical basis for "Fortunate Arabia" as a real description rather than pure flattery. The real, genuinely remarkable Marib Dam, an ancient engineering achievement supporting substantial irrigated agriculture, anchors this region's own terrain-and-infrastructure identity (§10). Real coastal terrain along both the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden gave this region control of a real, narrow maritime chokepoint between the Mediterranean world and the Indian Ocean trade network.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* A real, genuinely wealthy trade economy, in the same register as Nubia's own — this region controlled the actual real chokepoint through which frankincense, myrrh, and Indian Ocean goods reached the Mediterranean world, and taxed that flow accordingly. This document treats that wealth as real and independent, the same honest framing Nubia's own document applies to Kushite prosperity — a household here isn't extracting value on Rome's behalf, it's trading with Rome as a genuine outside economic actor controlling something Rome itself couldn't produce domestically (§8).

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

Consistent with Nubia's own precedent (Starting Regions: Nubia §4), this document doesn't attempt to describe South Arabia's own governance in Roman Legal Status terms — there was no Roman administration here, at any point, for those categories to apply to. Several real, distinct kingdoms (Saba, Himyar, Qataban, Hadramawt) shared this region across the timeline, their own relative power shifting genuinely across the centuries this game's range covers — a real, internally complex political landscape entirely independent of Rome, closer in spirit to the real diversity Syria's own document already modeled (Starting Regions: Syria / The Levant §1) than to a single unified kingdom.

---

## 5. The Household's Own Three Real Options

This document offers a genuine third option beyond the two-way choice Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Nubia each established, precisely because §16's own alternate-history layer opens a door those documents didn't need:

- **A resident Roman or Greco-Egyptian merchant household** — this document's own recommended default, real-historically grounded in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* (already established via Cultures of the Known World §10.1's own Indian trade-contact content), a genuine first-century Greek merchant's guide to exactly this trade network. A household here manages real trade interests as a resident foreigner, the same structural premise Nubia's own Gens Junia illustrates.
- **A native South Arabian noble household** — Sabaean, Himyarite, Qatabanian, or Hadrami — genuinely embedded in one of the real kingdoms sharing this region, with its own ambitions and rivalries entirely independent of Rome, the same default-option logic Nubia's own document applies to its own Kushite noble house.
- **A Roman colonial household** — available only under §16's own explicit alternate-history layer, and clearly marked as such rather than presented alongside the first two options as if equally grounded in the real historical record.

---

## 6. Reputation Duality — Not Applicable, the Same Way Nubia Isn't

For exactly the same reason Nubia's own document already established (Starting Regions: Nubia §6): there is no Roman administrative authority here, across §§2–15's own real historical default, for a household's standing to be dual with. This document doesn't repeat that reasoning at length — it simply confirms the same honest finding applies here for the same underlying reason. §16's own alternate-history layer, if a player opts into it, would need its own Reputation Duality treatment from scratch, most plausibly Full, the same way Mesopotamia's own Sustained Occupation outcome would generate fresh tension rather than inheriting an existing pattern.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

Real South Arabian religious tradition centered on a distinct pantheon, including the real attested moon-god Almaqah, particularly significant to the Sabaean kingdom specifically — genuinely separate from Egyptian, Arabian-Nabataean (Dushara, per Starting Regions: Syria / The Levant §7), or any other tradition already tracked on this roster. This document names this real, distinct religious identity honestly while flagging (§18, alongside §9) that it isn't yet a formally tracked entry in Religions of the Known World.

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade — Where Frankincense and Myrrh Actually Come From

**This document resolves a real, long-standing gap the same way this project has already resolved Ephesus, Cyrene, Cornish Tin, and Tyrian Purple's own points of origin.** Resources & Goods has tracked both Frankincense and Myrrh as Imported Goods since early in this project — genuinely never locally producible via any domestic Roman production chain, per that document's own §6 — without any region ever claiming to actually be where they come from. This document claims that directly: South Arabia (alongside the real Horn of Africa, per Aksum's own trade-contact culture) is the real historical point of origin for both goods, the actual reason this entire region earned its own Roman name. A household here doesn't produce Frankincense or Myrrh through any Buildings-doc production chain — it controls and taxes their real point of origin, a genuinely distinct economic role from every "goods-producing" region on this roster.

## 9. Population & Culture Distribution — A Third Gap This Roster Surfaces

Consistent with the honest precedent Sicily and the Alpine Provinces both already set, this document surfaces rather than papers over a real gap: no Sabaean, Himyarite, Qatabanian, or Hadrami culture value exists yet in Cultures of the Known World. This document names the real population honestly and flags the gap for that document's own future consideration (§18) rather than inventing a tracked value unilaterally.

| Population | Presence |
|---|---|
| South Arabian (Sabaean, Himyarite, Qatabanian, or Hadrami, depending on era and specific location) | Dominant |
| Roman/Greco-Egyptian (a small, real resident merchant community, per §5) | Rare |
| Nabataean and other Arabian trade contacts | Rare |
| Indian and East African trade contacts (per this region's own real Indian Ocean gateway role) | Rare, individual-level outliers |

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, spanning §5's own full range of options, including this document's own alternate-history-only entry.

- **The household of Karib'il** *(seated at Marib)* — a Sabaean noble house carrying a real, plausible South Arabian royal-pattern name (echoing real attested names like Karib'il Watar without claiming identity with any specific historical ruler), embedded in Saba's own real political life independent of Rome.
- **The household of Yada'ab** *(seated near Cana)* — a Hadrami trade family built on real frankincense-export wealth, distinct in both culture and economic base from Karib'il's own Sabaean royal orientation.
- **Gens Antonia** *(seated at Muza)* — a resident Roman/Greco-Egyptian merchant family, this document's own concrete illustration of §5's recommended default household.
- **Gens Aelia** *(seated near Marib, available only under §16's own alternate-history layer)* — a colonial family whose own name deliberately echoes Aelius Gallus without claiming descent from him specifically, in the same naming spirit as this project's earlier Balbina and Agricolana households, present only in a playthrough that has opted into §16.

**Near Muza**, under this document's own recommended default — the real, concrete trade-hub setting §5's own merchant household option is built around. A player choosing the native noble household option would naturally anchor near Marib instead; this document doesn't force a single anchor across all three of §5's options the way most regions on this roster do, consistent with how genuinely different those three premises actually are.

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Consistent with the precedent Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Nubia all set, this document doesn't force any of Core §5.2's four archetypes onto its own default, genuinely independent-kingdom premise — every rival house under §§2–15 is left unmapped. Gens Aelia, available only under §16, is the one exception: it gives **"veteran given a land grant"** a further flavor specifically scoped to this document's own alternate-history layer, explicitly not claimed as a normal, always-available reading the way every other region's own use of that archetype is.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 Aelius Gallus's Expedition (26–24 BC, closed history by default)

A real, documented Roman military expedition into South Arabia under Augustus, aiming to secure the incense trade — and a real, thorough failure, undone by disease, difficult desert terrain, and (per the real historian Strabo) a guide who may have deliberately misled the Roman force. This document names it factually as the real historical baseline §16 explicitly departs from.

### 15.2 The Slow Redirection of the Indian Ocean Trade (a gradual trend across the range, not a single date)

A second real, gradual-trend Timeline Hook, in the same register as Nubia's own Slow Rise of Aksum (Starting Regions: Nubia §15.2): the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* itself documents a real, ongoing shift as Roman and Greek sailors increasingly learned to use monsoon winds to sail directly between Egypt and India, gradually bypassing South Arabian ports as necessary middlemen. A household starting early in this game's range experiences this region near the height of its real chokepoint leverage; a household starting later experiences a genuinely fading version of that same advantage — the same "which decade you arrive in changes the whole picture" texture Nubia's own document already established, here driven by a real, different underlying cause.

## 16. The Alternate History Layer — Aelius Gallus Succeeds

**This section is explicitly, deliberately counterfactual, and this document names it as such rather than blending it into the historically grounded content above.** Per direction, this document opens a clearly labeled alternate-history option: a version of events in which Aelius Gallus's real historical expedition (§15.1) succeeds instead of failing — Marib falls, or a client relationship is imposed, and Augustus gains the genuine Roman foothold in South Arabia the real expedition never achieved.

This is a different kind of departure from Mesopotamia's own Sustained Occupation Divergence (Starting Regions: Mesopotamia §16). Mesopotamia's exception extended something that really happened for two real years; there is no equivalent real starting point here to extend from — the entire premise of a Roman colonial presence in South Arabia has to be invented from the point of divergence forward. This document handles that honestly by treating §16 as an explicit toggle a player must consciously select, closer in spirit to choosing a Scenario Start (Core §5.4) than to an outcome a normal playthrough of this region could organically reach through play alone.

Under this toggle: Gens Aelia (§11) becomes available as a colonial household, Marib's own Gazetteer entry gains a genuine Roman administrative layer alongside its real Sabaean one, and this region's own Reputation Duality would begin fresh, most plausibly Full, from the point of divergence forward (§6). Everything else in this document — the real trade economy, the real religious tradition, the real Gazetteer, the real Timeline Hooks — stays exactly as written; §16 only ever adds a layer on top, never replaces the honest default.

## 17. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** the sixth region built entirely outside the framework's original candidate list, following Sicily, the Alpine Provinces, Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Nubia — and the first to carry an explicit, separately-labeled alternate-history layer rather than a divergence extending real events.
- **Nubia:** this document's own closest structural sibling — shares that document's Royal Seat Gazetteer Role reuse, its independent-kingdom Reputation Duality reasoning (§6), and its basic two-household-option structure before §16 adds a third.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 finally resolves Frankincense's and Myrrh's own real point of origin, a gap this project's own goods registry has carried since early in its development.
- **Cultures of the Known World:** §9 surfaces this roster's third real population gap (after Sicily and the Alpine Provinces) — no South Arabian culture value exists yet.
- **Events:** §15.2 is this project's second gradual-trend Timeline Hook, after Nubia's own Slow Rise of Aksum, confirming that hook type as a genuinely reusable pattern rather than a one-off.
- **Mesopotamia:** §16 is this document's own point of comparison and contrast with that document's own Historical Divergence mechanic — extending a real two-year window versus inventing an entire counterfactual premise from a single failed expedition.

## 19. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and this region's own frankincense/myrrh output are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether South Arabian culture(s) warrant tracked values in Cultures of the Known World**, mirroring the same open question Sicily and the Alpine Provinces both raised for their own populations — a genuine, now three-region case for that document's own next pass to address.
- **Whether South Arabian religious tradition (§7) should be formally added to Religions of the Known World**, the same open question Sicily's own document raised for the Demeter/Persephone connection there.
- **Whether the Alternate History Layer (§16) needs a clearer, more prominent player-facing framing** than this document alone can specify — this is this project's most explicit departure from real history yet, more so than Mesopotamia's own Sustained Occupation outcome, and deserves at least as much care in how it's actually surfaced to a player.
- **Whether other regions with a single, real, failed Roman military venture** (rather than Mesopotamia's brief real success or Nubia's negotiated draw) might justify similar Alternate History Layer treatment in the future — this document treats Arabia Felix as the category's first, and, for now, only member.
- **Home Anchor's own split default (§12).** This document doesn't force one anchor across all three of §5's household options, unlike most regions on this roster — worth a future pass's judgment on whether that flexibility should extend elsewhere too, or whether Arabia Felix's own three-way option split is unusual enough to warrant it uniquely.
