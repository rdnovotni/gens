# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — The Balkans
*The fifth and final extensible-slate region to receive its own full document, closing out every candidate the framework itself ever named (Starting Regions §5.2). Fills in the Region Profile schema (§4) for a region carrying a genuine structural first: this is the only region on the entire roster where part of its own selectable territory didn't belong to Rome yet for a large stretch of this game's own range. Dacia's real conquest doesn't close until AD 106 — later than every other region's own closing date on this roster, Nabataea's AD 106 annexation (Starting Regions: Syria / The Levant §4) alone excepted — meaning a household starting early enough simply cannot settle there at all, because it wasn't Roman territory to settle.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — A Region Whose Own Map Grows Mid-Game

This region spans real, distinct ground: **Illyria/Dalmatia** along the Adriatic coast, thoroughly pacified before this game's own range even opens; **Pannonia**, a real frontier that closed comparatively early, following the real Bato's Revolt (§15.1); **Moesia**, the administrative and military core along the southern Danube; **Thrace**, culturally and religiously distinct, already carrying a real tie to Games & Spectacle via Spartacus's own attested origin (Cultures of the Known World §5); and **Dacia**, north of the Danube, the single latest major territory this entire launch-and-extensible roster ever adds to the map — not conquered until Trajan's real Dacian Wars concluded in AD 106.

That last fact is this document's own defining structural feature, and it's worth stating as plainly as possible: **for any playthrough starting before AD 106, Dacia is not part of this region at all.** It's a real, independent, formidable neighboring kingdom under its own real king, Decebalus — a genuine external Frontier relationship, not a selectable sub-area, until the conquest actually happens. A household choosing this region early in the timeline settles in Illyria, Pannonia, Moesia, or Thrace, and watches Dacia as a real, live neighbor rather than a home; a household starting after AD 106 gets Dacia as a genuine, if very new, fifth option.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Real, extensive river terrain along the Danube itself — the longest and most consistently significant river frontier on this entire roster — alongside genuine Adriatic coastal terrain in Illyria/Dalmatia, real fertile plains in Pannonia and Moesia, and genuine mountainous, mineral-rich terrain in both Thrace and, once conquered, Dacia specifically. Dacia's own real gold deposits, particularly around the historically attested mining region of Alburnus Maior (§10), are the actual historical basis for Cultures of the Known World's own "real gold-mining wealth" note on that culture's entry — a genuine, concrete resource this region alone can offer once (and only once) it becomes available.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Illyria, Pannonia, and Moesia support a real, moderate agricultural and river-trade economy, comparable in general character to Gallic Frontier's own settled interior rather than to any of this roster's wealthiest regions. Thrace runs similarly. Dacia, once available, is genuinely the newest and rawest land on the entire roster to actually settle — cheaper than even Gallic Frontier's own land at that region's own founding, precisely because it's had no time at all to develop before a Dacia-based household's own arrival, offset by real, substantial gold wealth for a household willing to take on genuinely new frontier risk this late in the timeline.

---

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

Illyria and Moesia's own Legal Status mix runs close to Gallic Frontier's own settled-interior model — real, majority citizen and Latin-rights populations in the established cities, with real remaining Peregrine status further from them. Pannonia carries a rawer mix, closer to Gallic Frontier's own earlier timeline, reflecting its own comparatively recent pacification. Dacia, once available, carries this roster's own newest and most thoroughly Peregrine population outside of a genuinely fresh conquest zone — there simply hasn't been time within this game's own range for Dacia's own population to move meaningfully up the Legal Status ladder the way a household starting in AD 107 would find already underway in, say, Gallic Frontier by that same year.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — A Neighbor That Changes Identity Over Time

This region's own external relationship is genuinely unique on the roster for changing *who* the neighbor actually is partway through the game's own range, rather than simply changing in intensity the way Iberian or North African Colony's own tapering relationships do:

- **Before AD 106:** the external Frontier neighbor is **Dacia itself** — a real, formidable, independent kingdom under Decebalus, the direct real historical threat behind the Dacian Wars (§15.2). This reads exactly like Gallic Frontier's own Germanic relationship in shape: live, adversarial, genuinely dangerous.
- **After AD 106:** Dacia becomes internal territory (§1), and the region's own new external neighbor becomes the **Sarmatian/Scythian** peoples of the Pontic steppe beyond it — already established as a Frontier culture in Cultures of the Known World §8, now given its own first concrete regional anchor.

This region also carries a real, substantial standing legionary garrison along the Danube for most of its own range — a frontier commitment genuinely comparable in scale to Gallic Frontier's own Rhine garrison, and, after AD 106, extended further still to cover Dacia's own new and genuinely exposed salient north of the river.

---

## 6. Reputation Duality — Localized Again, With a Genuine Twist

This document reuses Syria/The Levant's own **Localized** shape (Starting Regions: Syria / The Levant §6) rather than inventing a sixth mode — a deliberate choice demonstrating that Localized was always meant as a genuinely reusable pattern for any region with sufficiently varied internal sub-areas, not a one-off Syria-specific mechanic.

- **Illyria, Moesia, Thrace:** effectively **None** — thoroughly settled, no live local-standing tension worth tracking.
- **Pannonia:** **Tapering**, hinged on the real resolution of Bato's Revolt (§15.1) — the same three-position shape Iberian and North African Colony already use.
- **Dacia:** **Full**, and, per §1's own structural twist, only ever available in its own "early conquest" phase within this game's remaining timeline — Dacia is annexed in AD 106 and this game's own range ends in AD 235, meaning a Dacia-based household never actually gets the extended span of settled peace a Gallic Frontier or Iberian Colony household eventually enjoys. Dacia's own local standing is genuinely live for essentially the entire span any Dacia-based playthrough could ever cover.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

Thracian religious identity centers on the real, attested "Thracian Horseman" motif (Religions of the Known World §5) — a genuine, distinct visual and votive tradition well-documented in surviving reliefs, and this document's own concrete grounding for the household of Cotys's own equestrian flavor (§11). Illyrian/Pannonian religious tradition is a real, distinct native Balkan practice, honestly thin in individually-attested specific deity names, the same honest gap that document already notes for Numidian and Sarmatian tradition alike. Dacian religious tradition, once relevant, centers on the real, specifically attested figure of **Zalmoxis**, whom Herodotus and later sources describe as teaching a genuine doctrine of the soul's immortality — a real, philosophically substantial tradition rather than a generic "native paganism" placeholder.

---

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

Dacian gold (§2, §10) is this region's own single most valuable and most exclusive good, available only once the region's own map actually grows to include it. Illyria and Dalmatia offer real fine building stone from the Adriatic coast; Pannonia and Moesia support ordinary river-valley agriculture; Thrace's own real reputation for fine horses gives this region a distinct livestock-and-cavalry-flavor identity, directly tied to §7's own Thracian Horseman religious motif.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, with the Dacian household explicitly gated the same way the location it's seated in is.

- **Gens Cornificia** *(seated at Salona)* — a thoroughly Roman-integrated Dalmatian coastal trade family, commercially ambitious in the ordinary register this region's own calm Illyrian core supports.
- **The household of Verzobrus** *(seated near Sirmium)* — a Pannonian family carrying real, genuine memory of Bato's Revolt (§15.1) within recent household history, deliberately given a native name rather than a Roman one, echoing every other native-named household on this roster, and, per §13, this region's own contribution to the "clawing back status" archetype rather than a simple economic descriptor.
- **The household of Vezina** *(seated at Sarmizegetusa, available only after AD 106)* — a native Dacian aristocratic family surviving the real conquest's own genuine aftermath, presented with the same restraint and respect this project has extended to every other household whose own culture experienced real historical conquest or loss — Decebalus's own real, somber end is acknowledged as backdrop rather than dramatized, consistent with Events' own established treatment of every named historical figure on its roster.
- **The household of Cotys** *(seated in Thrace)* — an equestrian-and-horse-breeding family tied directly to §7's own Thracian Horseman motif and this region's own real cavalry reputation, carrying a real, recurring Thracian dynastic name used by several actual historical rulers rather than uniquely identifying any single one of them.

## 12. Home Anchor

**Along the Danube near Sirmium, in Pannonia.** This anchors the household in this region's own always-available core regardless of start year — unlike Dacia, Pannonia exists as selectable territory for the entire game's range — while keeping the region's own real river-frontier character close and felt, the same "lean into the defining feature" logic Gallic Frontier's own Rhine-adjacent anchor uses.

---

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

The household of Verzobrus gives **"impoverished patrician clawing back status"** a fourth flavor, alongside Latium's own primary claim and Greek East's and Syria's own prior secondary readings — grounded here in Bato's Revolt's own real, genuine hardship rather than a financial reversal, the same non-reductive treatment Syria's own household of Eleazar received. The household of Vezina's own conquest-survival story is deliberately left unmapped to any of Core §5.2's four archetypes, consistent with the precedent Anatolia's own household of Straton set — not every rival house needs to illustrate a starting template, particularly one whose own real historical context (a kingdom conquered within living memory of this game's own latest possible start dates) doesn't map cleanly onto any of them.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 Bato's Revolt (AD 6–9, closed history by default, era-conditional for very early starts)

A real, major Illyrian/Pannonian uprising, already named in the Events timeline as "among the toughest wars Rome fought in this whole era" — closed history for any ordinary default-era playthrough given how early it falls, but the direct real hinge behind §6's own Pannonia Tapering designation and the household of Verzobrus's own backstory.

### 15.2 The Dacian Wars (AD 101–106, the latest-closing conquest arc on the entire roster)

The defining event of this entire document, and this project's single latest real conquest-completion date anywhere on the launch-and-extensible roster, Nabataea's own identical AD 106 alone excepted. Trajan's real campaigns against Decebalus's Dacia, commemorated in the real, still-standing Trajan's Column in Rome itself, are the direct historical hinge behind §1's own entire structural premise — this document's own single most consequential Timeline Hook, since its resolution literally determines whether Dacia exists as selectable territory at all for a given playthrough.

### 15.3 The Marcomannic Wars, From the Danube's Own Side (AD 178–180, era-conditional)

The Events timeline already names this conflict under Germanic culture, tagged to "real, sustained Germanic frontier warfare under Marcus Aurelius." This document names the same real war from its own home ground's perspective, the same complementary-not-duplicating treatment Anatolia's own document already applied to the Mithridatic Wars (Starting Regions: Anatolia / Asia Minor §15.2) — Pannonia specifically was real, contested battleground for substantial stretches of this conflict, not merely an adjacent territory watching it happen elsewhere.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** promotes the Balkans from the extensible slate (§5.2) to a fully realized region — the last of the framework's own originally-named candidates — and reuses the Localized Reputation Duality shape (Starting Regions: Syria / The Levant §6) as a genuinely second application, confirming it as a reusable pattern rather than a one-off.
- **Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples:** §5's own time-shifting external relationship (Dacia, then Sarmatian/Scythian) is a genuine structural first for that system — no other region's own external neighbor actually changes identity partway through the range.
- **Cultures of the Known World:** inherits Illyrian/Pannonian, Thracian, Dacian, and Sarmatian/Scythian entries directly; §2 gives that document's own "real gold-mining wealth" note for Dacian culture its first concrete regional anchor.
- **Religions of the Known World:** §7 inherits the Thracian Horseman motif and the Zalmoxis tradition directly.
- **Games & Spectacle:** Thracian culture's existing tie to Spartacus (Cultures of the Known World §5) is inherited rather than redefined; the household of Cotys's own equestrian flavor gives that system a further concrete regional hook.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 gives Dacian gold its first named regional home, and Alburnus Maior (§10) anchors it to a real, specific, well-attested site.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), one of them — Vezina's — explicitly gated by the same AD 106 availability condition as its own seat.
- **Events:** §15.2 is this project's own most structurally consequential Timeline Hook yet, since its resolution determines a selectable sub-area's very existence rather than merely coloring an already-available one.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and this region's own agricultural/mining output balance are left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether a playthrough starting before AD 106 should be able to "grow into" Dacia later**, the same way a household can acquire a Distant Holding in another region entirely (Starting Regions §7) — this document assumes yes, consistent with that mechanic's own existing shape, but doesn't specify whether annexing into a newly-conquered sub-area of one's own starting region works identically to acquiring a holding in a different region altogether.
- **The household of Vezina's own long-term arc**, mirroring the same open question left for every other native-named household on this roster — deliberately left to a future Familia or Legal & Court pass, with the added structural note that this household's own availability is itself conditional in a way none of the others are.
- **Whether the Dacia-then-Sarmatian external relationship shift (§5) needs a defined transition mechanic**, or whether Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples can simply treat AD 106 as a hard cutover — this document doesn't specify whether any residual Dacian-culture population or relationship carries forward into the post-106 Sarmatian-facing frontier.
- **Byzantium's own Gazetteer treatment.** §10 deliberately undersells this city's own well-known later historical trajectory to stay honest about its Principate-era standing specifically — a judgment call worth flagging in case a future pass feels this undersells its real, if more modest, contemporary significance.
