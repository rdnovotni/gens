# GENS — Authored Content & Reconciliation: The Late Antique Timeline (AD 235 – AD 565)

*This document exists because four other documents disagreed with each other. Events' own Game Calendar (§6.2) states the supported range is "now fixed" at 133 BC – AD 235. The Historical Timeline content document is titled and scoped to that identical shorter range. Religions of the Known World frames its own incompleteness — "No Head of Faith," Manichaeism "just barely emerging" — as accurate only "past this game's AD 235 cutoff." But Technology & Discoveries' own Era table already runs six eras deep through "Justinian's Renewal, c. AD 500s–600s," and this project's own confirmed decision is that the game's real range is 133 BC to AD 565. One document was already built for the real range; three others were never updated to match it. This document resolves that: it corrects the calendar, authors the roughly 330 years of real history the shorter range left blank, and makes the handful of genuine design decisions (a Sassanid succession, Christianity's real centralization, what happens to a region Rome no longer rules) that extending the range actually requires. Per this project's own standing convention, this is a dense representative pass, not a claim of exhaustiveness — the same honest scope the original Historical Timeline document already set for itself.*

## 1. Scope & Role

This document does three distinct jobs at once, and doesn't pretend they're one job: it **corrects** a stale calendar definition (§2), it **authors** real historical content for the range that definition was wrongly excluding (§5–7, §9, §11), and it **resolves** three genuine design questions the range extension actually forces (§8's Sassanid succession, §7's Head of Faith exception, §10's successor-kingdom question) rather than leaving them as open questions a second time. Nothing here contradicts the earlier range's own content — the Late Republic through Severan material in the original Historical Timeline document stays exactly as authored. This is a continuation, not a revision, of everything before AD 235.

## 2. The Corrected Game Calendar — 133 BC to AD 565

Events §6.2 is corrected as follows: the supported range is **133 BC – AD 565**, not AD 235. The opening year and its own reasoning are unchanged — Tiberius Gracchus's tribunate, the real event behind Policies & Edicts' own Land Redistribution Edict. The closing year is now Justinian's own death in AD 565, chosen for the identical reason the original AD 235 boundary was chosen: it's a real, coherent, dateable stopping point — the end of Justinian's own reconquest and legal codification, not an arbitrary round number, and not an attempt to model the considerably messier and less-documented centuries that follow it in the actual historical record.

## 3. The Optimal Start — Trajan's Accession, AD 98

Per direction, the game's full range remains fully playable end to end, but a **recommended default start** is worth naming for a player who doesn't want to hand-pick a year: **AD 98, Trajan's accession**. This date is already a named entry in the original Historical Timeline ("AD 98 | Nerva dies; Trajan becomes Emperor") and sits exactly at Events' own existing High Principate era boundary (AD 96–192), which that document's own Era table already describes as "the Empire at its most stable and its largest territorial extent." AD 98 specifically, rather than AD 96, is the recommended point because the succession itself was genuinely stable (Trajan was already Nerva's adopted heir, not a contested seizure), and because it opens onto the empire's real territorial high-water mark — the Dacian Wars, the brief Parthian War creating short-lived provinces in Mesopotamia and Assyria — while sitting comfortably before the Antonine Plague (AD 165) or the Marcomannic Wars (AD 178). A Randomized start still draws uniformly across the full 133 BC – AD 565 range unless a chosen region's own flavor narrows it, exactly as Events § 6.2 already specifies; AD 98 is a *suggestion* surfaced to a player who wants one, never a restriction on the range itself.

### 7.1 A Head of Faith, At Last — Resolving Religion's Own Departure

Religion §8 and Religions of the Known World §8 both correctly describe "No Head of Faith" as the honest reality for nearly this entire project's range — but AD 380 is the real, dateable exception those documents already anticipated without being able to build, since it fell past their own then-current AD 235 ceiling. From this date forward, **Christianity alone** among every faith this project tracks gains a real, centralizing external hierarchy: the Bishop of Rome in the West, and an increasingly distinct set of Patriarchs in the East (Constantinople, Antioch, Alexandria, Jerusalem — the real historical Pentarchy). A household's own Priesthood-track appointment (Religion §6) tied to Christianity after this date answers to that real external hierarchy rather than functioning as a free-floating household or state cult office the way every other faith's Priesthood still does — a genuine, isolated structural exception, not a retroactive change to how every other faith's own Priesthood already works.

The mirror side of the same reversal is just as real and just as worth playing straight: from AD 391 onward, **traditional Roman, Hellenic, and Egyptian polytheism inherit the Persecution-Prone tenet** that previously belonged only to Christianity and Judaism. A household that keeps its own household Lares and Penates, or maintains a Patron Deity temple after this date, is now the one practicing something the state actively suppresses — a real, historically ironic, and genuinely poignant reversal this document names directly rather than glossing over, consistent with the same restrained, described-not-depicted frankness this project already applies to slavery and punishment.

## 8. The Sassanid Question, Resolved

Historical Timeline's own Mechanical Notes already named the real AD 224–226 Sassanid overthrow of Parthia as "this pass's own single biggest structural addition," and flagged its actual mechanical resolution as a genuine open question for Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples' "own future revisit." This is that revisit.

**The mechanism:** at the historical date, Parthia's own `LivingWorldActor` record (Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples §2, reusing Rival Houses' shared actor framework) is directly succeeded by a new Sassanid Persia actor, rather than either simply renamed in place or reset from zero. Any Standing the household holds with Parthia at the moment of succession carries over to Sassanid Persia at a **real, partial reduction** — institutional momentum inherited without having personally earned it, the identical shape Policies & Edicts' own Doctrine Affinity already uses across a household's own succession, applied here to a foreign power's succession instead. An active Peace Treaty or Trade Agreement doesn't survive automatically; it lapses and must be renegotiated, reflecting that this is a genuinely new regime, not new management of the old one.

**What actually changes beyond the name:** Sassanid Persia is written as a real, historically distinct upgrade in threat, not a reskin. It's more centralized than Parthia's own looser feudal confederacy ever was, and its Zoroastrianism carries real state backing in a way Parthia's own more syncretic religious practice never fully did — Religions of the Known World's existing Fire-Venerating tenet applies with sharper, more official force. Mechanically, this reads as a real, standing increase to Great Power Diplomacy's own baseline difficulty (§8 of that document) from the historical succession date forward — the Legate's job gets harder, not merely renamed. Valerian's own real 260 AD capture (§5) is this document's own concrete, dateable proof the change is real rather than cosmetic.

## 10. When Rome Leaves — A Household's Region Under New Rule

The real, honest design question §9's own content forces directly: what happens to a household playing in Gallic Frontier, Britannia, Iberian Colony, or North African Colony once Rome's own actual authority there genuinely ends within this game's own now-real range? This document resolves the principle without attempting the full region-by-region rewrite that principle implies — that remains real, appropriately-scoped future work for each affected Starting Region document's own eventual revision pass, named honestly here rather than silently assumed.

**The principle:** a region passing from direct Roman rule to a Successor Kingdom (Visigothic Iberia, Vandal North Africa, increasingly Frankish Gaul, Anglo-Saxon-encroached Britannia) is never a forced game-ending event for a household already established there. Client Kingdoms & Vassal Rulers' own existing vassal-relationship framework is the direct mechanical answer, simply read in the opposite direction: a Roman household that once held land under Roman provincial authority can continue holding it under a new, non-Roman ruler's own authority instead, on that same document's existing terms — tribute, service, and standing, just owed to a different court. A household's own Reputation Duality axis (Politics & Patronage §2.1) becomes, from that point forward, a household's own standing with the new ruling power specifically rather than with distant Rome — the same mechanic, a different audience. Emigration back toward the still-standing Eastern Empire, per §11, remains a real, always-available alternative to staying.

## 15. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — nothing in this document introduces a number.
- **Region-by-region successor-kingdom dating.** §10 names the real principle; the actual dated transition for each of Gallic Frontier, Britannia, Iberian Colony, and North African Colony specifically is left to each document's own future revision pass.
- **Whether the Eastern territories (Greek East, Anatolia, Syria/Levant, Egypt) need their own explicit "still fully Roman throughout" confirmation**, given how much of this document's own drama is a Western story — a reasonable, low-priority follow-up rather than a real gap, since nothing in those regions' own existing documents is contradicted by anything here.
- **Whether a household can meaningfully relocate from a Successor Kingdom region to the still-standing Eastern Empire mid-playthrough**, and what that migration costs — §10 names it as "a real, always-available alternative" without specifying the actual mechanism.
- **The exact severity of Great Power Diplomacy's own difficulty increase (§8)** once Sassanid Persia replaces Parthia — named as real and directional, not sized.
- **Whether the Gothic War's own real devastation (§11) should read as a standing regional economic penalty for Italian Heartland specifically** for the remainder of the game's own range, or remain flavor-only — left to a future Italian Heartland region-document revision.
