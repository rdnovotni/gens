# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — The Alpine Provinces (Raetia & Noricum)
*The seventh extensible-slate region, and the second built entirely outside the framework's own originally-named candidates (following Sicily). Fills in the Region Profile schema (§4) for a genuinely calm, genuinely small pair of Alpine territories whose real distinguishing feature isn't risk, wealth, or cultural friction — it's that they're the only region on this entire roster reachable from Italy, Gaul, and the Balkans alike without ever crossing open water at all.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Two Provinces, Two Different Ways of Joining Rome

This region pairs two real, adjacent Alpine territories that became Roman within a year of each other by two genuinely different real paths. **Raetia** was conquered outright in 15 BC, in a real campaign led by Tiberius and Drusus — Augustus's own stepsons, one of whom would later become Emperor himself. **Noricum**, real-historically a client kingdom (the Regnum Noricum) allied with Rome for generations beforehand, was absorbed peacefully around 16 BC, without the kind of war this project's other regions' own founding stories almost all involve. This is a genuine first for this roster: not a conquest-versus-calm contrast the way Gallic Frontier or Iberian Colony present it, but a contrast in *how the same outcome was actually reached* — one real war, one real handshake, arriving at the same destination within a year of each other.

Neither province stays remotely tense afterward. This document treats that shared, thorough calm as the honest finding it is, consistent with the precedent Anatolia and Sicily both already set (Starting Regions: Anatolia / Asia Minor §6, Starting Regions: Sicily §6) — a region doesn't need manufactured tension to be worth building.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile — The Most Vertical Region on the Roster

Genuinely the most mountainous terrain of any region on this roster — real high Alpine passes, real steep valleys, and real, comparatively limited flat agricultural land by comparison with almost anywhere else this project has built. What flat and fertile land exists concentrates in a handful of real valley floors and the northern approach toward the Danube. This is real, difficult terrain to farm at scale, and this document doesn't pretend otherwise — Raetia and Noricum's own real wealth, per §8, was never going to run through grain the way Sicily's or Egypt's does.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Land here is genuinely cheap by Mediterranean standards, simply because so much of it is mountain rather than farmland — closer to Gallic Frontier's own land-cost character than to any of this roster's wealthier regions. What this region trades that cheap, difficult land for is real, valuable, and distinctly *industrial* wealth (§8) rather than the agricultural or trade-hub wealth almost every other region on this roster is built around, and a genuine, structural economic role as the connective corridor between Italy and the Rhine/Danube frontier system (§14) — a household here can prosper on toll, transit, and portage income in a way no other region's own economy is built to emphasize this directly.

---

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

Raetia and Noricum's own real Legal Status mix reflects §1's own dual origin story: Noricum's real peaceful transition brought a comparatively smoother, faster path toward citizen and Latin-rights status for its own native elite, while Raetia's own conquered population moved up that same ladder somewhat more slowly, though — by any reasonable playthrough's own start date — both provinces are long since past any live tension over the distinction (§6). Politics & Patronage's own cursus honorum is a genuinely distant, difficult prospect from either province — this is quiet, provincial, unglamorous territory, not a stepping-stone to Roman prominence the way Iberian Colony's own Balbus-of-Gades precedent offers.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — A Shared Frontier, Not a New One

This document deliberately doesn't invent a new named external Frontier relationship for this region. Raetia and Noricum both border the same broader Germanic frontier system Gallic Frontier already claims as its own primary relationship (Starting Regions: Gallic Frontier §5) — this region shares that frontier's own general character rather than contesting Gaul's own primacy over it, the same non-competing resolution this project has already applied to Parthia (Greek East and Syria) and Tin (Gaul and Britannia). A real, standing but comparatively modest legionary presence exists here for most of the range, guarding the Alpine approaches rather than a live, contested border the way the Rhine itself is.

---

## 6. Reputation Duality — None, and Genuinely So

Consistent with §1's own finding, this document designates the Alpine Provinces' Reputation Duality as **None** — the third region on this roster, after Anatolia and Sicily, to reach this same honest conclusion rather than search for tension that isn't really there. Both provinces settle quickly and durably after their own real, different founding moments (§15.1–15.2), and neither carries anything resembling Judaea's, Britannia's, or Dacia's own genuine, lasting local friction.

---

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

Raetian religious tradition reflects the real, distinct (and, worth noting honestly, only thinly attested) non-Celtic Rhaetic linguistic and cultural identity native to the region — one of the more genuinely obscure native traditions on this entire roster, comparable in its own thin historical record to Numidian or Illyrian/Pannonian tradition elsewhere. Noric religious tradition blends this same general Alpine-native character with real, older influence from the Hallstatt cultural tradition (§8) that predates Rome's own arrival by centuries — a genuine, deep local antiquity this region can claim independent of anything Rome itself brought with it.

---

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade — Steel and Salt, Not Gold and Grain

This region's own goods profile is genuinely unlike anywhere else on the roster: **Noric steel** (*ferrum Noricum*) was real-historically famous across the ancient Mediterranean for its exceptional quality, used in real, documented Roman weapons and tools — a functional, military-grade metal identity distinct from every other region's own precious-metal or building-stone tags (Iberian and Dacian gold, Numidian and Pentelic marble, Cornish tin). **Salt**, mined in this region since long before Rome's own arrival — the real, historically significant Hallstatt mining tradition, old enough to give its own name to an entire prehistoric European archaeological period — gives this region a second, genuinely ancient extractive identity. **Raetian wine**, real-historically praised highly enough in antiquity that Augustus himself is recorded favoring it, rounds out this region's own goods profile with a genuine agricultural bright spot despite §2's own difficult terrain.

## 9. Population & Culture Distribution — A Second Gap This Document Surfaces

Consistent with Sicily's own honest precedent (Starting Regions: Sicily §9), this document surfaces rather than papers over a real gap: neither Raetian nor Noric culture has a tracked value in Cultures of the Known World yet. This document doesn't invent one unilaterally — it names the real population honestly and flags the gap in §18 for that document's own future consideration, the same restraint Sicily's document already modeled.

| Population | Presence |
|---|---|
| Native Raetian (Rhaetic-speaking, non-Celtic) | Dominant in Raetia specifically |
| Native Noric (Celtic-influenced, per the real historical Noric kingdom) | Dominant in Noricum specifically |
| Roman/Latin (colonists, administrators, and — per §1 — a comparatively faster-integrating Noric elite specifically) | Common |
| Any other population (traders passing through via this region's own real corridor role, per §14) | Rare, individual-level outliers |

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, reflecting §1's own dual origin story directly.

- **The household of Turixa** *(seated near Curia)* — a native Raetian family carrying a plausible, invented Rhaetic-pattern name, given how thin the real attested personal-name record for this specific culture actually is — echoing every other native-named household on this roster while being honest that this one's own naming grounding is thinner than most.
- **Gens Aquilia** *(seated at Virunum)* — a Noric family whose own comparatively smooth, peaceful path into the Roman world (§1, §4) gave it an earlier and easier route to full integration than a conquered population typically enjoys elsewhere on this roster.
- **Gens Otacilia** *(seated along the main Alpine trade route)* — a toll-and-transit family built on this region's own real corridor economy (§3, §14), prospering from controlling movement rather than producing goods directly — a genuinely distinct wealth model from every other rival house on this roster.
- **Gens Claudia** *(seated at Augusta Vindelicorum)* — a family tracing its own standing to real, ongoing service connected to the original Tiberian-Drusan conquest of Raetia (§15.1), carrying the same broad, real gens name as the campaign's own historical commander without claiming direct descent from him specifically — consistent with how this project has already handled Gens Vitellia and Gens Iulia Turona elsewhere.

## 12. Home Anchor

**In a valley along the main Alpine trade route, between Raetia and Noricum.** Consistent with §6's own genuinely calm designation, there's no danger here to lean toward or away from — this document instead anchors the household directly astride §3's and §14's own defining economic identity, the corridor itself, rather than in either province's own administrative center.

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Consistent with the precedent Sicily's own document set (Starting Regions: Sicily §13), this document adds no further reading to any of Core §5.2's four archetypes — every rival house here is left unmapped. A region this calm, this specifically built around a corridor-and-craft economy rather than land or conquest, doesn't need a forced connection to templates written with land, war, and political ambition primarily in mind.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 The Conquest of Raetia (15 BC, closed history by default)

The real campaign led by Tiberius and Drusus — Augustus's own stepsons — subduing Raetia by force. Closed history for any ordinary default-era playthrough, but the direct real grounding behind Gens Claudia's own backstory (§11) and this region's own §1 contrast with Noricum's different path.

### 15.2 The Peaceful Annexation of Noricum (c. 16 BC, closed history by default, deliberately anticlimactic)

A genuine structural novelty for this project's own Timeline Hooks: every other region's own founding moment involves real conflict, negotiation-under-pressure, or at minimum a formal treaty process. Noricum's real transition from client kingdom to province happened without a war at all — this document names that absence of drama as the hook's own actual content, a deliberate contrast with §15.1 rather than an oversight.

### 15.3 The Marcomannic Wars Reach Italy's Own Doorstep (AD 166–180, era-conditional)

A third, complementary treatment of the same real war Anatolia (from Pontus's side) and The Balkans (from the Danube's own side) have already named — this document's own contribution is genuinely distinct: real Marcomanni and Quadi raiding parties pushed through the Alpine passes far enough during this conflict to threaten Aquileia in northern Italy directly, the closest any hostile force comes to invading the Italian heartland itself anywhere in this game's own range. Where Anatolia and The Balkans tell this war's own origin-point and main-battleground stories respectively, this region tells the story of how close it came to reaching Rome's own doorstep.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** the second region built entirely outside the framework's own originally-named candidate list, following Sicily.
- **Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples:** §5 deliberately defers to Gallic Frontier's own existing Germanic relationship rather than inventing a competing claim.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 introduces Noric steel and Hallstatt salt as genuinely new goods identities, distinct from every metal or mineral tag used elsewhere on this roster.
- **Cultures of the Known World:** §9 surfaces a second real gap — no tracked Raetian or Noric culture value exists yet — flagged honestly rather than resolved unilaterally, consistent with Sicily's own precedent.
- **Events:** §15.2 is this project's first Timeline Hook whose entire point is the real absence of conflict; §15.3 is this project's third complementary treatment of the Marcomannic Wars, following Anatolia's and The Balkans' own prior angles on the same real conflict.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), directly reflecting §1's own dual conquest-versus-peaceful-annexation origin story.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and this region's own steel/salt/wine output are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether Raetian and Noric culture warrant their own tracked values in Cultures of the Known World**, mirroring the same open question Sicily's own document raised for its own population — a genuine, growing case for that document's own next pass to address more than one surfaced gap at once.
- **The household of Turixa's own naming grounding.** §11 is honest that the real Rhaetic personal-name record is thinner than almost any other native-named household's own basis elsewhere on this roster — worth a specialist review if this document's own invented name ever needs firmer real grounding.
- **Whether this region's own "all-land crossroads" status (§14) should carry any mechanical Travel benefit** beyond its own favorable Distance Tiers — this document treats the effect as already captured in those Tiers, but a future Travel-system pass could reasonably ask whether overland routes should behave differently from sea routes in ways this document doesn't specify.
- **Whether Noricum's own peaceful-annexation status (§15.2) should ever manifest as a distinct Legal Status or Cultural Drift bonus** for a Noric-heritage household specifically, given its real, comparatively smoother historical path into the Roman world — this document names the real history but doesn't propose a concrete mechanical reward for it.
