# GENS — Reference Document: Occupations & Trades
*A pure flavor and naming roster — no new mechanics, no new stats, no new gates. Every named Position in Companions & Court Positions, every pop group in Settlement Demographics, and every specialist Trait-linked profession (Historian, Master Craftsman, Augur, and the rest) already has a real mechanical home somewhere else in this project. What none of them supply is the ordinary word: the job title a lazily-instantiated Character, a Wandering Population encounter, a Notable Business proprietor, a Social Places extra, a piece of Graffiti, or a line of Correspondence actually reaches for. This document is that word bank — built the same way the Bestiary was built for fauna: real, historically attested, organized for practical use, and honest about carrying no numbers at all.*

## 1. Purpose & Scope

Three documents already carve up the working world at three different altitudes: Companions & Court Positions names the roles inside the player's own household; Settlement Demographics names the aggregate economic sectors a background population sorts into; a handful of Traits (Historian, Poet, Master Craftsman, Herbalist, and others) name the rare specialists whose work produces a tracked object or a mechanical bonus. None of the three was ever meant to answer a much smaller, much more constant question: when the game needs to describe an ordinary person — a stranger on the road, a rival's cousin, a name on a shop sign, a face at a Social Place, a client at the morning Salutatio — what does it call them?

This document answers that question with real Latin trade titles, real historical grounding, and nothing else. It adds no stat, no gate, no formula, and no new field to any existing record beyond a purely descriptive tag. Its job is to be read from, not built on top of.

---

## 2. How to Read This Roster

Every entry carries the same shape: a **Name**, its **Latin Term** where one is distinctly attested, a one-line **Real Grounding** note, and three flavor-only tags — **Typical Legal Status** (which tier most commonly held this job in reality; never a requirement or restriction), **Typical Gender Association** (again, description of historical norms, never a lock), and **Rural/Urban** setting. A **Cross-Reference** column, where populated, means this occupation already has a real mechanical home elsewhere and this entry exists purely to connect the ordinary word to that system.

## 18. Infamia — A Flavor-Only Note, Not a New Mechanic

Buildings & Production Chains flagged, and left explicitly open, that Roman law marked certain occupations — most centrally the actor, the gladiator, and sex work — with *infamia*, a real social/legal stigma layered on top of ordinary citizen/freedman/enslaved status. This document does not resolve that open question; building an actual Legal Status stigma flag remains Familia's own future decision to make. What this document does, honestly and without overreaching its own pure-flavor scope, is note which entries above carried that real historical association — Actor, Dancer, Mime Performer, Gladiator (cross-referenced, §17), the Public Executioner, and, to a lesser and more variable real degree, the Innkeeper and Tavern-Keeper — so that whenever a future pass does build that mechanic, or whenever generated flavor text wants to gesture at it honestly in the meantime, the real grounding is already sitting here waiting rather than needing to be researched from scratch. Consistent with Buildings' own deliberate restraint around the Brothel (§9 of that document: "nothing about this building is depicted or narrated beyond a name"), this roster does not add a named occupation entry for sex work specifically — the same real *infamia* association applies, but naming it no further than that respects a restraint decision this project already made rather than reopening it here.

## 19. Cross-System Integration

- **Characters:** this roster is the direct, ready-made word source for a lazily-instantiated Character's backfilled occupation flavor, wherever `source` isn't `"familia"` or a formal Position.
- **Paperdoll & Appearance:** the Hands descriptor (§4.4 of that document) already reads off a Character's current Duty Slot or Court Position; this roster extends the same logic to a background Character with no formal slot at all.
- **Settlement Demographics:** every Pop Group sector (Coloni, Operarii, Opifices, Negotiatores, Aeditui) now has real, specific named trades sitting underneath its own aggregate figure.
- **Notable Businesses:** a proprietor's own occupation title is now drawn from a real roster rather than invented ad hoc per business.
- **Wandering Populations:** several Wanderer types (Merchants & Peddlers, Physicians) gain concrete, named trade variety beneath their own archetype label.
- **Social Places:** background Characters encountered at a Tavern, Forum, or Thermopolium now carry a real, specific occupation rather than remaining unlabeled extras.
- **Graffiti, Dynamic Walls & Rumors:** Commercial Notices (§2 of that document) now have a real named trade behind whichever shop is advertising.
- **Correspondence & Letters:** the Tabellarius entry (§9) is this document's own direct tie to that system's physical delivery mechanism.
- **Collegia & Guilds:** several entries above (Fuller, Baker, Ropemaker, and others) are exactly the real trades that document's own guild structure organizes — a natural membership-flavor source for that document to draw on.
- **Labor & Slavery:** the Typical Legal Status tag throughout this roster gives that document's own enslaved-labor texture real, specific occupational variety beyond generic "fieldwork" or "domestic" Labor Skill categories.
- **Cultures of the Known World / Starting Regions:** §16's regional variants extend directly from that document's own roster and each region's own established economic identity.
- **Flora & Herbal Registry** *(future)*: the Herbalist/Apothecary entry (§10) is a direct, ready-made forward tie once that document is built.
- **Legal & Court:** the Advocate/Causidicus entry (§15) is a direct, concrete occupational tie to that document's own case-argument mechanics.
- **Buildings & Production Chains:** nearly every production-chain building named across that document now has a real worker's job title to go with its output, rather than an anonymous conversion process.
- **Private Ships & Shipping Ventures:** the Shipwright and Diver entries (§9) give that document's own vessel-construction and salvage content real, named trade texture.
- **Crime, Punishment & Imprisonment / Legal & Court:** the Prison-Keeper and Public Executioner entries (§15) give both documents' own punishment machinery real, named operators, consistent with the restrained, described-not-depicted treatment this project applies to punishment throughout.
- **Discovery Roster:** the Waystation-Keeper entry (§9) is a direct, concrete tie to the Cursus Publicus Formalization Discovery, giving that Discovery's own real institution a named face.
- **Art & Art Commissions:** the Pictor cross-reference (§17) closes a small gap — that document's own painters previously had no entry point from this roster.

## 21. Open Questions

- **Whether the flavor-only tags (Legal Status, Gender, Rural/Urban) should ever inform a future generation-weighting algorithm** — this document deliberately treats them as descriptive only, per direction; a future implementation pass may reasonably choose to weight lazy-instantiation rolls by them.
- **The *infamia* stigma flag itself.** §18 deliberately doesn't resolve Buildings' own standing open question — that remains Familia's decision to make, whenever it's made.
- **Regional coverage depth.** §16 covers twelve cultures with a real, specific trade variant; a culture without an entry there simply uses the Roman-core roster — the same honest, non-exhaustive treatment the Bestiary and Hair/Body Marking documents both already established for their own regional content.
- **Whether Collegia & Guilds should eventually reference specific `occupationId` values directly** for its own membership rosters, rather than naming trades in its own prose independently.
- **Total roster size.** This pass lands at roughly 125 named entries across fourteen sectors; whether a future pass should add further granularity (splitting, say, "Baker" into separate bread-tiers) is left open, consistent with this document's own intentionally lightweight, non-mechanical scope.
- **Sex work.** §18 deliberately declines to add a named occupation entry here, matching Buildings' own existing restraint around the Brothel — worth revisiting only if that document's own treatment ever changes.
- **The Quaestionarius (judicial torturer of enslaved witnesses).** A real, historically attested role adjacent to the Carnifex and Custos Carceris entries (§15); deliberately left out of this pass as a step further into punishment detail than this document's own light, flavor-only scope calls for.
