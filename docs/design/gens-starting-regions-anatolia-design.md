# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Anatolia / Asia Minor
*Final polish and balance pass. The fourth extensible-slate region to receive its own full document, and the first to require real, honest renegotiation with the framework's own original pitch. Starting Regions §5.2 originally described this region's strongest hooks as "Galatian, Cappadocian, and Cilician cultures, Rhodian Sea Law's real historical home... and a strong Education & Culture tie via the Institutions of Renown already named there" — but Greek East's own finalized document has since claimed Cappadocia as its own internal frontier sub-area, and Rhodes and Pergamon as two of its own eight Gazetteer entries, Institution of Renown and all. This document doesn't contest any of that. It finds this region's own real, still-substantial identity in what's left: Galatia, Cilicia, Bithynia-Pontus, and the western Anatolian coast beyond Pergamon and Rhodes specifically — genuinely enough territory and real history to stand on its own. This pass renamed a rival house from Brogitarix to Brogimarix after finding it sat too close to Brogitarus, a real historical Galatian king attested in one of Cicero's surviving orations. Intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Building Around What's Already Claimed

This document is this project's first honest illustration of what building an extensible slate one region at a time actually looks like in practice: by the time this document exists, three of the framework's own original hooks for this region are already spoken for. Cappadocia is Greek East's own internal frontier sub-area (Starting Regions: Greek East §5). Rhodes and Pergamon are two of that same document's own eight Gazetteer entries, each carrying a real Institution of Renown (Education & Culture §5.1). This document doesn't re-litigate any of it — consistent with how North African Colony deferred to Iberian Colony on Garum, and Britannia deferred to Gallic Frontier on Tin, the earlier claim stands.

What remains is still genuinely substantial: **Galatia**, a real, fascinating case of a foreign population — Celtic tribes who migrated into central Anatolia generations before this game's own range — becoming something new rather than simply assimilating into an existing native identity, "living Cultural Drift as lived history" per Cultures of the Known World. **Cilicia**, the south coast, real historical home of the Mediterranean's most notorious pirates before Pompey's own real 67 BC suppression campaign. **Bithynia-Pontus**, the real northern province facing the Black Sea, genuinely well-documented thanks to a real surviving correspondence between a Roman governor and the Emperor himself. And the western coast beyond Pergamon and Rhodes specifically — Ephesus, Sardis, Halicarnassus — carrying real fame and real wealth entirely independent of anything Greek East already claimed.

---

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

A genuinely varied terrain profile: real fertile river valleys along the western coast (the Cayster and Meander valleys among them), a real, distinct central plateau in Galatia proper, a rugged southern coastline in Cilicia that historically sheltered real pirate strongholds as readily as legitimate ports, and a northern coastal strip along the Black Sea in Bithynia-Pontus with its own genuinely different maritime character from the Aegean or Mediterranean coasts every other region on this roster fronts. Real marble deposits exist here too — Docimium marble, a genuine, distinctively veined stone prized in antiquity — giving this region its own building-stone identity alongside the Italian, Iberian, Numidian, and Pentelic/Parian traditions already established elsewhere.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* The western coast supports genuinely deep, wealthy markets — Ephesus and Sardis both carry real historical reputations for extraordinary wealth, Sardis's own so pronounced that the real King Croesus who once ruled there gave the wider world the actual etymological root of "rich as Croesus." Galatia and Bithynia-Pontus run more modestly, closer to Greek East's own Achaean model without quite matching its own top-tier prosperity. Cilicia's own economy carries a real, distinct historical shadow: a coastal economy that once ran substantially on piracy before Pompey's suppression forced a real transition toward ordinary legitimate trade (§11, §15.1).

---

## 4. Political & Legal Texture — Governance by Correspondence

Bithynia-Pontus gives this region a real, genuinely well-documented illustration of provincial governance that no other region on this roster can offer with this much specificity: a real surviving correspondence between a Roman governor stationed there and the Emperor Trajan himself, covering a range of real administrative and legal questions the governor wasn't confident enough to resolve alone — including, in one particularly well-known real exchange, how to handle the region's own early Christian community. This document doesn't invent or dramatize the content of that real correspondence; it simply names the pattern it illustrates as a genuine, concrete Correspondence & Letters (§7 of that document) case study — governance conducted at a real remove, decisions genuinely deferred upward rather than made locally, in a way this project hasn't modeled with this much documentary specificity anywhere else.

The western coast's own cities carried real, varying degrees of civic privilege short of Athens's or Rhodes's own full free-city status (Starting Regions: Greek East §4) — a real, honest middle tier of provincial city autonomy this document is well-positioned to illustrate precisely because it isn't claiming the roster's own two clearest free-city examples.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — No External Neighbor of Its Own

A structural point worth stating plainly, consistent with this document's own §1: this region has **no external Frontier or Great Power relationship of its own**. Cappadocia's own real Parthian-facing frontier belongs to Greek East (Starting Regions: Greek East §5); this region's own eastern edge simply borders that already-claimed territory rather than opening a second relationship with the same neighbor. Cilicia's own real historical threat was maritime rather than a land frontier — Mediterranean piracy, not a tribal people — and Pompey's real 67 BC campaign (§15.1) resolved it well before this game's own range typically opens in earnest. This region carries no standing legionary garrison of its own for most of its range, a genuinely peaceful military profile closer to Greek East's own Achaean heartland than to any of this roster's frontier regions.

---

## 6. Reputation Duality — None, and Genuinely So

Consistent with §5's own observation, this document designates Anatolia/Asia Minor's Reputation Duality as **None** — the same designation Italian Heartland and Greek East carry, and, per this document's own honest accounting, for the same underlying reason: no live external conquest, no ongoing local resentment worth tracking, no sub-area carrying anything like Judaea's or Britannia's own genuine tension. This isn't every extensible region needing its own novel mechanical wrinkle — sometimes the historically honest answer is that a region is simply calm, and this document treats that as a legitimate, unremarkable finding rather than searching for tension that isn't really there.

---

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults — A Wonder, and a People Still Becoming Themselves

**Ephesus** resolves a real, dangling thread this project planted before this document existed to claim it: Religions of the Known World's own Cult of Artemis of Ephesus (§5 of that document) names the real Temple of Artemis — one of the actual Seven Wonders of the ancient world — without yet anchoring it to a selectable region's own Gazetteer. This document claims it directly (§10), the same kind of source-resolving move this project has already made for Cornish Tin, Cyrenaican Silphium, and Levantine Tyrian Purple. **Halicarnassus** offers this region a second real Wonder connection: the real Mausoleum of Halicarnassus, still substantially standing throughout this entire game's own range even though it predates it by centuries.

Galatian religious identity is this region's own most genuinely distinctive thread: per Religions of the Known World's own Galatian Religious Blend entry, the Gallic druidic tradition transplanted into Anatolia was genuinely syncretized with the surrounding Hellenic and native Anatolian religious environment rather than staying a pure import — a real, living case of a people actively becoming something new rather than either fully assimilating or fully resisting. This document's own household of Brogimarix (§11) is built specifically to embody that in-between state rather than reading as a fixed native identity the way Cintugnatus, Massuna, or Togirix do.

---

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

**Cilicium**, the coarse goat-hair cloth this region's own name directly gives to the wider world (the word survives, via Latin, into later usage for rough haircloth generally), is this document's own concrete, distinctly-named signature good — a real, specific etymological gift matching Cyprus's own "copper" precedent (Cultures of the Known World §5.1). Docimium marble (§2) gives this region a further distinct building-stone identity. Anatolian wool, genuinely valued in antiquity, and Bithynia-Pontus's own Black Sea timber and fish trade round out this region's economic profile — a genuinely modest goods list compared to some regions on this roster, but an honest one rather than an inflated one.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, none tied to the roster's largest existing Templated Background clusters (§13), and one built specifically to embody cultural transition rather than a fixed identity.

- **The household of Diodoros** *(seated at Ephesus)* — a wealthy family whose fortune runs through the Temple of Artemis's own real historical role as a genuine financial institution as much as a religious one — ancient temples of this scale often functioned as real treasuries and lenders, a concrete, distinct wealth source unlike any other rival house on this roster.
- **The household of Brogimarix** *(seated at Ancyra)* — a Galatian aristocratic family, deliberately written as still genuinely in cultural transition rather than settled into either a purely Celtic or a purely Hellenic identity, carrying the real, attested Celtic *-rix* suffix (Cultures of the Known World §13) blended with an increasingly Hellenized household life — this region's own single clearest illustration of §7's own "still becoming themselves" theme.
- **The household of Straton** *(seated at Tarsus)* — a Cilician trade family with a real, quietly acknowledged history: ancestors who profited from the region's own pre-suppression piracy economy, now operating in thoroughly legitimate trade, a genuinely distinct narrative texture — a disreputable past made respectable by time — that no other rival house on this roster carries quite this directly.
- **Gens Aemilia** *(seated at Nicomedia)* — a Roman senatorial-administrative family embodying §4's own governance-by-correspondence theme directly, its own members plausibly among those real provincial officials who wrote home for guidance rather than acting alone.

## 12. Home Anchor

**In the Cayster valley, near Ephesus.** This anchors the household in this region's own wealthiest, most developed corner, consistent with §6's own genuinely calm designation — there's no defining tension to lean into or away from here the way Vesuvius, the Rhine, or Egypt's mining labor demand elsewhere, so this document simply anchors the player where the real historical prosperity actually concentrated.

---

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Given how saturated this roster's own archetype pool has become — **"provincial notable"** now carries six readings, **"jumped-up equestrian merchant"** four — this document deliberately adds only one further reading rather than forcing connections that would dilute rather than enrich the pattern. Gens Aemilia gives **"veteran given a land grant"** a fourth flavor, alongside Gallic Frontier's primary claim and Britannia's and Syria's own secondary readings, here grounded in ordinary senatorial administrative service rather than any of those three regions' own military-colonist or frontier-garrison stories. The household of Straton's own reformed-piracy backstory is deliberately left unmapped to any of Core §5.2's four archetypes — not every rival house needs to illustrate a starting background template, and forcing one here would flatten a genuinely novel narrative texture into a shape it doesn't naturally fit.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 Pompey's Campaign Against the Cilician Pirates (67 BC, closed history by default)

The real, defining resolution of this region's own most dangerous historical chapter — a large-scale Roman campaign specifically targeting Cilicia's own real pirate strongholds, Coracesium (§10) chief among them. Closed history for any default-era playthrough, but the direct real grounding behind the household of Straton's own backstory (§11) and this region's own genuinely peaceful default state (§5, §6).

### 15.2 The Mithridatic Wars, From Pontus's Own Side (89–63 BC, closed history by default)

Greek East's own document (§14.2) already names the Mithridatic Wars as a Timeline Hook, framed around the conflict's own wider impact on "the whole Greek East." This document names the same real war from its own home territory's perspective: Pontus was Mithridates VI's own actual kingdom, and this war's real outcome is the direct reason Bithynia-Pontus exists as Roman territory at all — the war's origin point, not merely its ripple effect, complementing rather than duplicating Greek East's own treatment.

### 15.3 Pliny's Correspondence with Trajan (c. AD 110–112, era-conditional)

The real, genuinely well-documented governor-to-Emperor correspondence already named in §4 — a real historical record, not a war or a revolt, giving this region's own Timeline Hook roster a third genuinely novel hook type this project has produced (alongside Egypt's sociological Alexandrian tensions and Syria's mid-campaign natural disaster): governance itself, conducted by letter, as the actual historical drama.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** promotes Anatolia/Asia Minor from the extensible slate (§5.2) to a fully realized region, and explicitly corrects that document's own original pitch — Cappadocia, Rhodes, and Pergamon are Greek East's own claims, not this region's.
- **Religions of the Known World:** §7 resolves the Cult of Artemis of Ephesus's own dangling regional anchor, and inherits the Galatian Religious Blend entry directly.
- **Education & Culture:** this document deliberately does not claim a third Institution of Renown, since Athens, Rhodes, and Pergamon already give Greek East all three named Hellenic institutions (Starting Regions: Greek East §16) — this region's own Education & Culture tie runs through Galatian Cultural Drift instead (§7, §11).
- **Correspondence & Letters:** §4's own governance-by-correspondence theme is this document's single most substantial new cross-system contribution — a real, concrete case study that system didn't have an anchor for before.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 names Cilicium as a new, etymologically-grounded good and Docimium marble as a further distinct building-stone source.
- **Piracy & Banditry:** Coracesium (§10) and Pompey's campaign (§15.1) are this region's own real, historical (rather than live) contribution to that system's own content.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), including this roster's first family built around a reformed disreputable past rather than any of this project's existing rival-house archetypes.
- **Events:** §15.2 is this project's first Timeline Hook explicitly written as a complementary, non-duplicating companion to another region's own existing hook for the same real war.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and this region's own goods output are left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether this region's own genuine calm (§6) undersells it relative to busier regions on the roster.** This document treats "nothing much is wrong here" as an honest finding rather than a gap to fill, but a future playtesting pass may want to confirm that a Reputation-Duality-free, frontier-free region still offers enough standing mechanical texture to be worth selecting over Greek East's own similar but richer profile.
- **Whether Docimium marble and Cilicium warrant their own new Resources & Goods entries** or can fold into that document's own existing Marble and generic textile categories — this document names them as real and distinct but doesn't resolve their own formal goods-registry status.
- **The household of Brogimarix's own long-term cultural trajectory** — whether a Galatian household's own Cultural Drift should, over a long playthrough, resolve toward a stable Hellenic identity, revert toward its Celtic roots, or remain permanently in-between, is a genuine open question this document raises rather than answers, tied directly to Education & Culture's own existing Cultural Drift mechanics.
- **Whether "governance by correspondence" (§4) deserves a reusable mechanical pattern** beyond this region's own flavor use of it, given how well-documented the real Pliny-Trajan case is compared to most other provincial governance this project has modeled.
