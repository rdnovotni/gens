# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Mesopotamia
*Final version, incorporating a direct revision to the region's own core premise. The ninth extensible-slate region, and the most extreme structural departure this project has attempted — now made deliberately, explicitly ahistorical at one specific point, by direction. Trajan's real Parthian War (AD 113–117) briefly created formal Roman provinces here and in Assyria before Hadrian's real withdrawal in AD 117. This document keeps that withdrawal as the honest historical default — most playthroughs will still see it happen exactly as history records — but, per direction, opens a genuine, difficult path for it not to: a household whose own success in the region is extraordinary enough can plausibly change Hadrian's own real policy calculus, even though his own real accession and Trajan's own real death stay fixed exactly as they happened. This document is explicit throughout about where the real history ends and the deliberate departure begins.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — The Two-Year Colony, Unless the Household Changes That

Every region built before this one, Armenia included, offers a household a real, if sometimes unusual, span of time to build a life. Mesopotamia's own honest historical default offers two years. Trajan's real Parthian War (AD 113–117) briefly created formal Roman provinces here and in Assyria — ordinary administrative structure, a real governor, real land grants — before Hadrian's own real, deliberate decision to abandon both undid it entirely. This document treats that as the region's own default identity: a household that selects it should expect, going in, that its whole premise is a certain, foreknown ending, and that most of the actual gameplay is what the household does with that knowledge (§16).

**Per direction, this document also builds a genuine, difficult exception into that default — an explicit, acknowledged departure from this project's own standing rule that real, settled historical outcomes aren't rewritten by player action.** Trajan's own real death and Hadrian's own real accession in AD 117 stay exactly where history puts them, the same as every other Named Historical Figure's real biographical facts on this roster (Events: Historical Timeline Content §6). What becomes genuinely contingent is narrower and more honest than rewriting history outright: **Hadrian's own real policy choice about this one specific territory** — a decision, not a biographical fact — can plausibly land differently if a household's own governance here is extraordinary enough to change the calculus that led the real Hadrian to withdraw (§15.3, §16). This document is explicit throughout about exactly where the real history ends and this deliberate departure begins, rather than blurring the two together.

This document is best understood as a genuine hybrid — mechanically shaped like every other Region Profile document, but functioning in practice closer to a Scenario Start (Core §5.4) with full regional depth behind it, and now carrying its own internal fork between a historically faithful default outcome and a rare, difficult, openly ahistorical alternative.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

The real Tigris-Euphrates river system dominates this region's own terrain — genuinely fertile alluvial land between the two rivers, some of the oldest continuously cultivated ground anywhere in the ancient world, long before Rome, Parthia, or any empire this game tracks ever existed. Real desert terrain borders the cultivated river valleys on both sides. This is rich land in the abstract; this document's own §3 is honest that two years is nowhere near enough time for a household to actually develop it the way a longer-settled region's own economy reflects generations of investment.

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Genuinely undeveloped from a Roman household's own perspective, whatever its real underlying fertility — there simply wasn't time for Roman-style estate agriculture, trade infrastructure, or market depth to take root before the withdrawal. A household here is working with real potential and no real head start, the most extreme version of "raw settlement" this project has ever modeled, more so even than Gallic Frontier's or Britannia's own founding years, because Gaul and Britain at least got decades before this project's own default start date typically arrives; Mesopotamia never gets more than two.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

For the brief real duration this region exists, ordinary Roman provincial administration and Legal Status genuinely applied — a real governor, real land grants, real citizen and Latin-rights status extended to colonists and cooperative local elites alike. This document doesn't complicate that; the complication is entirely temporal (§1), not administrative. What makes this region's own Legal Status picture unusual isn't its shape, but its default, foreknown expiration — every Legal Status gain a household makes here is real while it lasts and then, absent the rare Sustained Occupation Divergence (§16), becomes a real, live problem the moment Roman administration actually leaves.

## 5. The Household's Own Two Real Options — Roman Colonist or Local Household Under New Rule

Consistent with the precedent Armenia's own document set (Starting Regions: Armenia §5), this document names two genuinely different, both-legitimate ways a household enters this region's own brief window:

- **A Roman colonist or veteran household**, granted land as part of Trajan's own real settlement push — this document's own recommended default, and the option every other region's own "veteran given a land grant" Templated Background archetype most naturally maps onto, though here with the built-in twist that the grant itself carries a default expiration date the household doesn't yet know when this playthrough begins, escapable only through the rare Sustained Occupation Divergence (§16).
- **A local household — Babylonian, Aramaic, Greek-Seleucid-descended, or Babylonian-Jewish (§9)** — already living in this region when Roman administration briefly arrives, experiencing conquest, however administratively straightforward, as something happening *to* them rather than something they came to build. This option carries real, additional weight given §15.2's own Kitos War content, and this document treats it with the same care extended to every other region's own difficult history.

## 6. Reputation Duality — Not Yet Formed, Honestly

Consistent with this project's own established willingness to find "no, this doesn't apply" as an honest answer (Anatolia, Sicily, and the Alpine Provinces all reached "None" for ordinary reasons; Armenia found the entire framework didn't fit and built a replacement), this document reaches a third kind of honest conclusion for the region's own historical default span: **there simply isn't enough time for Reputation Duality, in any of this project's existing shapes, to meaningfully develop within two years.** This document doesn't invent a mechanic to fill that gap for the default timeline — it names the absence directly, the same restraint Armenia's own document showed by building only what the real history actually supported.

This finding has one real exception, per §16: if a household achieves the rare Sustained Occupation Divergence and the region persists past AD 117, Reputation Duality starts accumulating from that point forward like any genuinely new, fresh occupation — most plausibly at Full intensity, the same shape Gallic Frontier and Britannia use, since an artificially-extended presence would still be young and contested rather than settled.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults — Faded Glory, Living Faith

A real, poignant detail worth naming directly: Babylon itself still stood, by this era, as a real but greatly diminished shadow of its own ancient glory — and Trajan himself is recorded as having visited its ruins and remarked on how far the once-great city had fallen. This document leans into that resonance rather than avoiding it: Rome's own brief, doomed presence here echoes Babylon's own long, slow fade from world-defining power to a real, minor provincial town, two different empires' own transience sitting side by side in the same real place.

This region's own living religious identity runs through several real, distinct threads: local Babylonian and Aramaic tradition, genuine Hellenic religious practice at Seleucia (§10, founded as a real Greek colonial city under the earlier Seleucid dynasty), Parthian Zoroastrian-adjacent influence, and a real, deep, historically significant Jewish community — Babylonian Jewry, whose own roots reached back centuries before this game's own range even opens, to the real historical Babylonian Exile. This last thread carries particular weight given §15.2.

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

Genuinely underdeveloped from a Roman commercial perspective, consistent with §3's own honest accounting — real Mesopotamian agricultural fertility (grain, dates, and other river-valley produce) exists, but this document doesn't claim any of it as a developed, Roman-integrated trade good the way every other region's own goods profile assumes years of established production. What real value this region offers a household is Ctesiphon's own captured wealth (§10, §15.1) — a real, one-time windfall rather than an ongoing production chain, consistent with this region's own entire compressed, non-durable economic character.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Three houses, deliberately fewer than the usual four, consistent with Armenia's own precedent for a region this compressed in scope.

- **Gens Marcia** *(seated near a Roman land grant outside Seleucia)* — a veteran-colonist family building on newly granted land, this document's own concrete illustration of §5's recommended default household, its own optimism about the future carrying real, built-in dramatic irony the household itself doesn't yet know about.
- **The household of Marduka** *(seated near Babylon)* — a native Babylonian-Aramaic family, its own name a generic theophoric echo of Marduk in the same naming pattern this project's other theophoric-tradition cultures already use, watching a third empire pass through their ancestral land without particular illusion that this one will last any longer than the others have.
- **The household of Yehuda** *(seated near Seleucia)* — a Babylonian-Jewish family with real, deep roots predating this game's own entire range, given a common, safely generic Hebrew name in the same register as Syria's own household of Eleazar — and, per §15.2, a household whose own real historical moment is defined less by Rome's arrival than by the real, widespread Jewish revolt breaking out across the diaspora at almost the exact same time.

## 12. Home Anchor

**Near Seleucia**, on the Tigris across from Ctesiphon. This anchors the household at the real center of gravity of this region's own brief existence — close enough to Trajan's own captured prize to feel its weight, and close enough to Seleucia's own large, real Hellenic population to give a Roman colonist household genuine, functioning civic infrastructure to work with rather than nothing at all.

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Gens Marcia gives **"veteran given a land grant"** its own most historically compressed reading yet — every other region's own use of this archetype (Gallic Frontier's primary claim, Britannia's and Syria's own secondary readings) assumes the land grant is the *start* of a multi-generational story. Here, per §1 and §16, the household knows — or will soon learn — that the grant itself is temporary, a genuinely distinct emotional register for an archetype this project has otherwise treated as a stable, durable beginning.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks — A Compressed, Inevitable Arc

Unlike every other region's own Timeline Hooks, spread across decades or centuries, this region's own entire hook sequence fits inside three real years — a genuine structural first reflecting §1's own compressed premise directly.

### 15.1 The Conquest of Mesopotamia and the Capture of Ctesiphon (AD 115–116)

Trajan's real campaign not only annexed this region but captured Parthia's own actual capital — the single most dramatic military achievement against Parthia anywhere in this game's own range, and the founding moment of this region's entire selectable existence.

### 15.2 The Kitos War (AD 115–117, handled with real care)

A real, widespread Jewish revolt broke out across much of the Roman world — Cyrenaica, Egypt, Cyprus, and Mesopotamia specifically — during the exact same years as Trajan's own campaign, seriously complicating Roman control of the newly won territory at the very moment it was least prepared to absorb a crisis. Consistent with this project's own standing restraint around every other region's own difficult history (Judaea's revolts, Sicily's Servile Wars, Boudicca), this document names the revolt factually, acknowledges its own real scale and significance, and doesn't dramatize or invent detail beyond what's already established elsewhere in this project. The household of Yehuda (§11) exists partly to give this real, significant event a household-level presence rather than leaving it purely as background noise.

### 15.3 Hadrian's Withdrawal (AD 117, this region's own historical default — see §16 for the exception)

Hadrian's real, deliberate decision to abandon both Mesopotamia and Assyria, consolidating the empire back to a defensible line rather than holding newly conquered but genuinely difficult territory. This document treats it as the honest, historically faithful default outcome for this region — the ending most playthroughs should expect — while §16 opens a genuine, difficult, explicitly ahistorical path for a household's own extraordinary success to change the specific policy calculus behind it, without altering Hadrian's own real accession or Trajan's own real death.

## 16. Historical Divergence — Extending the Occupation Beyond AD 117

This document introduces a genuine structural concept no other region has needed, and per direction, gives it a real fork rather than a single fixed outcome. The default path — what happens absent extraordinary success — is that **the household does not get to choose whether Roman administration leaves.** This is unlike the framework's own existing Distant Holding mechanic (Starting Regions §7), which is always the household's own voluntary choice to expand outward; by default here, the household's own home region itself is withdrawn from around it.

**The exception, per direction, is where this document deliberately departs from real history.** A household whose own governance during the occupation is extraordinary — genuinely exceptional Settlement-tier development at Estate & Settlement's own highest bands, a swiftly and thoroughly contained response to the Kitos War (§15.2) rather than a struggling one, and strong, durable Standing with the region's own Babylonian, Jewish, and Greek-Seleucid populations rather than resentful or merely tolerant relations — can plausibly present the newly-acceded Hadrian with a genuinely different real-world calculus than the one that led the actual historical Hadrian to withdraw. This document frames that difference honestly: Hadrian's own real accession still happens on schedule, and the household never interacts with him directly any more than any other Named Historical Figure allows (Events: Historical Timeline Content §6.5) — what a household can change is the specific administrative report reaching Rome about this one territory's own viability, not the man himself or the fact of his becoming Emperor.

**Three outcomes, not two:**

- **The historical default — withdrawal (§15.3).** Most playthroughs. The household evacuates to Syria, stays and adapts to renewed Parthian rule, or takes some other path (§19's own "some other path" question, unchanged).
- **Sustained Occupation — the Divergence outcome.** Genuinely rare, requiring the exceptional success described above. The region converts from a temporary province into an ordinary, ongoing one from AD 117 onward — the household's own home no longer disappears out from under it, and this document's own §6 finding that "Reputation Duality hasn't had time to form" stops applying, since a region that persists past its real historical end starts accumulating exactly that kind of standing from this point forward, most plausibly beginning at Full intensity (the same shape Gallic Frontier and Britannia use) given how genuinely fresh and contested an artificially-extended occupation would still be.
- **A worse-than-historical collapse**, if the Kitos War or local resentment overwhelms the household's own position before AD 117 even arrives — this document doesn't treat the historical withdrawal as the *floor* outcome, only the default one; a sufficiently mismanaged occupation could plausibly end worse and sooner than real history actually recorded.

This is, by direction, the one place in this entire project where a household's own success can genuinely outrun the real historical record rather than merely coloring how a fixed outcome is experienced — and this document names that fact directly rather than letting it blend quietly into the rest of its otherwise strict historical grounding.

## 17. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** the fourth region built entirely outside the framework's original candidate list, following Sicily, the Alpine Provinces, and Armenia — and the first whose entire premise revolves around a historically-default ending that a household's own exceptional success can, per direction, genuinely defy.
- **Armenia:** shares that document's own dual-household-option structure (§5) and its Royal Seat Gazetteer Role (§10, reused directly for Ctesiphon rather than redefined).
- **Events:** §15's own compressed, three-hook, three-year sequence is a genuine structural first, as is §15.3's status as a historical-default rather than absolute Divergence point — this project's first Timeline Hook a household can genuinely outrun rather than merely experience differently.
- **Religions of the Known World, Cultures of the Known World:** this document leans on real, existing Judaean and Jewish-diaspora content for §15.2 rather than inventing new material, consistent with this project's own established restraint on that subject throughout.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** §16's own three outcomes each give that system a genuinely distinctive, permanent flavor of household memory — "present for Trajan's Mesopotamia," "the household that made Hadrian change his mind," or "the household that lost everything before history even called for it" — unlike anything any other region's own history can produce.
- **Companions & Court Positions:** Seleucia's own real, large Hellenic population (§9, §10) gives a Roman colonist household plausible access to genuinely competent local administrative and commercial talent despite the region's own overall lack of development.
- **Estate & Settlement:** §16's own Sustained Occupation requirements lean directly on that system's own highest Settlement tiers as one of the three real conditions for defying the historical default.

## 19. Open Questions

- **Whether this region belongs in the Region Profile schema at all, versus being formally reclassified as a Scenario Start with unusually deep supporting material.** §1 names this tension directly rather than resolving it — a genuine, open structural question for whichever future pass actually implements region selection.
- **The exact Sustained Occupation success thresholds (§16).** This document names the three qualitative conditions (exceptional Settlement tier, swift Kitos War containment, strong local Standing) but leaves their precise, balanced difficulty to a future pass — this is deliberately meant to be rare, and getting that rarity right matters more here than almost anywhere else on the roster, given how large a departure the outcome represents.
- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and Ctesiphon's own one-time captured-wealth windfall are left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether the premature-collapse outcome (§16) needs its own distinct Timeline Hook or Dynasty Chronicle treatment**, separate from the historical-default withdrawal — this document names it as a real possibility but doesn't yet flesh out its own distinct texture.
- **The "some other path" branch of the historical-default withdrawal (§16).** This document deliberately doesn't enumerate every possible household response, leaving room for Familia, Politics & Patronage, and Legal & Court to specify further.
- **Whether a household that stays under renewed Parthian rule (§16) can ever meaningfully reconnect with the Roman world again**, or whether that path effectively exits this project's own Roman-household-management premise entirely — a genuine, unresolved question this document raises without presuming to answer.
- **Whether other historically brief Roman territories might justify similar "temporary province with an escapable default" treatment** in the future, now that this document has established the category — this document treats Mesopotamia as the category's first and, for now, only member.
- **Whether this document's own explicit historical exception (§1, §16) needs a clearer player-facing framing** distinguishing it from every other region's own strict adherence to real, settled outcomes — this document is internally consistent about where the line sits, but hasn't specified how a player encountering this region for the first time should be made aware that it's the one place on the roster where success can outrun history itself.
