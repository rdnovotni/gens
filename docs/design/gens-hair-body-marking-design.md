# GENS — System Design: Hair, Facial Hair & Body Marking
*The third companion to Fashion & Dress and its Garment Roster — everything a Character carries on their own body rather than over it: hairstyle and color, facial hair, tattoos, scarification, piercings, body paint, branding, and the rare, real religious body modifications this game's own historical range actually includes. Where the Garment Roster's own Layer Stack (§16 of that document) gave clothing a rendering home, this document does the same for what sits directly on the skin beneath it — genuinely permanent or semi-permanent choices, distinct from both a Character's fixed Appearance genetics and Fashion & Dress's own swappable, Occasion-driven Wardrobe. This pass adds a substantial new cross-cultural hairstyle and grooming section — the real Suebian knot, limed Gallic hair, the Dacian pileati/capillati class marker, Egyptian priestly head-shaving, Han-era Chinese uncut hair, the Hellenistic clean-shave shift, Palmyrene funerary portraiture, and Scythian/Sarmatian gold hair ornaments — alongside a second, non-Roman illustrative example. This further pass fills out the Roman core into a genuinely complete seven-style women's and five-style men's chronological progression spanning the full 133 BC–AD 235 range, adds a cross-cultural coming-of-age hair customs comparison (the Hellenic Koureion, the Egyptian Sidelock of Youth), a Nubian royal skullcap, Indian ascetic Jata, and the Judaean Nazirite vow. This final pass adds complexion as a real, sun-exposure-and-occupation-driven marker distinct from fixed genetics, hair length and grooming upkeep as their own dimension, eyebrow style and fullness as two concrete fields, and commemorative/devotional tattoos as a meaningful, non-punitive use of permanent marking.*

## 1. Scope & Role — Three Layers of "What a Character Looks Like"

This project now has three genuinely distinct layers governing how a Character looks, and it's worth stating the boundary between all three plainly before adding a fourth:

1. **Fixed Appearance (Familia §2.4 / Core §7.11).** Natural hair color, eye color, build, complexion — genetics. Never changes, never chosen, the permanent canvas everything else sits on.
2. **The Wardrobe (Fashion & Dress, in full).** Clothing, jewelry, and cosmetics — swappable per Occasion, entirely reversible, no different at Meager tier than at Opulent beyond quality and access.
3. **Body Marking (this document).** Hairstyle, hair color treatment, facial hair, tattoos, scars, piercings, and the rare cases of genuinely permanent religious or punitive body modification. The dividing line from Layer 2: everything in this document is either **irreversible** (a tattoo, a scar, a brand, a circumcision) or **durable across many Occasions rather than swapped per-scene** (a hairstyle or beard is re-cut and re-styled occasionally, not re-selected every time a Character attends a different event the way an outfit is). This document's content sits *underneath* clothing in the Layer Stack (§16) but is chosen, earned, or inflicted with real and often lasting weight — closer in permanence to a Trait than to a Garment.

## 2. Hairstyles — A Real, Era-Dated Roman Catalog

Fashion & Dress §3 already established hairstyle as a real, chosen fashion layer and flagged it as a genuine Era Drift signal (that document's own §11); this section supplies the actual, real, datable catalog behind that flag for the Roman core specifically — expanded substantially this pass into a genuinely complete chronological progression spanning the full 133 BC–AD 235 range, not just three or four representative snapshots — §3 immediately following does the same for the rest of this project's own cultural roster.

### 2.1 Women's Hairstyles — The Full Chronological Progression

| Style | Real Era | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Republican Plain Style** | c. 133 BC – 30 BC | The real, comparatively unadorned norm of the middle-to-late Republic — a simple bound queue or modest bun, without the elaborate curling apparatus later eras developed; genuinely attested via surviving mid-Republican portraiture and terracotta figures, and the honest, correct default for this game's own opening decades, well before any of the more famous Imperial styles below existed |
| **The Nodus** | Augustan, c. 30 BC – AD 14 | A real, simple, elegant style — hair rolled back from the forehead into a small knot — genuinely attested via surviving portraiture of Livia and other early Imperial women |
| **Julio-Claudian Waves** | AD 14 – 68 | A real, distinct successor style — hair center-parted, waved softly to either side, and gathered into a low coil or bun at the nape, attested through portraiture of later Julio-Claudian women including Agrippina the Younger; genuinely different in silhouette from the tighter, plainer Nodus it replaced |
| **The Flavian Toupet (*Orbis*)** | AD 69 – 96 | A dramatically tall, elaborately curled arrangement piled over the forehead, often built over a wire or fabric frame — real, well-documented, and genuinely striking; ancient sources refer to the tall curled front section itself as the *orbis* ("circle/wheel") |
| **The Melon Coiffure** | Trajanic, AD 98 – 117 | A real, distinct, and separately attested style from what follows it — hair sectioned into narrow braided or waved rows drawn back and coiled flat against the skull in ridged bands resembling a melon's rind (the actual descriptive term modern art historians use for this real, recognizable style), rather than piled forward the way the Flavian toupet was |
| **The Antonine Coronet Braid** | Hadrianic–Antonine, c. AD 117 – 192 | Hair waved back from the face and gathered into a genuine woven braid wound around the crown like a coronet — real, and a further, later, and clearly distinct successor to the Melon Coiffure |
| **Severan Waves** | AD 193 – 235 | The real, softer, closing style of this game's own entire range — hair waved gently back from a center part, often covering more of the ear than earlier styles, and gathered into a simple low bun, attested through Severan-dynasty portraiture (Julia Domna's own surviving portraits are the best-known real example); a deliberate, genuine style softening after nearly a century of increasingly architectural curled arrangements |
| **Simple/Practical Braid or Bun** | Any era, any class | The unremarkable, functional default for a working woman, a rural Character, or simply anyone not dressing for a Formal Occasion (Fashion & Dress §4) — the real, honest majority-case style across every single era above, next to which all seven are genuinely exceptional, elite-only fashion statements |
| **The Seni Crines** | Bridal-specific, any era | The Garment Roster's own ritual bridal hairstyle (§9 of that document) — real-historically also used, per §2.2 below, for a newly initiated Vestal |

### 2.2 Men's Hairstyles — The Full Chronological Progression

Paired directly with §5's own beard catalog below — hair and facial hair moved together as a single, jointly-datable "look" at every point in this progression, never independently.

| Style | Real Era | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Republican Close Crop** | c. 133 BC – 30 BC | Short, unstyled, unremarkable — paired with the clean-shaven Republican default (§5) |
| **Julio-Claudian/Flavian Combed Fringe** | c. 30 BC – AD 96 | A real, recognizable pattern across surviving portraiture of this period — short hair combed forward into a simple, unparted fringe across the forehead, distinct from the plainer Republican crop that preceded it, still worn clean-shaven |
| **Trajanic Practical Cut** | AD 98 – 117 | Short and unfussy, marking the last clean-shaven-as-default stretch of this game's own range immediately before Hadrian's own real, historically attested beard shift (§5) |
| **Hadrianic–Antonine Curled Hair** | AD 117 – 192 | Fuller, genuinely curled hair worn together with the full philosopher's beard (§5) — a single, jointly recognizable era-look rather than two independent fashion choices |
| **Severan Tight Ringlets** | AD 193 – 235 | A real, denser, more tightly curled variant continuing the Hadrianic-Antonine beard-and-hair pairing into this game's own final decades — surviving Severan-era portrait sculpture shows a distinctly deeper, more uniformly drilled ringlet pattern than the looser Hadrianic-Antonine curl, closing out the men's progression the same way Severan Waves close out the women's |

A household that keeps a materfamilias or daughter (or a paterfamilias or son) styled in a fashion associated with a prior era, per Fashion & Dress §11, reads as a small, deliberate Traditionalist statement — this catalog, now covering the entire 133 BC–AD 235 range in both directions rather than a handful of snapshots, is what makes that statement mechanically identifiable at any point in a campaign rather than only during a few especially well-documented decades.

### 2.3 Coming-of-Age & Youth Hair Customs Across Cultures

New this pass — a real, worthwhile unifying thread this document didn't draw out explicitly before: several of this project's own cultures mark the transition out of childhood with a specific, real hair-cutting or hair-dedication custom, distinct from (and in the Roman case, running alongside) the Bulla and Toga Virilis already established.

| Culture | Custom | Real Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| **Roman** | A lock of hair, alongside the first beard (§5's Depositio Barbae), sometimes formally dedicated to a household or state god around the Toga Virilis milestone | The broader ancient Mediterranean practice of dedicating youthful hair at a coming-of-age threshold, of which the Depositio Barbae is this project's own already-established Roman-specific instance |
| **Hellenic** | The ***Koureion*** | A real, specific, and well-documented Athenian custom: at the autumn Apatouria festival, an adolescent boy's hair was formally cut and dedicated as part of his recognition into his father's phratry (kinship group) — a genuine, real Hellenic coming-of-age hair rite distinct from, but structurally parallel to, the Roman Depositio Barbae |
| **Egyptian** | **The Sidelock of Youth** | A real, iconic, and extensively attested Egyptian practice: elite and royal children wore their heads largely shaved except for a single long lock or braid worn at the side of the head — the real, standard Egyptian artistic convention for depicting a child, closely associated with the child-god Horus — formally cut off at the transition into adulthood. A genuine, direct hairstyle-based parallel to the Roman Bulla's own jewelry-based childhood marker, worth naming as the two cultures' independently-arrived-at answers to the same real human need to visibly mark "not yet grown" |

A Character's own coming-of-age Event (Familia's Adolescence-to-Adulthood transition, Events §2) can draw on whichever of these three real customs actually fits their culture tag, giving that milestone a genuinely different, historically specific physical beat depending on where in this project's own cultural roster the Character actually comes from, rather than a single Roman-only default applied everywhere.

## 3. Cultural Hairstyle & Grooming Traditions — Beyond the Roman Core

New this pass, and the direct answer to covering the full cultural roster rather than a Roman-only default: real, specific, individually-grounded hair and grooming traditions for as much of Cultures of the Known World's own list as the historical record actually supports well. Consistent with this document's own established discipline (§6's own restraint around under-evidenced practice), a culture without a strong, specific, real attestation is deliberately left with the general "practical, era/region-appropriate default" treatment already implied elsewhere rather than an invented one — see §19.

| Culture | Real Tradition | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| **Germanic** | **The Suebian Knot** (*nodus Suebicus*) | Genuinely one of the single best-documented "barbarian" hairstyles in the entire ancient world: the real Roman historian Tacitus, in his own *Germania*, describes the Suebi specifically tying their hair up in a distinctive knot at the side of the head, and notes directly that a more elaborate knot marked higher status within the tribe — a real, textually explicit hairstyle-as-status-marker parallel to Thracian tattooing (§7), from a wholly independent real source |
| **Gallic** | **Limed, spiked hair** | Real, vivid, and specifically attested by the ancient historian Diodorus Siculus: Gallic warriors reportedly washed their hair in a lime-based solution and combed it straight back, stiffening it into a mane-like effect explicitly compared, in the original source, to a horse's mane — a striking, real, and genuinely distinct visual from anything in the Roman core catalog (§2) |
| **British/Caledonian** | Long hair and prominent mustaches, alongside body art | Real ancient sources (including Caesar's own account, already cited for body art at §7) describe British warriors wearing their hair long, a real, natural pairing with the mustache-without-beard convention already noted at §5 for the broader Gallic/Germanic/British cultural group |
| **Dacian** | **Pileati and Capillati — a real, literal cap-and-hair social class marker** | A genuinely excellent, well-documented real parallel to this project's own laticlavus/angusticlavus status system: real Roman sources, and the actual surviving reliefs of Trajan's Column itself, distinguish two classes of Dacian men by headwear and hair alone — the *pileati* ("cap-wearers"), Dacia's own noble class, shown wearing a distinctive soft cap, versus the *capillati* ("long-haired ones"), the ordinary free population, shown bare-headed with long hair. A real, visually confirmed, class-legible hair-and-headwear system worth naming in full rather than compressed to a single line |
| **Hellenic** | The Hellenistic clean-shave shift | A real, specific, and genuinely useful counterpoint to the Roman Hadrianic beard trend (§5): Alexander the Great's own real, historically attested preference for a clean-shaven look (reportedly so opposing soldiers couldn't grab a beard in close combat) spread as elite fashion across the Hellenistic world that followed him, creating a real, attested tension against the older Classical-era convention of the bearded philosopher (Fashion & Dress §2's own Pallium-wearer) — the same beard-as-era-marker logic §5 already uses for Rome, independently attested for the Greek world on its own terms and its own timeline |
| **Palmyrene** | Elaborate hairstyles and jewelry, drawn directly from surviving funerary portrait busts | Worth a specific methodological note: unlike most of this roster, real Palmyrene hairstyle detail survives in unusually rich, direct form — a genuine, large body of surviving Palmyrene funerary reliefs and portrait busts, blending Greek, Parthian, and local Aramaic style, gives this one specific culture a real archaeological richness most of the rest of this roster simply doesn't have |
| **Egyptian** | **Priestly head-shaving, and the wig worn over it** | A real, specific, and historically distinct motivation worth contrasting directly against the Roman wig's own purely fashion-driven use (Garment Roster §13): the real historian Herodotus records that Egyptian priests shaved their entire bodies every few days as a matter of ritual cleanliness, wearing a wig over the shaved scalp rather than styling natural hair at all — the same physical object (a wig) serving a genuinely different real-world purpose (purity, not fashion) depending on which culture is wearing it |
| **Sarmatian / Scythian / Bosporan Kingdom** | **Elaborate gold hair ornaments** | A real, famous, and richly attested archaeological tradition: Scythian goldwork is among the best-known material-culture legacies of the ancient steppe world, including real, surviving gold hair and head ornaments of genuine craftsmanship — a natural, concrete parallel to the Northern Frontier's own Amber trade (Garment Roster §5) as a second, distinct non-Mediterranean luxury-goods and personal-adornment tradition, and a fitting visual marker for the Bosporan Kingdom's own real, attested hybrid Greco-Scythian elite identity |
| **Parthia** | Voluminous curled hair and full curled beard, per royal coin portraiture | A real, consistent visual convention across surviving Parthian royal coinage — elaborately curled hair and beard together as a single deliberate presentation of authority, genuinely distinct in texture from both the clean-shaven Roman default and the Hellenistic clean-shave trend (above), reinforcing Parthia's own established East-vs-West visual contrast (Garment Roster §10) |
| **Chinese** *(Beyond the Frontier)* | **Uncut hair, worn long and bound, per real Confucian principle** | A real, genuinely distinct motivation from every other entry in this table, worth stating plainly: Han-era Chinese custom, grounded in a real, attested Confucian ethical principle that the body — hair included — is a gift from one's parents and should not be voluntarily cut, meant adult men and women alike wore their hair uncut and long throughout life, typically bound up under a cap or pin rather than cropped short in the Mediterranean manner. Rare among this project's roster in reflecting a real, explicit philosophical-ethical position rather than a purely aesthetic or status-driven choice — a genuinely different *reason* to look the way this Character looks, exactly the texture a rare Beyond-the-Frontier encounter (Cultures §10.7) should carry |
| **Nubia** | A distinct royal skullcap, per surviving Kushite royal iconography | Real, substantial archaeological evidence survives from the Napatan and Meroitic royal centers (the temples of Jebel Barkal, the pyramid fields of Meroe) showing Kushite rulers depicted in a distinctive close-fitting skullcap, often paired with a uraeus or ram-horn diadem reflecting the real, attested Egyptian-influenced-but-genuinely-distinct royal visual tradition of that kingdom — a real, concrete addition to the Nubia entry already broken out individually in the Garment Roster (§10 of that document) |
| **India** *(Beyond the Frontier)* | ***Jata*** — matted, coiled, vow-bound ascetic hair | A real, long-attested, and still-recognizable Indian religious practice: certain ascetics and holy men deliberately left their hair uncut and unwashed, allowing it to mat and coil, as a visible mark of a genuine religious renunciate vow — structurally comparable to the Judaean Nazirite vow (§12) and the Cult of Cybele's own Gallus commitment (§12) as a real instance of this project's broader pattern of hair (or its removal) marking an extreme, voluntary religious commitment, independently arrived at across three genuinely unrelated real traditions |

## 4. Hair Color, Complexion & Baldness

A Character's *natural* hair color is Familia §2.4's own fixed attribute and doesn't move here. What does: real, attested Roman-world practices for changing that natural color, covering it, or coping with its natural loss — and, new this pass, the genuinely real way lifestyle and occupation visibly modify complexion on top of the fixed genetic base.

| Treatment | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Henna dye** | A real, genuinely attested reddish hair dye across the ancient Mediterranean world — a real, concrete, and cheaper alternative to a full wig for a Character wanting a different hair color at Modest-to-Respectable Outfit Tier rather than Fine-or-above |
| **Lightening treatments** | Real Roman sources attest to women using various preparations (some genuinely harsh, ash- or lye-based) attempting to lighten hair toward the blonde tones associated with Germanic/Gallic wigs (Garment Roster §13) — a real, natural cheaper-but-riskier alternative to importing an actual foreign wig, and a small, optional Health-trend cost in the same register as Cerussa (Fashion & Dress §3) if played straight |
| **Going/kept grey or white** | The honest, unmodified default with age — a real, legitimate choice for an older Character, and, per §2's Traditionalist-coding logic, sometimes a deliberate one even where a wig or dye is affordable |
| **Baldness** | A real, ordinary, age-linked physical development rather than a chosen treatment — and, per real, well-attested Roman social history, a genuine source of personal self-consciousness even at the very highest levels of society (Julius Caesar's own real, historically documented sensitivity about his hair loss, and his real, attested habit of combing hair forward and favoring the laurel wreath partly for the coverage it offered, is the single best-known real illustration). This document treats baldness as a natural late-Adulthood/Elder development, with **concealment** — a favored hairstyle combed to cover it, a habitually-worn wreath or cap at Occasions where one would otherwise be unusual, or, at the wealthiest tiers, a discreet hairpiece distinct from a full Wig — as a small, real, and entirely optional Vain-trait-adjacent (Traits §5.1's Refined/Coarse register) personal choice rather than anything mechanically penalized on its own |

### 4.1 Complexion, Sun Exposure & Occupation

New this pass, and a genuinely well-grounded real addition worth drawing out explicitly: pale skin and tanned or weathered skin carried real, opposite class connotations across much of the ancient Mediterranean world, entirely independent of a Character's fixed genetic complexion (Familia §2.4). Real ancient Egyptian artistic convention is the clearest surviving illustration — elite women were conventionally painted with notably lighter skin than men, a direct visual code for a life spent indoors and free from labor, while a darker, sun-weathered tone marked an outdoor, working life; the same basic association (pale skin as a mark of leisure and protection from the elements, sun-darkened skin as a mark of labor) held broadly across the wider Roman world as well. This document treats sun exposure as a real, slow, and entirely natural modifier layered on top of a Character's own fixed base complexion — a field-worker, a soldier, a sailor, or an outdoor laborer of any Legal Status visibly weathers and tans over months and years regardless of their underlying genetic complexion, while a Character whose life is genuinely spent indoors (an elite matron, a scholar, a Character with a sedentary Court Position) keeps closer to their original, unweathered tone. This is a real, free piece of visible, legible texture distinguishing an estate's own labor-and-management population at a glance, without requiring a single new stat: it's simply which Duty Slot or Court Position (Companions & Court Positions, Labor & Slavery) a Character actually occupies, expressed visually.

### 4.2 Hair Length & Grooming Upkeep

New this pass: **hair length** is a real, distinct dimension from hairstyle (§2's own catalog governs shape and arrangement; length is simply how much hair there is to arrange in the first place) — Shaved, Cropped, Short, Shoulder-Length, and Long are the honest, plain range any Character's hair can actually be at, chosen independently of which era-appropriate style it's currently worked into. Consistent with the Garment Roster's own Wear Condition concept (§16.2 of that document, applied there to clothing), a chosen length isn't a permanent state — hair keeps growing, and a Character's own **Grooming Upkeep** (regular attention from a Tonsor or Ornatrix, Garment Roster §13, or simply the player's own standing attention) is what actually maintains a chosen length and style against natural growth. A well-kept household member at Respectable Outfit Tier or above reads as deliberately, consistently groomed; a neglected one — a Character under a harsh Regimen (Labor & Slavery §5), or simply one nobody's paying attention to — drifts toward untended, overgrown hair over time regardless of what length was originally chosen, the same honest, low-cost visual signal §4.1's complexion modifier already provides for occupation.

## 5. Facial Hair

The single clearest visual expression of Fashion & Dress §11's own Era Drift concept for the Roman core, and worth its own real, named catalog rather than a single generic "beard or not" toggle. §3 above supplies the equivalent cultural contrast points for the Hellenic world (Alexander's own clean-shave shift) and Parthia (the curled royal beard).

| Style | Era/Cultural Association | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean-shaven** | Republic through early Principate (the default up to roughly Trajan's reign) | The ordinary, expected look for a Roman citizen man for most of this game's own earlier range |
| **Full philosopher's beard** | Hadrian onward | Real, well-documented, and famously associated with the emperor Hadrian's own personal preference — the single most legible "which part of the 133 BC–AD 235 range is this" visual signal in the entire roster |
| **Mustache without beard** | Gallic/Germanic/British cultural marker, not a Roman fashion at any era | A real, specific, and genuinely pointed cultural contrast: Roman writers repeatedly commented on northern "barbarian" mustache-without-beard styles as a marker distinguishing them from clean-shaven Roman norms — a Gallic-, Germanic-, or British-culture Character wearing this style even inside a thoroughly Roman household is a small, real, and legible Assimilated/Unbowed (Fashion & Dress §10) signal |
| **Full beard, any other era** | A real, available Traditionalist-or-simply-unfashionable choice at any point | Doesn't require special gating — a Character (or a household) can simply be out of step with the current era's own facial-hair fashion, exactly as §2's hairstyle logic already allows |

**The Depositio Barbae — a real, small coming-of-age beat.** A young man's first beard trim was, in real Roman practice, itself a small, genuine ceremonial moment — the clippings sometimes formally dedicated to a household or state god. (The emperor Nero's own real, attested gold-box dedication of his first beard clippings to Jupiter is the single best-documented example, though the underlying custom was ordinary rather than uniquely Imperial.) This document ties the Depositio Barbae directly to the Toga Virilis milestone (Garment Roster §9) as an optional, small additional flourish.

**The tool behind the practice:** the *novaculum* (razor) and, functionally, a *tonsor* (barber, Garment Roster §13) perform this — no new Companion role is needed beyond the one already flagged there.

## 6. Eyebrows, Teeth & Other Small Vanities

Two small, real, and genuinely fun pieces of ancient beauty-standard texture worth naming rather than leaving this document silent on, plus a small, real structural addition to how eyebrows specifically are described.

- **The Hellenic unibrow.** A real, specific, and well-attested ancient Greek beauty standard, genuinely distinct from Roman convention: connected eyebrows were considered attractive, to the point that real sources describe women darkening the brow-bridge with soot or kohl, or in some cases applying a false connecting piece made of goat hair, to achieve the look artificially where nature hadn't provided it. A small, real, and immediately legible Hellenic-culture (§10 of the Garment Roster) cosmetic distinction from the Roman core wardrobe's own separate-eyebrow default.
- **Dental whitening, and its regional mockery.** A real, genuinely funny piece of surviving Roman literature: the poet Catullus's own Poem 39 mocks a man named Egnatius specifically for his habit of brushing his teeth with a Celtiberian (Iberian) urine-based whitening preparation, and for smiling constantly to show off the result. This document treats teeth-whitening as a small, real, optional Grooming choice — and Catullus's own joke as a ready-made, historically airtight template for exactly the kind of light social mockery Scandal's own shared aftermath engine (Fashion & Dress §13) can draw on: a genuine cosmetic vanity, tied to a specific real regional stereotype, mocked by a rival rather than seriously punished.

### 6.1 Eyebrow Style & Fullness

New this pass: eyebrows resolve as two small, independent descriptive dimensions rather than a single line — a natural **fullness** (thin, natural, full) inherited the same way any other Fixed Appearance detail is, and a chosen **style** (left entirely natural and unshaped, or deliberately groomed/shaped at Fine Outfit Tier and above, consistent with the general principle that active grooming attention is itself a small, visible marker of wealth and leisure time). This gives the Full Description (Paper Doll §4) a real, concrete pair of fields to draw on beyond the single Hellenic-specific note above, applicable to any Character regardless of culture.

## 7. Tattoos & Scarification

Real, genuinely permanent body marking, distinct from anything in the Garment Roster and handled with the same restraint this project already applies to Punishment and to sexuality content — described factually, without dwelling.

| Practice | Real Grounding | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Thracian elite tattooing** | A real, specific, and well-attested Classical claim: Herodotus records that among the Thracians, tattooing was a genuine mark of noble birth — commoners went unmarked, while elite status was visibly, permanently inked | A real, concrete, and unusually well-documented cultural body-marking tradition to hang directly on the Thracian culture entry (Cultures of the Known World §5) — the single strongest "tattooing signals status, not deviance" example available to this project |
| **British/Caledonian body art** | Real ancient sources (including Caesar's own account) describe native Britons using body coloring/marking in battle and ritual contexts | Whether a given instance renders as temporary paint or a more permanent marking is left to §11/this section's own overlap rather than forced into one or the other |
| **North/Sub-Saharan African scarification traditions** | A real, broad, and genuinely attested body-art tradition across numerous historical African cultures, relevant here specifically for a Nubian-culture Character (Garment Roster §10's own broken-out Nubia entry) | Kept deliberately general rather than over-specified to a single invented tribal pattern |
| **Stigmata — punitive tattooing** | A real, specific, and genuinely dark Roman legal-and-social practice worth naming factually: certain criminals and, less commonly, some enslaved individuals judged high flight-risk were tattooed, typically on the forehead, as a permanent, visible mark of their status or offense. The Greek term for this mark, *stigma*, is the direct, literal etymological ancestor of the modern English word "stigma" | This document names it as a real, available, and severe escalation distinct from (and generally more severe than) the slave collar (Garment Roster §8) |
| **Later Roman military tattooing** | A real, attested later-Imperial-era practice (recorded by writers including Vegetius): army recruits were sometimes tattooed for identification, partly as a real, practical desertion deterrent | A genuine, concrete Era Drift-appropriate detail |

### 7.1 Commemorative & Devotional Tattoos

New this pass, and a genuinely meaningful, entirely non-punitive use of the same permanent marking already established above — a direct expression of Design Pillar #7 sitting right alongside the Imagines (Garment Roster §9) and a household's own Dynasty Chronicle. Where a Character's own culture already treats tattooing as an ordinary, legitimate practice (§7's own Thracian and British/Caledonian entries, or simply a personal choice available more broadly), a small, specific tattoo can mark:

- **A real personal milestone** — the birth of a child, a great personal or Ambition-tier achievement (Character Ambitions, §8 of that document), or the loss of someone close, in the same register as a Toga Virilis or a Dona Militaria commemorates a different kind of milestone through dress instead of skin.
- **A devotional mark** — a small, specific symbol tied to a Character's own personal cult affiliation (Religion §7's light-touch personal devotion tier) — an Isis symbol, a small Mithraic bull, or an equivalent mark of the god or cult a Character has personally taken up. A real, visible, and permanent expression of faith, distinct from and smaller in stakes than the Cult of Cybele's own extreme Gallus commitment (§12).

Both are simple, optional, player- or narrative-triggered additions to a Character's existing BodyMarking record (§18) — no new mechanic, only a new, meaningful reason a Character might have one.

## 8. Branding & Punitive Marking

A distinct, if closely related, practice from tattooing (§7) worth its own brief, separate note: real Roman practice also included branding — a burned rather than inked mark — for some categories of condemned criminal and, in specific real cases, an enslaved individual. Real ancient sources attest to specific marks denoting a specific offense, though exact, universal letter conventions are less firmly documented than the general practice itself; this document treats the specific letter or symbol used as flavor-level detail an Event author can select freely rather than a fixed, universal code. This document treats Branding and the Stigmata tattoo (§7) as **the same mechanical tier and the same severity register** — two real, historically distinct techniques producing functionally the same game outcome — rather than doubling up on punishment content.

## 9. Forced Head-Shaving — A Distinct Punishment Tier

A deliberate step between two things this project already has: Fashion & Dress §13's own **Mild**-tier degrading-dress Punishment, and §7–8's own **permanent** Stigmata/Branding escalation. Forced shaving of the head is a real, cross-culturally attested form of punitive humiliation, and a genuinely plausible fit for Roman practice specifically given how much real social and aesthetic weight this document's own §2 and §3 already establish hair carried across nearly every culture in this roster — stripping it visibly and suddenly is a real, legible act of degradation distinct from simply downgrading someone's clothing tier. Mechanically, this document places it as a **Severe**-tier Labor & Slavery Punishment option, deliberately positioned between Mild and the fully permanent Stigmata/Brand: **it grows back**, and therefore carries real, lasting Scandal/humiliation weight for a genuine but bounded period rather than becoming a lifelong mark.

## 10. Piercings

Kept deliberately light for the Roman core, but genuinely broadened for the Near East specifically, where the real historical record actually supports more than an ears-only default.

| Practice | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Women's ear piercing** | Real, near-universal across the Roman world and most of the cultures this project covers — the default assumption for earrings (Garment Roster §5) to actually be worn |
| **Men's ear piercing** | Genuinely **not** a normative Roman practice — real Roman sources treat it as foreign/exotic when it appears at all. A pierced-ear male Character reads as a deliberate, legible non-Roman cultural signal (Garment Roster §10, Assimilated/Unbowed in the other direction) |
| **Nose rings — Judaean, Nabataean, Arabian cultures** | A real, specific, and textually attested Near Eastern jewelry tradition genuinely distinct from the Mediterranean ears-only default — nose rings appear as real worn ornaments in the region's own historical and textual record, including within the Hebrew Bible's own references to them as a real gift/adornment |
| **Other piercing** | Not a meaningful category in this project's own historical scope beyond the above — deliberately left uncatalogued rather than inventing anachronistic practice |

## 11. Body Paint

The temporary counterpart to §7's permanent tattoos: real, attested British/Caledonian and (to a lesser extent) Germanic use of blue woad-based body coloring, already flagged as a textile-dye alternative use in the Garment Roster (§10, §14 of that document). Unlike a tattoo, this document treats body paint as **Occasion-scoped** rather than a standing body-marking choice — applied for a specific battle, ritual, or festival context and gone afterward. A Character can hold both: a permanent Thracian-style tattoo (§7) *and* temporary ritual paint for a specific occasion, without the two treatments conflicting.

## 12. Ritual & Religious Body Modification

Two real, specific, and historically documented practices, both handled with real restraint and at real, appropriate rarity.

**Circumcision** is a real, central, and long-standing element of Judaean religious practice (Cultures of the Known World §6's own "categorically distinct... monotheistic tradition" note) — noted here factually as a real cultural-religious marker for that culture specifically, with the one real, genuinely significant historical tie this project should actually surface: the Roman state's own real, historical restrictions on circumcision under Hadrian are widely understood by historians as a real contributing tension behind the Bar Kokhba revolt (AD 132–136, already a dated entry in this project's own Historical Timeline) — a legitimate, real Legal & Court/Religion policy-friction Event hook for a Judaean-culture population under an Assimilationist or otherwise heavy-handed Provincial Administration Posture (Politics & Patronage §2.1).

**The Nazirite Vow** is a second, distinct, and genuinely well-documented Judaean religious practice worth naming alongside circumcision rather than folding into it: a real, textually attested (Hebrew Bible, Numbers 6) voluntary vow of consecration under which a person let their hair grow entirely uncut for the vow's duration, then formally shaved their head and offered the hair at the conclusion of the vow — a real, temporary, and specifically bounded religious hair commitment, genuinely distinct from both the permanent Chinese uncut-hair principle and the permanent Indian Jata (§3) despite superficial similarity, since a Nazirite vow always had a defined real end point built into the practice itself. A Character undertaking one is a small, legitimate, temporary Religious Devotion flavor state — long, visibly uncut hair for its duration, followed by a real, concrete, and genuinely dramatic single-day head-shaving event marking its formal completion.

**The Galli of Cybele** are a real, specific, and well-documented element of the Cult of Cybele (Magna Mater), already named in Religion §7 and Religions of the Known World §6: self-castrated devotee-priests, a genuine and extreme feature of that cult's real historical practice (attested directly in ancient sources, including Catullus's own poem on the subject). This document names the practice factually, once, without further elaboration — mechanically, becoming a Gallus is the single most extreme possible expression of that cult's own Initiatory tier (Religions of the Known World §6), gated at the highest possible Zealotry/Devotee threshold and never something the game defaults toward or trivializes.

## 13. Scars — The Body's Own Ledger

Not a new mechanic — Traits already established **Scarred** as a standalone trait marking "permanent injury's visible mark" (Traits §6.2), and **Battle-Hardened** as Military & Combat's own real aftermath trait. This document's addition is the direct, explicit rendering tie (§16): a Scarred or Battle-Hardened Character's portrait carries a real, visible physical scar as part of their own fixed Body layer going forward, exactly the same permanence this document's tattoos and brands already have, earned through combat or a Games & Spectacle arena appearance rather than chosen. Taken together with §7's tattoos, §8's brands, and §9's shaved heads, a Character's body genuinely becomes a second, physical record of their own history sitting right alongside their Dynasty Chronicle entries — Design Pillar #7 rendered directly onto the portrait itself, not merely written down elsewhere.

## 14. Concealment & Disguise — When a Mark Works Against You

A direct resolution of an open question the prior pass left standing: **can a Stigmata mark or Brand ever be hidden?** Real Rome had no medical removal, but partial *concealment* is genuinely plausible and worth modeling directly.

A permanently marked Character can attempt concealment through entirely ordinary means already established elsewhere in this document family: a strategically chosen hairstyle or headwear covering a forehead mark (§2, §3, Garment Roster §16.1's own Headwear layer), heavy cosmetic coverage (§4's own cerussa-adjacent use, applied here for coverage rather than complexion), or simply favoring long sleeves and high collars over the marked limb. This is never a full removal — a close inspection, a bathing Occasion (Garment Roster §11), or a determined search still reveals it — but it is a real, meaningful reduction in everyday visibility, and the direct, concrete mechanical link Fashion & Dress §12's own Disguise & Deception mechanic needs: a marked individual attempting to pass as free, or a marked deserter attempting to re-enlist under a different identity, faces a real, additional, and specifically named complication layered on top of that system's own existing odds.

A visiting, more Traditionalist Roman guest reads it as a mark of foreignness worth a raised eyebrow; the household's own children, who've never known him any other way, don't register it as unusual at all — the same object, two entirely different readings, depending on who's actually looking.

## 16. Rendering Integration — Extending the Layer Stack

This document's entire content slots into the Garment Roster's own Layer Stack (§16 of that document) at exactly two points, both sitting *beneath* clothing rather than replacing any existing Garment layer:

- **Layer 0 (Body) gains a permanent Skin Marking sub-layer** — tattoos (§7), brands (§8), the Stigmata mark, and Scars (§13) all live here, rendered directly onto the Body layer itself rather than as a removable item, and visible wherever clothing (any layer above) doesn't cover that part of the body.
- **Layer 1 (Hair Base) gains a Facial Hair companion layer**, following the same Era Drift and cultural-default substitution logic the Garment Roster's own §16.3 already establishes for clothing. §3's own cultural catalog is this layer's real content library: a Germanic-culture Character defaults to the Suebian Knot, a Gallic-culture one to limed spiked hair, a Dacian noble-tier Character to the pileus-cap-and-hair marker, a Chinese-culture Character to long, uncut, bound hair — each a genuine, real, distinct default asset rather than a shared generic "foreign hairstyle" placeholder. A Bald Character (§4) simply renders Layer 1 as absent or reduced, with a concealment choice resolving as a Headwear-layer or Hair-layer override respectively.
- **Body paint (§11)** renders as a genuine, distinct, *temporary* overlay — occupying the same visual position as a Skin Marking but flagged, unlike a tattoo, to only appear for the specific Occasion it was applied for, then revert.
- **Piercings (§10)** render as a small, minor addition to the Neck/Chest and Hand/Finger Accessory layers already established, present only insofar as actual Jewelry (Garment Roster §5) is currently worn in that spot.
- **Forced Head-Shaving (§9)** is modeled as a temporary override of Layer 1 itself — the Hair Base layer renders as absent for the Punishment's own bounded duration, then reverts automatically.

## 19. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — no Health-trend magnitude, Opinion penalty, Dignitas effect, Disguise-odds penalty, or Scandal severity is specified for any entry in this document.
- **Portrait rendering asset scope.** Consistent with the Garment Roster's own §16 open question, this document doesn't specify how many actual hairstyle/facial-hair/marking visual variants must be produced per era per culture.
- **Cultures still without a specific, real hairstyle/grooming attestation.** §3 now covers thirteen cultures with strong, real, findable historical grounding (up from nine); Hibernian, Numidian/Mauri, Cappadocian, Illyrian/Pannonian, Cretan, Cilician, Blemmyes, and the still more thinly-attested Beyond-the-Frontier entries (Garamantian, Aksumite, Taprobane, Sogdian) remain deliberately on the general "practical, era/region-appropriate default" treatment rather than given an invented specific — a future pass with deeper research access could responsibly add to this list, but this document declines to fabricate detail it can't actually stand behind.
- **Whether the Nazirite vow and the Indian Jata should be modeled as the same VowBoundHairRecord type despite one being completable and the other, in ordinary practice, permanent.** §12's data model currently unifies them for structural-parallel convenience; a future pass may find they warrant fully separate record types instead.
- **Complexion modifier's actual visual granularity.** §4.1 establishes sun exposure as a real, slow modifier without specifying how many discrete visible steps (or a continuous slider) the rendering pipeline should support between "indoor pale" and "heavily weathered."
- **Grooming Upkeep's exact neglect threshold.** §4.2 ties untended hair to Regimen tier and Outfit Tier generally without specifying the precise condition that flips a Character from "maintained" to "neglected."
- **Concealment's exact residual-visibility math**, and **head-shaving's exact regrowth duration** — both deferred with the rest of this document's numeric sizing.
- **The Gallus commitment's full downstream effect** — left to Religion's own eventual deeper pass on that specific cult.
- **Whether the Dacian pileati/capillati distinction should extend into a broader mechanical Social Class marker** (parallel to the laticlavus/angusticlavus) rather than remaining flavor-level hairstyle content — a natural follow-up question for Dacia's own eventual dedicated Starting Region-style treatment, if one is ever added to this project's own regional roster.
