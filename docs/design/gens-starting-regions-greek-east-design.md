# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Greek East
*Final polish and balance pass, completing the launch roster. Fills in the Starting Regions framework's Region Profile schema (§4) for a single unified region whose defining trait is one no other region shares: this is the only launch region that borders a genuine Great Power (Parthia, via the real Cappadocian frontier and the Armenian Contested Buffer) rather than a tribal Frontier people — giving Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples' own Great Power machinery (§8–9 of that document) its first real regional home. No Reputation Duality here, per the framework's own §6 table; this region's own defining tension runs through prestige, culture, and Rome's own repeated habit of settling its biggest fights on Greek soil rather than through conquest or extraction. This pass fixes a self-contradicting sentence in §5, adds a worked example (§5.1) for the Great Power diplomacy mechanic, and gives "impoverished patrician clawing back status" its first-ever secondary reading anywhere on the roster (§12) — a genuine coverage gap the first draft's own Templated Background grouping had missed. Intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Where Rome Comes to Fight Its Own Wars

Every other region on this roster has its own conquest story — a war Rome fought to take it, a revolt it fought to keep it, or, in Italy's case, no story at all because it was never anyone else's to take. Greek East's own defining historical pattern is different and genuinely distinct: this is the region where **outside powers' own conflicts get settled**, not the region's own struggle against Rome. Mithridates VI of Pontus fought Rome for control of this territory across three real wars (89–63 BC); and, more strikingly still, Rome's own civil wars — the conflicts that actually decided who ruled Rome itself — were fought to their real, decisive conclusions on Greek soil twice: Philippi (42 BC) and Actium (31 BC), both real, both here, both settling questions that had nothing to do with Greek East's own governance and everything to do with who would govern Rome (§14).

Consistent with the precedent set by every other multi-province region on this roster, Greek East stays a single selectable region rather than splitting, but carries a real internal contrast of its own: a thoroughly demilitarized, senatorial **Achaean and Aegean heartland** — mainland Greece, the islands, western Anatolia — against a genuinely militarized **Cappadocian frontier** at the region's own eastern edge, where Rome's real legions actually face the one neighbor on this entire roster that isn't a tribe: Parthia itself.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

A real, genuine mix of fertile coastal plains (limited compared to Italy or North Africa's own agricultural richness — much of mainland Greece's own terrain is real, famously rocky and mountainous, a genuine historical reason this region built its wealth on trade and craft rather than raw agricultural surplus) and extensive, real coastal access across an unusually large number of islands and peninsulas — genuinely the most maritime-oriented terrain profile on the roster, ahead even of Gallic Frontier's or Iberian Colony's own dual-coast arrangements. Real marble deposits in the Attic and Aegean hill country (Pentelic marble, quarried near Athens itself, and Parian marble from the island of Paros) give this region its own distinct, historically famous building-stone identity — the actual real source material behind the Parthenon and some of the finest surviving ancient sculpture, distinct from Numidian and Iberian marble's own more workaday building applications.

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Consistent with the core doc's own "culture/education bonuses, strong trade networks" — this region's wealth runs through trade, craft, and cultural prestige rather than raw land value or resource extraction, a genuinely different economic shape from every other region on the roster. Land cost sits comparably to Iberian Colony's own moderate tier, but the real driver of a Greek East household's prosperity is market access and reputation rather than acreage — a household here can be genuinely wealthy on a comparatively modest landholding if its trade connections and cultural standing are strong enough, a real, distinct path Design Pillar #1's "no dominant setting" promise is well served by.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture — Free Cities, a Rebuilt Capital

This region's own "distinct legal/social wrinkles," per the core doc, center on a real, genuine Roman legal category: **civitas libera**, "free city" status — a real, formal exemption from ordinary provincial taxation and administration granted to a handful of especially favored or especially prestigious Greek cities. Athens held this status for essentially this entire game's range; Rhodes held it too, though — worth noting honestly rather than glossing over — Rhodes's own real relationship with Rome fluctuated, including real periods where Roman favor cooled and certain privileges were curtailed, a genuine, live political fact rather than a fixed guarantee.

**A real, striking parallel to North African Colony's own Carthage/Utica story:** Corinth, this region's own real administrative capital, was destroyed by Rome in the exact same year as Carthage — 146 BC — and later rebuilt as a Roman colony, becoming the actual seat of provincial government for Achaea. Athens, meanwhile, was never destroyed and never became the administrative capital, yet remains the region's own unmatched cultural and prestige center. This is a genuine, historically real illustration of a pattern now recurring across this project's own region documents: administrative power and cultural prestige aren't the same thing, and don't have to sit in the same city (Naples/Puteoli in Campania carries the same lesson at smaller scale).

Achaea and Macedonia were both real senatorial provinces for substantial stretches of this game's range — governed by proconsuls, genuinely peaceful, carrying essentially no standing legionary presence, the same demilitarized administrative model Iberian Colony's own Baetica uses. Cappadocia, at this region's own eastern edge, is the opposite: a real imperial province specifically *because* of its frontier-facing role against Parthia (§5).

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — A Great Power Border, Not a Tribal One

This is the single most structurally distinct feature on Greek East's own profile, and worth stating plainly: of the four other regions on this roster, only Gallic Frontier carries an external Diplomacy relationship at all, and even that one is with a tribal Frontier people — Germanic tribes across the Rhine. Iberian and North African Colony have no external relationship whatsoever, per their own documents. Greek East is different from all of them. Its own eastern edge, via Cappadocia, borders **Armenia's own Contested Buffer** and, beyond it, **Parthia itself** — the only genuine Great Power on the entire launch roster's own doorstep, per Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples §8–9.

This gives Greek East access to a real, distinct diplomatic toolkit no other region uses: the Legate's own formal negotiation channel, real mutual Hostage Exchange (distinct in kind from a Frontier people's one-directional version), Peace Treaties and Trade Agreements reaching toward the real Silk Road, and a genuine stake in Armenia's own contested allegiance, resolved by covert Scheme or overt campaign per that document's §9. Rather than a raiding relationship, a Greek East household's own outward-facing drama is the far higher-stakes, far more formal drama of Great Power diplomacy — a real, historically attested Roman anxiety (Crassus's own catastrophic 53 BC defeat at Carrhae, per Cultures of the Known World and the Events timeline, looms over this relationship the way Teutoburg looms over Gallic Frontier's own).

The real Cappadocian frontier fortress of Satala (§9) anchors this relationship directly, and carries this region's own real standing legionary presence — a genuine military contrast with the thoroughly demilitarized Achaean heartland (§4), the same core/periphery pattern this project's other multi-province regions already establish.

### 5.1 A Worked Example — Two Households, Two Kinds of Risk

Consider two Greek East households, both with real standing at Satala. The first pursues careful, Legate-mediated diplomacy: Peace Treaties, a Trade Agreement reaching toward the Silk Road, and, when Armenia's own allegiance comes into question, a covert Scheme rather than open force (Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples §9) — a slow, formal, genuinely lower-risk path, closer in spirit to how a household might handle any ordinary provincial administration. The second gambles directly on Armenia's own contested allegiance through an overt Military & Combat campaign, risking exactly the kind of escalation that produced the real historical disaster at Carrhae (§5) if Parthia itself responds in force. Neither household is raiding or being raided the way a Gallic Frontier household would be — the stakes here are diplomatic and military at a categorically larger scale, Rome's own relationship with a peer power rather than a tribe, and the consequences of getting it wrong are correspondingly graver than anything Reputation Duality's own local-standing axis ever produces elsewhere on the roster.

## 6. Religious & Cultural Defaults — The Densest Sacred Geography in the Game

No other region on this roster comes close to Greek East's own concentration of genuinely famous, real religious sites. **Delphi** — home of the Oracle, arguably the single most famous prophecy site in the entire ancient Mediterranean world, outranking even Cumae's real Sibyl (Starting Regions: Italian Heartland §4.5) in sheer historical renown. **Eleusis**, real home of the Eleusinian Mysteries, one of antiquity's most famous mystery cults, a direct and concrete tie into Religions of the Known World's own Mystery Cults content. **Olympia**, sacred to Zeus and home to the real ancient Olympic Games (§14 notes these ran continuously throughout this entire game's timeline). This region isn't simply "the Hellenic culture's home region" the way the framework's own culture-distribution logic might suggest by default — it's the actual, concrete geography behind Religion's own richest available content, full stop.

Cultural identity here is overwhelmingly Hellenic, per Cultures of the Known World, with two real, distinct minority threads at the region's own margins: Galatian culture (a genuinely fascinating real case of Celtic tribes who migrated into central Anatolia generations ago and are now themselves substantially Hellenized — "living Cultural Drift as lived history," per that document) and Cappadocian/Anatolian native culture at the frontier edge (§5). Sparta itself carries a small, distinct, genuinely charming real flavor worth naming directly: by this game's own era, Sparta had become something close to a living museum of its own former self, and real, documented Roman visitors traveled there specifically to observe its preserved, by-then largely ceremonial ancestral customs — a genuine "tourism of one's own conquered past," unique to this single city anywhere on the roster.

## 7. Regional Goods & Trade

Wine and olive oil (shared with the Italian Heartland's own tags, Resources & Goods §7), fine pottery and amphorae, Saffron, and the real, specifically named Hymettus honey — a genuine, prized ancient delicacy already tagged to this region in Resources & Goods' own registry — are this region's established signature goods. Tyrian Purple (Murex) is shared with Iberian Colony's own coastal tradition (Starting Regions: Iberian Colony §8) — both real, both coexisting sources, with no tier distinction needed the way Garum's own resolution required, since neither document claims an exclusive luxury tier here. Pentelic and Parian marble (§2) give this region its own distinct sculptural-and-monumental building-stone identity, genuinely a cut above ordinary construction stone even by this project's own already-generous marble tagging elsewhere.

**A note on Silphium, resolving a boundary this document doesn't re-litigate:** Resources & Goods tags Silphium to the "Greek East/Iberian-NA border," and North African Colony's own document (§8, §18) has already claimed the concrete Gazetteer anchor — Cyrene — on the strength of that connection. This document doesn't re-claim it; Cyrenaica sits genuinely at the boundary between the two regions, and North African Colony spoke for it first.

## 10. Rival Seeding

Four houses, deliberately including the roster's first household defined by cultural prestige rather than political, military, or trade ambition.

- **The household of Nikandros** *(seated at Athens)* — a wealthy, thoroughly citizen-integrated Athenian family that has deliberately kept a Greek naming convention rather than adopting a Roman one, entirely uninterested in the cursus honorum and instead pursuing Cultural Prestige (Education & Culture §7) as its own primary measure of standing — the region's own clearest illustration that Athens's own real prestige and formal political power were never the same thing (§4).
- **The household of Philokrates** *(seated at Rhodes)* — a maritime trade and legal family whose own fortunes have real, historically plausible ups and downs tracking Rhodes's own fluctuating Roman favor (§4) — a rival whose standing is genuinely more volatile than any other house on this roster, tied to forces outside its own control, and per §12, this roster's own clearest secondary illustration of "clawing back status" rather than a straightforward merchant story.
- **Gens Munatia** *(seated at Corinth)* — a commercially aggressive Roman colonial family descended from the real historical pattern of Italian colonists Corinth's own refounding actually drew, contrasting the household of Nikandros's own disinterested cultural prestige with a practical, administrative, trade-focused ambition.
- **Gens Vinicia** *(seated at Satala)* — a garrison-connected family tied to the real legionary presence facing Parthia, structurally the same archetype as Gallic Frontier's Gens Fulvia, Iberian Colony's Gens Terentia, and North African Colony's Gens Petronia, here justified by proximity to a Great Power rather than a tribal frontier or an internal military split.

## 11. Home Anchor

**In the Attic countryside, within easy reach of both Athens and Corinth.** This anchors the household in this region's own default, demilitarized, culturally saturated heartland — direct access to the Academy's own real prestige and Corinth's own real administrative and trade machinery — consistent with every other region's own pattern of anchoring in the calm core (Baetica, Africa Proconsularis) rather than the militarized edge (Satala) as the default identity.

## 12. Templated Background Flavor

Every one of Core §5.2's four Templated Background archetypes already carries at least one prior claim elsewhere on the roster. Greek East offers its own further readings rather than competing for primacy: the household of Nikandros gives **"provincial notable"** a fourth flavor — a cultural-prestige claim to notability distinct from Gallic Frontier's straddling aristocrat, Iberian Colony's economic-power variant, and North African Colony's native royal-descent claim; Gens Munatia gives **"jumped-up equestrian merchant"** a fourth flavor in turn, a colonial-trade reading distinct from Campania's shipping wealth, Iberian Colony's mining-and-garum wealth, and North African Colony's grain-and-marble wealth; and the household of Philokrates gives **"impoverished patrician clawing back status"** its first secondary reading anywhere on the roster — Rhodes's own real, fluctuating Roman favor (§4, §10) makes a family whose standing has genuinely risen and fallen with forces outside its own control a more natural fit for that archetype than for a straightforward merchant story.

## 14. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 14.1 The Sack of Corinth (146 BC, closed history by default)

The real, direct destruction behind Corinth's own Gazetteer entry (§9, §4) — falling in the exact same year as Carthage's own destruction, a genuine, striking parallel between this region and North African Colony's own founding trauma. Closed history for any default-era playthrough, but the essential backstory behind Corinth's own later rebuilding.

### 14.2 The Mithridatic Wars (89–63 BC, era-conditional)

Three real, major conflicts against Mithridates VI of Pontus, genuinely reshaping the whole Greek East and threatening Roman control of the region directly — the closest thing this region has to an external-conquest-threat story, though it comes from a rival Hellenistic king rather than a native uprising. Era-conditional, closed history by default Pax Romana start.

### 14.3 Rome's Own Civil Wars, Settled Here — Philippi and Actium (42 BC and 31 BC)

This region's own single most distinctive historical identity: the two most decisive battles of Rome's own final civil wars — Philippi, deciding the fate of Caesar's assassins, and Actium, deciding the fate of the entire Mediterranean world between Octavian and Antony — were both fought on Greek East's own soil. Unlike Iberian Colony's Sertorian War or North African Colony's Battle of Thapsus, this isn't a case of Rome's own internal conflict spilling into a colony's own territory incidentally — Greek East is where Rome's biggest questions actually got answered, twice, within a single generation. Closed history by default Pax Romana start, but the clearest possible illustration of §1's own defining identity.

## 15. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** fulfills the Region Profile schema (§4) for Greek East, confirms that document's own "no Reputation Duality" call (§6), and gives Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples' own Great Power machinery (§8–9 of that document) its first concrete regional home anywhere on the launch roster.
- **Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples:** §5 is this document's own direct, substantial extension of that system's Parthia and Armenia content, previously designed but never anchored to an actual selectable region; §5.1's worked example gives that relationship its first concrete regional illustration anywhere on the roster.
- **Education & Culture:** three of that document's five named Institutions of Renown — Athens, Rhodes, and Pergamon (§5.1 of that document) — sit inside this single region, making Greek East this project's own natural "home turf" for that system's entire premise.
- **Religion, Religions of the Known World:** Delphi and Eleusis (§6) give this region the densest concentration of real, famous religious content anywhere on the roster, exceeding even Campania's own Cumae-and-Isis pairing.
- **Games & Spectacle:** Olympia (§6, §9) gives that system a genuine non-gladiatorial, non-venatio flagship content source — the real ancient Olympics themselves.
- **Legal & Court:** civitas libera (§4) and Rhodian Sea Law (§9) are both real, concrete legal-flavor ties distinct from anything the prior four region documents offer.
- **Politics & Patronage:** Achaea and Macedonia's own senatorial, demilitarized administration (§4) parallels Iberian Colony's own Baetica model; Cappadocia's imperial, frontier-facing status (§5) parallels that same document's own Tarraconensis contrast.
- **Military & Combat:** §5's Satala garrison gives that system a fourth distinct military-texture model — Great Power border defense, distinct from Gallic Frontier's multi-legion tribal frontier, Iberian Colony's internal senatorial/imperial split, and North African Colony's single-legion footprint.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§10), including this roster's first house defined by cultural prestige rather than political, military, or trade ambition.
- **Events:** §14's three Timeline Hooks complete this project's own "Rome fractures against itself" pattern with its most dramatic instance yet — not a colony's own rebellion, but the empire's own defining civil wars settled on this region's soil.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** Corinth's own rebuilt-from-destruction irony (§4, §14.1) gives that system this document's own equivalent of Alba Longa's, Numantia's, and Carthage's memorial-flavor locations.

## 17. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §13 and this region's own trade-versus-land economic balance are left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether any of §14's Timeline Hooks should ever be live rather than purely closed-history.** Unlike every other region's own document, all three hooks here are closed by the default Pax Romana start — a genuine, honest reflection of how early this region's own major drama falls in the timeline, but worth flagging as a real asymmetry rather than pretending it isn't there.
- **The household of Philokrates's own volatility.** §10 describes Rhodes's own fluctuating Roman favor as a real, live force on this house's standing, but doesn't specify how that volatility should actually be modeled mechanically — a natural Politics & Patronage or Rival Houses question more than this document's own to resolve.
- **Whether Galatian and Cappadocian culture deserve their own light Reputation-adjacent tracking**, even with Reputation Duality itself confirmed off for this region (§6 of the framework) — this document treats both as population texture only, per §8, but a household deeply embedded in the Cappadocian frontier specifically might plausibly want something closer to a light version of what Iberian and North African Colony's own tapering mechanic provides.
- **Cyprus's own status.** This document mentions Cypriot trade contact only in passing (§8) rather than giving it a Gazetteer entry — a plausible future addition if a later pass wants this region's own eastern Mediterranean reach to feel more complete.
