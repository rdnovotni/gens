# GENS — Reference Document: The Flora & Herbal Registry (Plants of the Known World)

*Built the same way the Bestiary was built for fauna, and its direct, deliberate sibling: a standalone, authoritative registry for everything rooted in the ground rather than walking, swimming, or flying across it. Where the Bestiary consolidated animal content already scattered across Hunts, Resources & Goods, and Religion, this document does the same for plants — and goes one step further than a pure flavor bank by giving several live mechanics (the Herbalist Trait, Masterworks' Poison Ring, Disease & Public Health's remedies, Religion's sacred plants) the shared reference layer they've been quietly missing. Per direction, this pass also fully re-lists every crop Resources & Goods already named — Grain, Grapes, Olives, Flax, Lavender, Woad, Saffron, Silphium, and generic "Herbs" — giving each a real species identity and flavor grounding, while that document keeps sole ownership of storage, perishability, and pricing.*

## 1. Purpose & Scope

Every plant-adjacent mechanic in this project so far has either invented its own small local list (a generic "Herbs" goods line, an unnamed "poison" input to a Scheme) or gestured at real content without ever naming it (the Herbalist Trait's own poison-identification bonus, the Court Physician's remedies, Religion's sacred groves). This document is where all of that consolidates into one real, comprehensive registry — the single place any future document should check before inventing a new plant, and the single place several already-real mechanics finally have named material to point at.

This document changes no existing mechanic. Resources & Goods keeps sole ownership of every crop's storage category, perishability, and pricing; Masterworks keeps sole ownership of the Poison Ring's own mechanical function; Disease & Public Health keeps sole ownership of its own remedy/treatment math. This is the shared reference layer underneath all of them — real species, real grounding, real historical use, and nothing numeric.

---

## 2. Taxonomy — How This Document Is Organized

Twelve practical categories, chosen for how the game actually uses a plant rather than strict botanical classification — the same organizing principle the Bestiary already established for fauna. A plant with more than one real historical use (Bay Laurel is both sacred and culinary; Mistletoe is both mildly toxic and sacred to Druidic practice; the Opium Poppy is both medicinal and sacred to Ceres) appears in every category its real use actually earns, rather than being forced into a single bucket.

## 16. Notable Absences — What's Deliberately Not Here

Consistent with the Bestiary's own honest precedent: this registry deliberately excludes every plant genuinely unavailable to the Mediterranean and Near Eastern world across 133 BC – AD 235 on real geographic grounds — tobacco, tomato, potato, maize, chili pepper, and true tea are all New World or East Asian domesticates with no real historical presence in this game's own setting, and no amount of narrative convenience justifies inventing an early appearance for any of them.

## 19. Open Questions

- **Whether Poison entries (§8) should eventually carry an actual potency or rarity tier** feeding Masterworks' Poison Ring more concretely, or remain purely descriptive as this pass leaves them.
- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention, remains absent throughout — this document adds identity and grounding only.
- **Regional coverage.** §15 now covers sixteen of seventeen Starting Regions with a real, specific flora entry — every region except Italian Heartland, which is deliberately left as the document's own Roman-core baseline rather than a variant of itself.
- **Hellebore, Squill, and Castor Bean (§8)** are deliberately described only at the level of their real historical reputation and use-case, never at the level of dosage or preparation — consistent with this project's standing caution around anything that could double as real-world guidance.
- **Whether the Claudius mushroom-poisoning anecdote and the Cherry's own Lucullan introduction** should be formally added to the Historical Timeline roster, or remain this document's own flavor-only footnote.
- **Food Culture's own future spice/flavoring content.** Several entries here (§6, §13) plausibly overlap with ground that document may want to develop further in its own right; this document's entries should be read as the shared botanical layer underneath whatever culinary depth that document eventually adds, not a competing claim to it.
