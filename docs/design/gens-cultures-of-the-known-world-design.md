# GENS — Reference Document: Cultures of the Known World
*An extension of Education & Culture's own `culture` field, now covering thirty-six real, playable cultures — including this pass's own minor-but-mechanically-real additions: Caledonian (a real, distinct third "unconquered" texture, backed by the real Hadrian's and Antonine Walls), Batavian (real, prized Roman auxiliary troops), Cretan (real archer specialists), Palmyrene (a real, later-rising desert trade city), and Alexandrian Greek (the real, distinct urban population inside an otherwise Egyptian-culture province) — plus a further seven lighter sub-culture footnotes (Belgae, Cantabrian, Rhodian, Spartan, Cypriot, Samaritan, Idumaean) noted for real historical texture without full tracked values. Also newly acknowledged: even "Roman" culture itself was already a real blend of absorbed Italic peoples (Samnite, Sardinian, Ligurian) by the time this game's own range opens.*

## 1. Scope & Role

Education & Culture's own `culture` field was built with four values, matching the game's four starting regions. This document expands that roster heavily into a real, historically grounded catalog spanning the actual world Rome had contact with — political, military, or purely commercial — across this game's own fixed 133 BC – AD 235 range. It adds no new mechanics: every Character carrying one of these culture values reads against exactly the Cultural Drift, blended-marriage, and Institution-of-Renown machinery Education & Culture already built.

---

## 2. Categorizing the Roster — Four Real Categories

- **Provincial** — already within Roman administration for most or all of the range, including, new this pass, a real sub-case: a culture already fully absorbed and culturally influential *before the range even opens* (§3.1).
- **Frontier** — genuinely outside Roman control for some or all of the range, the real subject of Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples. A culture's category can shift mid-range at its own real historical conquest date.
- **Great Power** — Parthia; Armenia sits in its own real, distinct Contested Buffer position between the other two.
- **Trade Contact Only** — real, playable cultures Rome had commercial and cultural contact with but never political or military contact with at meaningful scale — extended this pass from two entries to four.

### 3.1 Cultures Already in Eclipse Before This Game's Range Even Opens

Punic, above, is one real example of a culture this game finds already fading rather than rising. It has real company:

**Etruscan** — by 133 BC, Etruria had already been politically absorbed into Rome for well over a century, and this document doesn't pretend otherwise: there is no meaningful Etruscan Frontier or Client state left by the time this game's own range opens, and a Character of Etruscan culture is, by this point, simply a regional flavor of Roman rather than a distinct political identity. What survives, and survives *directly* into mechanics this project already built, is **religious influence**: Religion's own Priesthood section (§6.2 of that doc) already names the real historical fact that Roman augury and haruspicy — the whole Auspices mechanic (Religion §4.2) — descended directly from real Etruscan religious practice, and that document's own itinerant Haruspex is explicitly modeled as "traditionally an Etruscan specialist." Etruscan culture, in other words, is worth naming here specifically because Rome absorbed the *people* completely while keeping a real, load-bearing piece of their *religion* — a genuine, accurate historical irony worth having on record rather than a culture this document needs its own live tracked value for.

**Further Italic peoples, noted more briefly** — Etruscan isn't the only real, distinct pre-Roman identity already dissolving into "Roman" by this game's own start date. The real Samnites and other Oscan-speaking Italic peoples were only fully enfranchised as citizens following the real Social War (91–88 BC), just decades before this game's range opens; native pre-Punic **Sardinian** identity and the small coastal **Ligurian** peoples occupy a similar "already fading, once real and distinct" position. None of these merit their own tracked culture value — by 133 BC they're genuinely, functionally Roman — but they're worth naming for the same reason Etruscan is: **"Roman" itself was already a real blend before this game's own clock even starts**, not a pure, unmixed starting point the rest of this document's cultures are drifting away from.

### 3.2 Minor Sub-Groups, Noted in Passing

A handful of further real, distinct sub-groupings exist within cultures this document already tracks under one broader tag — genuinely real, but narrow enough in scope that they stay flavor-level detail rather than separate tracked values, consistent with this document's own "one tag per broad grouping" standard:

- **Belgae** — a real, distinct northern Gallic confederation Julius Caesar himself explicitly distinguishes from the rest of Gaul in his own *Commentarii* — real enough to warrant its own separate treatment in a primary Roman source, even though this document keeps it inside the single Gallic tag.
- **Cantabrian** — a real, fiercely resistant people of northern Iberia; the real Cantabrian Wars (29–19 BC), which Augustus personally oversaw part of, mark the actual final Roman conquest of the Iberian peninsula — a genuine, dateable capstone to the Iberian entry's own Provincial status.

### 5.1 Minor Sub-Groups, Noted in Passing

- **Rhodian** — already a real, named destination in Education & Culture's own Institutions of Renown (§5 of that document, the premier real Rhetoric destination); worth a further note here for its real historical **Rhodian Sea Law**, an actual ancient maritime legal code genuinely influential enough to echo into later Roman and even later Byzantine maritime law — a real, concrete Legal & Court flavor tie distinct from the Institution's own Rhetoric-training role.
- **Spartan / Laconian** — a real, distinct case within the Hellenic world: by the Roman period, Sparta had become something close to a living museum of its own former self, and Romans are real, documented visitors specifically curious about Sparta's preserved (if by then largely ceremonial) ancestral customs — a genuine "tourism of one's own conquered past" flavor unique to this single city.
- **Cypriot** — a real island culture at the crossroads of the Hellenic and Levantine worlds, worth naming for one concrete etymological fact: the modern word "copper" itself derives from *Cyprium*, "Cyprus metal" — real, ancient, and famous enough as a copper source that Resources & Goods' own metal trade can point directly at this specific island by name.

## 10. Beyond the Frontier — Real Cultures, Rarely Encountered

Extended this pass from four entries to six, all sharing the identical treatment: real, valid `culture` values, genuinely playable, deliberately and extremely rare in actual appearance.

### 10.1 Indian *(unchanged)*

Real, extensively documented contact, anchored to Augustus's own recorded Indian embassies and the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*.

### 10.2 Chinese *(unchanged)*

Anchored to the real AD 166 "Andun" mission the *Hou Hanshu* documents, landing almost exactly inside the same window as the Parthian War and Antonine Plague.

### 10.3 Garamantian *(unchanged)*

A real trans-Saharan civilization, anchored to a real Augustan-era punitive expedition.

### 10.4 Aksumite *(unchanged)*

A real, rising East African power whose ascent genuinely begins within this game's own range.

### 10.5 Taprobane *(new)*

Real, and — worth stating plainly — even more concretely documented than the China entry above: the real Roman historian Pliny the Elder records a specific, detailed account of contact with Taprobane (identified with reasonable confidence as ancient Sri Lanka) during Claudius's own reign. A freedman of a Roman tax-farmer, sailing the real Red Sea trade route, was blown off course and ended up at Taprobane's own royal court; the local king, hearing his account of Rome, sent his own embassy back — a real, named, narratively complete historical episode this document can draw on almost without embellishment. A Character of Taprobane culture is exactly the kind of rare, real possibility that specific historical episode makes plausible.

### 10.6 Sogdian *(new)*

A real, and arguably *more* historically accurate addition than assuming direct Chinese contact was common: the actual Silk Road ran through many real hands, and Sogdian merchants — based in Central Asia, in cities like the real historical Samarkand — were among its single most important real historical intermediaries, moving goods (and, occasionally, people) between the Chinese and Parthian-and-Roman worlds without either end ever meeting the other directly. A Sogdian Character is, if anything, a *more* plausible distant-trade origin for a household to encounter than a Chinese one specifically, since most of what reached Rome from the East passed through Sogdian hands at some point along the way.

### 10.7 How Such a Character Actually Enters Play *(unchanged in mechanism, now covering six cultures)*

The same three real, deliberately rare channels apply uniformly across all six Trade-Contact-Only cultures: a rare **Travel Encounter** weighted to the relevant trade-route termini; a **Slave Market** novelty listing carrying a real Dignitas curiosity-value; or a **Companion recruitment** through Travel's existing machinery. Once such a Character joins the household, nothing about them is special-cased.

## 11. Legendary Edges of the Map — Where History Blurs Into Belief

A deliberate, clearly-marked exception to everything above. Every culture in §3–10 is a real people this document is confident actually existed and actually had the relationship with Rome described. This section is different on purpose: it covers places Romans themselves genuinely wrote about and, in many cases, genuinely believed in — real ancient beliefs, attested in real ancient sources — that modern historical scholarship regards as legendary, wildly exaggerated, or geographically confused. Per direction, this is where the document allows itself to push a little past confirmed historicity, while drawing a firm, explicit line: **nothing here is a valid `culture` value a Character can carry.** This is rumor, legend, and the edge of what a Roman geographer would draw before the map simply ran out — real content, but Correspondence and Travel flavor exclusively, never a playable origin.

- **Hyperborea** — a real, attested ancient Greek and Roman geographic legend, discussed by Pliny the Elder among others as if it were a real place: a paradise beyond the north wind, home to a people untouched by war or hardship. Modern scholarship treats this as myth rather than a garbled account of any real northern people. Included here as exactly that — a legend real Romans told, not a real culture this document asserts existed.
- **Thule** — genuinely more grounded than Hyperborea, and worth distinguishing from it directly: the real ancient explorer Pytheas claimed to have actually sailed there, and later Roman geographers treated it as a real, if remote and vaguely located, northern land (modern guesses range from Norway to Iceland to Scotland's own Shetland Islands). "Ultima Thule" — the edge of the known world — became a real, standing Roman idiom for exactly that idea. This document treats Thule as the honest northern counterpart to Serica/China's own eastern edge: real enough that someone genuinely tried to reach it, vague enough that nobody in this game's own fiction should be confident what, if anything, is actually there.
- **The Fortunate Isles** — a real ancient Atlantic legend, discussed by both Pliny and Plutarch, of idyllic islands somewhere beyond the Pillars of Hercules (most plausibly, in modern reckoning, a garbled real account of the Canary Islands). Worth one specific, real, delightful detail: Plutarch records that the real Roman general Sertorius seriously considered retiring there. A legend real enough that a real historical figure reportedly wanted to actually go.

**How this is used, concretely:** a rare Travel Encounter or a Character's own personal Ambition can reference any of these three as a genuine in-fiction rumor or goal — a Character who's heard sailors' tales of the Fortunate Isles and wants, someday, to actually go looking; a geographer-Companion who insists Thule is real and reachable. None of this should ever resolve into an actual playable Character *from* Hyperborea, Thule, or the Fortunate Isles — the moment this document did that, it would cross from "real historical belief, played straight" into invention, exactly the line direction asked to approach without crossing.

## 13. Naming Conventions — Real Patterns, Not Exhaustive Lists

- **Gallic/British/Hibernian:** chieftain names frequently carry the real, attested Celtic *-rix* suffix ("king"), as in the historical Vercingetorix.
- **Germanic:** names are frequently real two-element compounds.
- **Egyptian:** names are frequently real theophoric constructions.
- **Judaean:** names are frequently real patronymic or theophoric Hebrew forms.
- **Parthian/Armenian:** names frequently carry real Persian-derived elements.
- **Etruscan:** names frequently follow a real, distinct (and famously non-Indo-European) naming structure, still visible in a handful of Roman family names that were themselves originally Etruscan.
