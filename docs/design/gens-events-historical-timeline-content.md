# GENS — Authored Content: The Historical Timeline (133 BC – AD 235)
*Now enriched with real, vivid, genuinely fun detail on top of the prior pass's structural density: Elagabalus's own wild reign (a teenage Syrian sun-god priest attempting to overturn Jupiter's own primacy — real history, and a direct, empire-scale echo of Education & Culture's own Hellenization tension), Commodus fighting as a gladiator, Pliny the Elder dying while observing Vesuvius up close, the real Antioch earthquake that nearly killed Trajan mid-campaign, Nero's extravagant Domus Aurea, the Baths of Caracalla, and the honest detail that Rome abandoned the Antonine Wall within twenty years of building it. A real gap in the Severan succession (Macrinus and Elagabalus, both previously skipped) is also closed.*

## 7. Mechanical Notes & Direct Game-System Ties

- **The Sassanid transition (AD 224–226) is this pass's own single biggest structural addition.** Parthia has carried enormous weight across Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples, Cultures of the Known World, and Religions — a genuine Great Power, never "barbarian" flavor. This is the real, historical end of that specific dynasty, falling squarely within the playable range. A playthrough that reaches the mid-220s should feel the ground shift under whatever Parthian relationship it had built — Hostage Exchange arrangements, a Legate's own standing, an Armenian allegiance balance — all resting on a power that's genuinely ending, not a stable backdrop.
- **Elagabalus's own reign (AD 218–222) is this pass's own richest thematic tie, and arguably the most fun one.** A teenage hereditary priest of a Syrian sun-god becoming Emperor and then genuinely attempting to place his own foreign cult above Jupiter as Rome's own chief state religion is real history, not invented drama — and it's the exact same Hellenization-versus-Mos-Maiorum tension Education & Culture's own §7–8 already models for a single household, now playing out at the absolute top of the Roman state itself. A household deep in its own Hellenization arc during this specific reign has real, dateable Imperial cover for what it's doing; a staunchly Mos Maiorum household living through it has a real, concrete, empire-scale outrage to react to.
- **AD 212's Constitutio Antoniniana** remains this document's richest tie to Policies & Edicts' own Citizenship Grant Edict, unchanged from the first pass.
- **Regional density is now real across all four starting regions**, not concentrated in the Late Republic alone: Iberia (Numantine War, Cantabrian Wars), the Greek East (the Mithridatic Wars, Galatia, Cappadocia, Cyprus), and the Gallic frontier (the Gallic Wars, Vercingetorix specifically) all have genuine, spread-out events now.
- **AD 211–212 stacks three real, connected entries in rapid succession** (Severus's death, Geta's murder, the Constitutio Antoniniana) — a real, historically accurate cluster of a violent succession immediately followed by one of the single most consequential legal acts in Roman history, worth firing as a connected sequence per Events §8's chaining model rather than three unrelated coincidences.
- **The Marcomannic Wars (AD 178–180) and Avidius Cassius's revolt (AD 175)** both puncture the "High Principate as uninterrupted golden age" read a player might otherwise take from the calmer Nerva-Antonine stretch — real, genuine trouble existed even at the Empire's own high point, without requiring the player to wait for the Severan era's own more obvious instability.

## 8. Open Questions / Remaining Gaps

- **Still not the complete catalog.** Genuinely comprehensive authoring of all 368 years remains real future work; this is now a dense double pass rather than a claim of exhaustiveness.
- **Egypt and Nubia specifically** remain thinner after 30 BC than the other regions now are — a plausible next target given how much real administrative and cultural material survives from Roman Egypt specifically.
- **The Sassanid transition's own downstream content** — how Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples should actually treat a "new" Persian power replacing Parthia partway through a playthrough — is flagged here as a real, meaningful design question for that document's own future revisit, not resolved in this authoring pass.
- **All numeric sizing** remains deferred per this project's standing convention.
