# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — North African Colony
*Final polish and balance pass. Fills in the Starting Regions framework's Region Profile schema (§4) for a single unified region carrying a real double contrast: an older, long-settled eastern core (Africa Proconsularis, built quite literally on Carthage's own ashes) against a more recently annexed western half (Mauretania), and — like Iberian Colony — a "tapering" Reputation Duality (Starting Regions §6) with its own concrete mechanical shape, keyed here to Mauretania's real annexation in AD 44 rather than Iberia's Cantabrian Wars. This pass adds a worked example (§6.1) for the doubled tapering mechanic, fixes a counting error in the Templated Background section, and proactively flags Cyrene's own genuine boundary position between this document and the not-yet-written Greek East document rather than waiting for that future document to discover the conflict. Intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — Built on Carthage's Own Ashes

This region carries one of the most striking real ironies available anywhere in this project: Rome destroyed Carthage in 146 BC, salting the earth of its own enemy's capital in the most total act of conquest this game's entire timeline contains — and then, within a few generations, rebuilt it as a thriving Roman colony that became the real administrative capital of Africa Proconsularis. A household settling here isn't just extracting a colony's wealth; it's building a life, quite literally, on ground Rome once tried to erase.

Like Iberian Colony, this region carries a real internal contrast rather than being split into two selectable regions: **Africa Proconsularis** (the old Numidian and Carthaginian heartland, running east) became Roman territory early and thoroughly — Numidia's own core was already effectively provincial after Caesar's real victory at Thapsus in 46 BC — while **Mauretania** (running west, toward the Atlantic) remained a nominally independent client kingdom for decades longer, only formally annexed under Claudius in AD 44 following the real Aedemon's Revolt. This document keeps both halves under one region, consistent with the precedent set by both Italian Heartland's Latium/Campania split rationale (kept separate) and Gallic Frontier's own single-region choice (kept together) — here, the historical stagger between east and west is temporal as much as spatial, and reads more naturally as one region's own internal timeline than as two separate starting choices.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Real, exceptional agricultural fertility along the coastal strip and the Bagradas river valley — the actual historical basis for this region's own real status as one of Rome's primary grain suppliers, alongside Egypt and Sicily. Genuine hill and mountain terrain further inland carries real marble deposits — the actual historical source of the real, distinctive yellow-toned Numidian marble, quarried at Simitthu. Coastal terrain runs the length of this region's own long Mediterranean frontage; unlike Gaul or Iberia, there's no meaningfully distinct second coast here — the Atlantic-facing far west (around Volubilis) is a real, much smaller and more marginal maritime zone by comparison. The interior, moving south and west toward the Sahara, carries the roster's most genuinely arid, marginal terrain — a real, honest limit on this region's own agricultural expansion that the fertile coastal strip doesn't extend indefinitely inland.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Consistent with the core doc's own "strong trade and resource extraction... moderate risk, colonial-administration flavor" — the same category Iberian Colony occupies, and for similar reasons. Land along the fertile coastal strip, particularly near Carthage and Utica, supports genuinely deep agricultural wealth and correspondingly higher cost; land further inland, toward the arid margin, is cheaper and thinner, closer to Gallic Frontier's own character without that region's own security risk to go with it. Mauretania's own more recently annexed status (§1, §6) keeps its markets rawer and less developed than the long-settled Proconsular core.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

This region's own Legal Status mix runs along the same east/west line as everything else: Africa Proconsularis, settled and provincial for most of this game's own range, carries a Legal Status mix approaching Iberian Colony's own Baetica — genuinely Romanized, citizen-heavy in its cities. Mauretania, still a client kingdom for a real, substantial portion of this game's own timeline (§6), carries a rawer, more Peregrine-leaning population, especially away from Volubilis. Politics & Patronage's own cursus honorum access here sits closer to Iberian Colony's own moderate difficulty than to Gallic Frontier's own harder path — a real, workable route to Roman advancement, without either Baetica's own spectacular Balbus-of-Gades precedent or Latium's own ease.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — One Legion, No External Neighbor

Like Iberian Colony, and for a similar structural reason: **this region has no standing external Frontier neighbor** once its own conquest arc closes (§6, §15). The Sahara's own real, practical vastness functions less like a hostile border and more like a hard edge to the inhabited and administered world — genuine, rare desert trade contact exists (the Trade Contact Only Garamantian culture, per Cultures of the Known World §10), but nothing resembling Gallic Frontier's own live, negotiable Germanic relationship.

What makes this region's own military texture genuinely distinct from either Gaul's or Iberia's: **this entire region, for most of its history, was garrisoned by a single legion** — the real Legio III Augusta, based across several real successive fortresses over time, this document's own Gazetteer naming its best-attested and longest-lasting home, Lambaesis. One legion, holding down a region this large, is a real, concrete illustration of just how settled and low-threat this territory actually was by Roman military calculation — a genuinely lighter military footprint than even Iberia's own Legio VII Gemina commands in Tarraconensis alone, let alone Gallic Frontier's own multi-legion Rhine commitment.

## 6. Reputation Duality — Tapering by Annexation Date, East and West

The framework document names North African Colony a **tapering** Reputation Duality region, with local standing remaining real but at lower intensity than an active frontier, "since Punic cultural legacy in eclipse rather than erased means local standing remains a real, distinct thing to hold even once formal conquest is old news" (Starting Regions §6). This document gives that taper the same three-position shape Iberian Colony uses, but anchored to a different real date and, unlike Iberia's own single hinge, applied **separately to each half of the region**:

- **Africa Proconsularis (the east):** hinges on the real Battle of Thapsus (46 BC). Any default Pax Romana start reads this half of the region as **Long-Settled** — Reputation Duality here functions close to a single, converged Dignitas value for the entire remainder of this game's own range.
- **Mauretania (the west):** hinges on the real annexation of AD 44. A playthrough starting before that date reads Mauretania as **Active Conquest** or **Recently Pacified** depending on proximity to Aedemon's Revolt (§15.2); a playthrough starting well after it reads Mauretania as **Long-Settled** in turn, though — consistent with Iberian Colony's own §6 — this document treats that convergence as gradual and substantial rather than absolute.

The practical effect: a household anchored in the east (§12) experiences Reputation Duality as largely a settled, background concern for nearly the entire game's default range, while a household that deliberately expands or relocates west into Mauretania (§14's own Distance Tier making that a genuinely short move) can find itself managing a real, live local-standing axis much later into the timeline than its eastern neighbors ever have to.

### 6.1 A Worked Example — One Household, Two Timelines

Consider a household anchored at its own default Home Anchor (§12), near Carthage, in a default Pax Romana playthrough. Its own Africa Proconsularis position sits Long-Settled: an Exploitative Provincial Administration Posture (Policies & Edicts §2.10) here produces almost no local-standing movement at all, the same muted effect Iberian Colony's own §6.1 describes for a Long-Settled Baetica. Now suppose that same household, generations later, acquires a second holding in Mauretania (Starting Regions §7, Distant Holding) — a short, Near-tier move per §14's own internal table. If that acquisition happens early enough in the timeline, relative to the real AD 44 annexation, the household suddenly finds itself managing a genuinely live, diverging local-standing axis at its own new western holding, even though its long-settled eastern seat has felt none of that tension in generations. The same household, the same Doctrine, two entirely different lived experiences of Reputation Duality — purely a function of which of this region's own two real historical timelines a given holding happens to sit on.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

This region's own real, lingering Punic cultural legacy — Baal Hammon and Tanit, per Cultures of the Known World §3 — sits at its most concrete anywhere in the game here, more so even than Iberian Colony's own residual Punic thread (Starting Regions: Iberian Colony §7), since Carthage itself, this document's own capital Gazetteer entry (§10), is the actual historical seat of that tradition rather than a colonial outpost of it. Numidian/Mauri native religious tradition, per Religions of the Known World, is a real, though honestly thin-record, native practice existing alongside the Punic thread rather than being absorbed by it. A small, real Greek colonial religious presence exists too, at Cyrene (§10) specifically — a third real Greek colonial pocket on this document's own roster, alongside Massilia (Gallic Frontier) and Emporion (Iberian Colony).

---

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

Grain and olive oil, exported to Rome in real, substantial quantities, are this region's own primary economic identity — a genuine peer to Egypt's own future grain role, and, per §3, the actual historical basis of this region's coastal agricultural wealth. Numidian marble (§2) gives this region its own distinct building-stone luxury identity, and Exotic Beasts — already tagged an "Iberian/North African source" in Resources & Goods' own registry (§7 of that document) — are a real, concrete Games & Spectacle supply line: North Africa was a genuine, major real historical source of lions and other arena beasts. A further real, concrete detail worth naming directly: the historical Mauretanian client king Juba II is real-documented as having established a genuine royal purple-dye manufacturing operation on the "Purple Islands" off this region's own Atlantic coast — a second, distinct source of Tyrian Purple (Resources & Goods §7) beyond the Iberian and Greek East coastal traditions already tagged there.

**Silphium's own real home.** This project's own signature permanently-depletable good (Resources & Goods §6.1) is real-historically rooted specifically in Cyrenaica — the eastern edge of this region, bordering the Greek East. Cyrene (§10) is this document's own concrete Gazetteer anchor for that mechanic, giving the game's single most historically poignant resource-depletion story a real, named home rather than an abstract border-zone footnote.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, spanning this region's own real Punic-to-Numidian-to-Roman range.

- **Gens Malchia** *(seated at Carthage)* — a Romanized Punic merchant house, its own name echoing the real, historically attested Punic name-element *Malchus* without claiming descent from any specific real individual; built on grain and trade wealth flowing through the rebuilt capital, and this region's own clearest illustration of Punic heritage persisting in genuinely prosperous, thoroughly Roman-integrated form rather than as a fading curiosity.
- **The household of Massuna** *(seated at Cirta)* — a native Numidian aristocratic house with a real, plausible claim of descent from the old royal line that once ruled from this same city, deliberately given a native name rather than a Roman one, echoing Gallic Frontier's household of Cintugnatus and Iberian Colony's household of Segontius. This region's own clearest illustration of §6's local-standing axis, especially in Mauretania or during an early-enough start in the Proconsular east.
- **Gens Fadia** *(seated at Leptis Magna)* — a marble-and-grain trade house, commercially minded and structurally similar to Iberian Colony's own Gens Annia, but built on this region's own distinct goods (§8) rather than mining wealth.
- **Gens Petronia** *(seated at Lambaesis)* — a garrison-connected family tied to Legio III Augusta's own real, long institutional presence — structurally the same archetype as Gallic Frontier's Gens Fulvia and Iberian Colony's Gens Terentia, here justified by §5's own single-legion military identity rather than an external frontier or an internal north/south split.

## 12. Home Anchor

**Along the fertile coastal strip near Carthage and Utica**, in the heart of Africa Proconsularis. This anchors the household in this region's own default, long-settled, agriculturally prosperous identity — consistent with the core doc's "moderate risk" framing, and, per §1, deliberately keeps the household's own prosperity built visibly and knowingly on the site of Rome's own most total historical act of destruction in this game's entire timeline. This is this region's own version of Campania's Vesuvius-adjacent and Iberian Colony's mining-adjacent Home Anchor choices: leaning into the region's defining historical weight rather than settling somewhere it can be politely ignored.

---

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Every one of Core §5.2's four Templated Background archetypes already carries a primary claim elsewhere on the roster. North African Colony offers its own further **secondary** expressions rather than competing for primacy: Gens Malchia and Gens Fadia (§11) give **"jumped-up equestrian merchant"** a grain-and-marble-trade flavor, a third distinct reading alongside Campania's shipping wealth and Iberian Colony's mining-and-garum wealth; and the household of Massuna gives **"provincial notable"** a third flavor — a native royal-descent claim distinct from Gallic Frontier's straddling aristocrat and Iberian Colony's economic-power variant.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 The Jugurthine War (111–105 BC, closed history by default)

A real, historically significant early Roman intervention in Numidia, falling within this game's own opening decades — effectively closed history for any default-era playthrough, but the direct real grounding behind Cirta's own Gazetteer entry (§10) and the household of Massuna's own claimed royal lineage (§11).

### 15.2 The Battle of Thapsus and Africa Nova (46 BC, closed history by default)

Caesar's real, decisive North African campaign against the forces backing Pompey, ending in the creation of Africa Nova from the old Numidian core — this region's own "Rome fractures against itself" hook, in the same register as Iberian Colony's Sertorian War, though closed history for any default-era playthrough given how early it falls. The direct real hinge behind §6's own designation of Africa Proconsularis as Long-Settled under any ordinary start.

### 15.3 Aedemon's Revolt and Mauretania's Annexation (AD 40–44)

The defining event of this document's own western half: a real North African uprising following the real execution of Mauretania's own client king, resolved by Mauretania's formal annexation as a Roman province under Claudius in AD 44. Unlike §15.1 and §15.2, this hook isn't purely flavor for a default-era playthrough — it's the actual historical event §6's own Mauretania-specific Conquest Timeline Position is built around, live for any playthrough starting close enough to it.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** fulfills the Region Profile schema (§4) for North African Colony, and gives that document's own "tapering" Reputation Duality call (§6) a second concrete implementation, distinct from Iberian Colony's own single-hinge version by applying two separate hinges to two halves of one region.
- **Politics & Patronage:** §4's Legal Status texture and cursus honorum framing sit deliberately between Iberian Colony's own moderate difficulty and Gallic Frontier's own harder path.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 specifies that document's existing North African tags (Numidian marble, Exotic Beasts) and gives Silphium's own permanently-depletable mechanic its first named, concrete regional home; the Purple Islands detail extends that document's existing Tyrian Purple tag with a second, real source distinct from the Iberian and Greek East coastal tradition.
- **Games & Spectacle:** the Exotic Beasts supply line (§8) is this document's own direct, concrete contribution to that system's venatio content.
- **Religion, Religions of the Known World:** this region's own Punic religious thread (§7) is this document's strongest expression of that tradition anywhere on the launch roster, more concrete even than Iberian Colony's own residual version.
- **Military & Combat:** §5's single-legion footprint gives that system a genuine third military-texture model, distinct from Gallic Frontier's multi-legion frontier and Iberian Colony's own internal senatorial/imperial split.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), deliberately spanning from a still-native royal-descent household to a thoroughly Romanized Punic trade family.
- **Events:** §15's three Timeline Hooks include this project's second "Rome fractures against itself" flashpoint (Thapsus), and a Mauretania-specific annexation hook that, unlike Iberia's single Cantabrian Wars hinge, only governs half of one region.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** Carthage's own real, ironic weight (§1, §10) gives that system this document's own equivalent of Alba Longa's and Numantia's memorial-flavor locations, arguably the most thematically loaded of the three.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — both sub-regions' own local-standing swing multipliers, every Distance Tier in §14, and this region's own agricultural-vs-arid terrain balance are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether a household can meaningfully "hold" both sub-regions' own Reputation Duality states at once.** §6 describes Africa Proconsularis and Mauretania as independently tracked, but doesn't resolve what a household actually anchored in one and later expanding into the other (§14's own short internal Distance Tier makes this a real, plausible move) experiences mechanically — likely a Starting Regions §7 Distant Holding question more than this document's own to resolve.
- **The household of Massuna's own long-term arc**, mirroring the same open question left for the household of Cintugnatus (Gallic Frontier) and the household of Segontius (Iberian Colony) — deliberately left to a future Familia or Legal & Court pass.
- **Whether Cyrene's own Silphium anchor implies any special mechanical weight beyond the good's own existing depletion rules** — this document ties the location to the good but doesn't specify whether settling near Cyrene should carry any distinct bonus, risk, or narrative flag beyond ordinary access.
- **The Purple Islands' own Gazetteer status.** §8 mentions this real site in the goods discussion but doesn't promote it to a full Gazetteer entry in §10 — a future pass could reasonably add it if Tyrian Purple ever needs a more concrete regional anchor than the Volubilis/western-Mauretania area generally provides.
- **Cyrene's own regional ownership.** §10 flags this directly: Cyrenaica was real-historically paired administratively with Crete, not with Africa proper, putting Cyrene genuinely at the boundary between this document and the not-yet-written Greek East document. This document claims it first, on the strength of the Silphium connection (§8) — the future Greek East document should treat Cyrene as already spoken for here rather than re-claiming it, the same way this document itself deferred to Iberian Colony's own prior claim on Garum.
