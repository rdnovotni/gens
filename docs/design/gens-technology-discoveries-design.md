# GENS — System Design: Technology & Discoveries (§6.53, finalized)
*Comprehensive expansion and finalization pass. A genuinely new axis this project hasn't had until now: every Building tier, Villa room, and piece of Military equipment has so far been treated as static content, gated only by regional Economic Package or Estate stage — never by whether it actually exists yet in the world. This document adds that missing dimension, and does it the way this project has handled its previous deliberate historical departures (Arabia Felix's counterfactual toggle, Mesopotamia's alternate-history layer, Hunts' confirmed mythological creatures): named plainly, bounded on purpose, and built to add real content rather than to quietly rewrite history. This closing pass resolves every open structural question the first draft left standing: Discoveries, not whole Eras, are now the real unit of both natural arrival and acceleration; publication now has a real diffusion mechanic borrowed directly from Cultural Drift; two households can now genuinely race for the same breakthrough; a rival's exclusive edge can now actually be stolen; and, in the most on-theme addition of the whole document, a Discovery can be genuinely lost again, mirroring the real, well-documented fact that some Roman engineering knowledge really did vanish for centuries after the Western Empire's own decline. This document is the engine; a dedicated companion roster of every individual Discovery follows separately.*

## 1. Scope & Role

Nothing in this project currently asks "does this exist yet." This document adds that real, missing axis: individual, named **Discoveries** (not whole Eras — see §2) that, by default, become available to the whole known world on their own real historical schedule, with real, deliberate levers (§6) a household can pull to access a specific one well ahead of that schedule, and a real, personal edge (§7) for whoever gets there first. Per direction, this document combines three approaches rather than picking one purely: a shared, historically-paced backbone; genuine household-level early access; and Cultures of the Known World's own existing Cultural Drift providing natural regional variation.

---

## 2. The Known World's Real Historical Floor

The first draft treated whole Eras as the unit of advancement; this pass corrects that, since real history didn't arrive in six clean, uniform jumps — individual technologies and reforms landed at genuinely different times. **The real unit of this document is the individual Discovery, not the Era.** Every Discovery carries its own real **Natural Arrival Year** — the point at which it becomes available to the whole known world automatically, with no player involvement required, exactly matching Events: Historical Timeline's own real-dated convention. A household that never touches §6's acceleration levers simply experiences every Discovery precisely on its own real historical schedule — full historical grounding preserved by default, for anyone who wants nothing to do with this system at all.

Eras (§12) remain useful purely as **organizational buckets** — a convenient way to group and browse the future roster document by rough historical period — but carry no mechanical trigger of their own. Nothing "unlocks" because an Era arrives; things unlock because a specific Discovery's own Natural Arrival Year passes, or because a household accelerates it early.

## 3. Discovery Categories — Organizing the Future Roster

A real, fixed taxonomy this document establishes so the upcoming companion roster has a consistent shape to populate, mirroring how the Bestiary organized itself into nine categories before any individual creature was listed:

- **Engineering & Construction** (domes, aqueducts, fortification)
- **Military** (equipment, doctrine, siege and naval weapons)
- **Administrative & Legal** (currency, bureaucracy, legal codification)
- **Agricultural & Economic** (production, trade infrastructure)
- **Medical & Scientific** (Disease & Public Health, Naturalist-adjacent content)
- **Cultural & Religious** (kept deliberately light and cross-referential rather than duplicating Religion's own independent timeline)

---

## 4. Discovery Tiers — Minor and Major

A real, useful distinction the first draft's flat treatment lacked:

- **Minor Discoveries** — incremental, real improvements (a refined tool, a modest administrative streamlining) — faster and cheaper to pursue through §6's levers, lower Dignitas/Prestige payoff, and the more common outcome of an ordinary Institutional Patronage project.
- **Major Discoveries** — genuinely era-defining breakthroughs (a new Building capstone, a new Military unit type, a full legal codification) — rare, slow, expensive, and carrying real, outsized prestige and Chronicle weight precisely because they're hard to reach early.

---

## 5. Prerequisites — Some Discoveries Build on Others

A real, honest structural rule: certain Discoveries — particularly Major ones — logically depend on an earlier one already being known, exactly the way a real engineering or legal tradition builds on what came before it rather than appearing from nothing. A household attempting to accelerate a Major Discovery whose real prerequisite hasn't yet arrived (naturally or by acceleration) simply cannot begin that project — a real, legible gate rather than an arbitrary lock, and the natural reason a sufficiently ambitious household might deliberately accelerate a whole short prerequisite chain in sequence to reach one genuinely coveted Major Discovery early.

---

## 6. Acceleration — Three Real Levers, and Real Failure Risk

### 6.1 Institutional Patronage

Funding an Institution of Renown (Education & Culture §5) or a household's own Bibliotheca/Scriptorium research effort, reusing Literary Patronage's own commission pattern directly.

### 6.2 Individual Genius

A rare, sufficiently Learned Character holding the Engineer, Naturalist, Architect, or Astrologer Trait can, through a sustained personal Authorship-style project (Books & Manuscripts §3), produce a genuine technical treatise that is itself the Discovery.

### 6.3 Cultural and Regional Head Start

No special player effort required: a household seated at Alexandria or Pergamon holds a real, built-in advantage for certain categories, purely from real historical fact about where the ancient world's own centers of learning actually were.

### 6.4 Real Failure — Not Every Attempt Succeeds

New this pass, and a necessary honesty check: an Institutional Patronage project or an Individual Genius effort is a genuine gamble, not a guaranteed purchase. A promising research line can run its full course and simply fail to produce the targeted Discovery — the investment isn't refunded, but it isn't entirely wasted either, contributing a small, real, permanent head start toward a future attempt at the same target, the same honest "progress isn't guaranteed, but effort isn't erased" logic Masterworks' own Land Reclamation (Private Infrastructure §5) already established for a completely different kind of long, uncertain project.

---

## 7. Household Innovation — A Personal, Early Edge

A successful acceleration (§6) does **not** automatically become available to the whole known world. It first becomes the discovering household's own **exclusive early edge** — a genuinely futuristic Building tier, Masterwork material, or administrative option usable only by that household, at real risk of theft (§10).

### 7.1 Keep It, or Give It Away

A household can jealously guard a Discovery as a standing private advantage for as long as it can defend it, or deliberately **publish** it (§8) — releasing a real technical treatise via Books & Manuscripts, or making an outright civic gift of it per Public Works & Euergetism — in exchange for genuine, outsized Cultural Prestige, Dignitas, and a real Renown Attracts Renown trigger. Exclusive advantage versus shared legacy — a real tradeoff, not a dominant choice either way.

---

## 8. Publication & Diffusion — How a Discovery Actually Spreads

Resolved directly this pass: publishing a Discovery doesn't make it instantly available everywhere. It pulls that Discovery's own Natural Arrival Year forward **for the wider world**, and then spreads through the exact same real machinery Cultures of the Known World's own Cultural Drift already uses — trade-route proximity, Institution of Renown contact, and regional adjacency all genuinely matter, meaning a published Discovery reaches a well-connected Greek East settlement long before it plausibly reaches an isolated Gallic Frontier outpost, exactly mirroring how real ancient knowledge actually propagated unevenly rather than uniformly.

---

## 9. Discovery Races — Competing for the Same Breakthrough

New this pass, resolving a real, flagged gap: two households, or a household and a rival Institution, can pursue the same target Discovery simultaneously, each running their own independent §6 effort against it. Whoever completes it first claims the full Household Innovation edge (§7); the loser's own investment converts into the same partial, permanent head start §6.4 already grants a failed attempt — a real loss, but never a total waste, and a genuinely dramatic, legible new front for Rival Houses' own existing competitive machinery to operate on.

---

## 10. Theft — Stealing a Rival's Edge

A rival's exclusive Household Innovation edge (§7) is a real, concrete Espionage target, resolved as a new, specific Scheme type through that document's own existing engine (Characters §10): a successful theft doesn't remove the original household's own access, but grants the thief a parallel, independent exclusive edge of their own — a real, felt loss of exclusivity for the victim without the more punishing "and now you have nothing" outcome, keeping the consequence proportionate to what was actually taken.

---

## 11. Lost Knowledge — When a Discovery Can Be Un-Made

The single most thematically resonant addition this pass makes, and a direct, deliberate extension of Books & Manuscripts' own Lost Work mechanic (§8.2 of that document) into this system: a Discovery whose only surviving detailed knowledge exists in a single Work or a single household's own closely-guarded practice can, in principle, be **lost again** if that Work is destroyed (per Books & Manuscripts' own Lost Work rules) and no other household ever independently rediscovers or publishes it. This isn't a punitive mechanic hunting for a chance to erase player progress — it's a rare, genuinely evocative honest possibility, mirroring the real, well-documented historical fact that some Roman engineering and administrative knowledge really did fade for generations after the Western Empire's own decline before being independently rediscovered or reconstructed far later. Once a Discovery has genuinely spread and "worldAdopted" status is reached across a broad enough footprint, this risk effectively disappears — Lost Knowledge is a real threat only to a still-narrow, still-exclusive, or still-freshly-published Discovery, never to something the wider world has already thoroughly absorbed.

## 13. Extending the Game — A New End Date

The game's own hard end date extends to **AD 565**, the real, historically famous death of Justinian I — a genuine, meaningful, widely recognized closing point for Late Antiquity, and the natural terminus for Era VI's own content.

### 13.1 Era V's Own Regional Variance, Resolved

Per this pass's own resolution of the first draft's flagged question: Era V's own instability flavor severity reads directly against a region's existing **Stability Legacy** — the same real regional-stability framing Policies & Edicts and Natural Disasters already apply elsewhere — rather than landing identically everywhere. A household seated somewhere with a plausibly stronger, more historically durable administrative core reads Era V's own strain more lightly than one in a region genuinely more exposed to the real historical pressures of the age.

### 13.2 The Honest Content Gap

Events: Historical Timeline's own dense, real, dated-event pool covers 133 BC–AD 235 with real historical density, and does not yet cover AD 235–565 with anything like the same depth — flagged here as real, necessary future work for that document, while this document's own Discovery content supplies fresh, structured material to keep the extended range from feeling empty in the meantime.

## 14. Cross-System Integration

- **Events: Historical Timeline:** the new AD 565 end date and the real, flagged AD 235–565 content gap (§13.2) are this document's single largest structural implication for that document's own future work.
- **Books & Manuscripts:** Authorship is the direct mechanism behind an Individual Genius Discovery (§6.2) and publishing one (§7.1); Lost Work is the direct mechanism behind Lost Knowledge (§11).
- **Education & Culture:** Institutions of Renown and Literary Patronage are directly reused for Institutional Patronage (§6.1); Renown Attracts Renown is a direct payoff for publishing.
- **Cultures of the Known World:** Cultural Drift is both the natural regional-variation lever (§6.3) and the literal diffusion mechanism (§8).
- **Espionage / Characters:** theft (§10) is a new, specific Scheme type running on that document's own existing engine.
- **Rival Houses:** Discovery Races (§9) are a genuine new competitive front for that document's own machinery.
- **Public Works & Euergetism:** donating a Discovery is a real civic-generosity option.
- **Celebrities & Influential Figures / Dynasty Chronicle:** every Major Discovery, every publication, and every Discovery Race outcome is real, guaranteed-weight material.
- **Legal & Court:** the *Corpus Juris Civilis* is a real Administrative & Legal capstone.
- **Military & Combat:** cataphract heavy cavalry and Greek Fire are real Military-category content.
- **Monuments & Legacy Building:** the Hagia Sophia-caliber dome is a real Engineering & Construction capstone.
- **Economy & Finance:** the Era III currency reform is a direct, real counter to Debasement.
- **Private Infrastructure / Correspondence & Letters:** the matured aqueduct tier and the *cursus publicus* are real Engineering and Administrative-category content.
- **Policies & Edicts / Natural Disasters:** Stability Legacy (§13.1) is directly reused rather than reinvented.
- **Religion:** explicitly kept parallel — that document's own real, dated Christianity-adoption timeline continues on its own established schedule.

## 16. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per convention — Discovery cost/duration by tier, failure-attempt head-start magnitude, diffusion speed, and Lost Knowledge's own real trigger threshold are all unsized.
- **Whether a Discovery Race (§9) should ever be visible to both competing households in advance**, or whether a household only learns it's racing once a rival's own effort is somehow discovered — left open.
- **The exact Lost Knowledge threshold** for when a Discovery is considered safely "worldAdopted" enough that §11's own risk stops applying — a real, necessary balancing number this document deliberately leaves unset.
- **Whether Prerequisites (§5) should ever be discoverable out of order** through sheer luck (an Individual Genius stumbling onto a later Discovery without the expected earlier step) — this document assumes no, but doesn't hard-rule it out for a future revision.
