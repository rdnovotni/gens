# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — The Bosporan Kingdom
*The twelfth extensible-slate region, and, per direction, the last in this current series. Fills in the Region Profile schema (§4) for a region carrying a genuinely distinct fourth relationship to Rome — not Provincial, not Independent, not a Contested Buffer, but a real, stable **Client** kingdom that never converts to full annexation at any point across this game's entire 368-year range, the only such case on the whole roster. Where Nubia and Arabia Felix both needed to build their premise almost entirely outside Roman categories, the Bosporan Kingdom's own royal house was, by real historical practice, already blended — Greek, Scythian, and Roman-patronized all at once.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — The Permanent Client

Every other client-status culture on this roster eventually converts to full Roman provincial administration within this game's own range: Judaea (AD 6), Egypt (30 BC), Cappadocia (AD 17), Thrace (AD 46), Mauretania (AD 44), Nabataea (AD 106). The Bosporan Kingdom, real and continuously tracked as a Client culture in Cultures of the Known World, never does. It remains a real, stable, Roman-backed client kingdom for the entire 133 BC – AD 235 span this game covers — the single longest-running unconverted client relationship this project has modeled, and a genuinely distinct fourth shape alongside Nubia's Independent kingdom, Armenia's Contested Buffer, and Mesopotamia's brief, lost province.

This document's own real structural gift is that it doesn't need Nubia's or Arabia Felix's own stark "native versus foreign" household split. Real, documented Bosporan royal practice had its client kings actually take Roman imperial names as part of their own royal titulature — a real, visible, self-chosen blending of Greek civic heritage, Scythian steppe connection, and Roman patronage into one identity, not three separate options a household has to pick between. A household here is, by the region's own real historical default, already all three at once.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Real terrain around the Cimmerian Bosporus (the modern Kerch Strait) connecting the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov — genuinely fertile grain-growing land on both sides of the strait, a real, ancient basis for this region's own grain-export identity (§8), alongside real steppe terrain further inland where the kingdom's own territory meets the wider Sarmatian/Scythian world (§5).

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* A real, genuinely old trade economy — Bosporan grain exports predate this game's own range by centuries, historically significant enough to the wider Greek world that Athens itself once depended on Bosporan grain shipments. By this game's own era, Rome's own grain dependency runs primarily through Egypt, Sicily, and North Africa (per those documents' own established content), so this document treats Bosporan grain as a real, genuine regional export rather than claiming it as a Rome-critical supply the way those three regions' own documents do — an honest, non-competing distinction.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture — A Throne That Took Roman Names

This document doesn't attempt to describe the Bosporan Kingdom's own governance in ordinary citizen/Latin-rights/Peregrine terms, the same honesty Armenia's, Nubia's, and Arabia Felix's own documents already extend to their own regions — there is no direct Roman provincial administration here, at any point across the range, for those categories to describe. What this region has instead is real and distinctive: a client king's own throne, its legitimacy resting on real, ongoing Roman recognition and support, generation after generation, without Rome ever needing to administer the territory directly. The real historical practice of Bosporan kings adopting Roman imperial names as part of their own royal titulature is this document's own clearest illustration of what a stable, long-term client relationship actually looked like day to day — not tribute extracted by force, but a real, mutually convenient arrangement neither side had reason to disturb.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — A Real Frontier That Isn't Rome's Own

A genuine structural first for this roster: the Bosporan Kingdom borders the real, already-tracked Sarmatian/Scythian Frontier culture (Cultures of the Known World §8) directly — but this isn't Rome's own frontier the way the Rhine or the Danube is. It's the client king's own frontier, managed by the Bosporan court itself, with Roman backing available at a distance rather than Roman legions standing directly on the line. This gives a household here a real, live, adversarial-or-cooperative relationship to manage using Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples' own existing Frontier toolkit (§6–7 of that document) — the same mechanics Gallic Frontier's own document already puts to use, simply reframed as a kingdom's own diplomacy rather than an empire's. Real, documented intermarriage between Bosporan Greek elites and Sarmatian steppe nobility gives this relationship a genuine cooperative dimension alongside its adversarial one, distinct from Gaul's own more uniformly tense Germanic relationship.

---

## 6. Reputation Duality — Reusing an Existing System Rather Than Inventing a New One

Consistent with this project's own growing preference for reuse over invention (Syria's Localized shape reused by the Balkans; Nubia's Royal Seat Role reused by Armenia, Mesopotamia, and Arabia Felix in turn), this document makes a deliberate choice not to invent a new mechanic here. This region's relationship with Rome is genuinely low-friction — a stable, mutually convenient client arrangement with no live local-standing tension worth tracking, the same honest "None" finding Anatolia, Sicily, and the Alpine Provinces all reached for more ordinary reasons. What is genuinely live here is §5's own Frontier relationship with the Sarmatian/Scythian steppe — and that doesn't need a bespoke Bosporan mechanic either, because Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples' existing Frontier toolkit already covers exactly this shape of relationship. This document's own real contribution isn't a new system; it's recognizing that an existing one already fits.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

Already tracked directly in Religions of the Known World: the real Bosporan Religious Blend, "a real, genuine blend of Hellenic colonial religious practice... with Scythian/Sarmatian steppe influence, a distinct third flavor from either parent tradition alone." This document inherits that entry rather than redefining it — the clearest possible confirmation that this region's own religious identity already reflects the same three-way blend (Greek, steppe, and, by extension of the client relationship, Roman-adjacent) that §1 and §4 describe politically.

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

Grain (§3) is this region's own oldest and most historically significant export. Salted fish, a real, historically attested Sea of Azov industry, gives this region a garum-adjacent trade identity distinct from any other region's own fish-based goods. Real horse trade, plausible given this region's own direct steppe proximity and the real Sarmatian reputation for exceptional cavalry mounts and cataphract warfare, rounds out a goods profile built on this region's own genuine position straddling the Greek maritime world and the steppe.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Three houses, consistent with the precedent every genuinely exceptional region on this roster has set.

- **The household of Tiberius Julius Sauromates** *(seated at Panticapaeum)* — a royal-line noble house carrying the real, recurring naming pattern several actual historical Bosporan client kings used, the same safe-because-recurring naming logic already applied to the Balkans' household of Cotys and Nubia's household of Amanikhalika — this document's own clearest illustration of §1's own already-blended royal identity.
- **The household of Radamsades** *(seated near Tanais)* — a noble house with real, close Sarmatian steppe ties through intermarriage (§5, §9), representing this kingdom's own cooperative frontier dimension rather than its Hellenic-royal one.
- **Gens Fabia** *(seated at Phanagoria)* — a resident Roman/Pontic trade family managing grain and fish-export interests, this region's own modest, real foreign-merchant presence.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 Mithridates VI's Death at Panticapaeum (63 BC, closed history by default)

A real, dramatic conclusion to the Mithridatic Wars already named in Greek East's own document (Starting Regions: Greek East §14.2) and complemented by Anatolia's own "from Pontus's side" telling (Starting Regions: Anatolia / Asia Minor §15.2) — this document adds a genuine third perspective, the war's actual endpoint: Mithridates, using the Bosporan Kingdom as his own final refuge, died at Panticapaeum itself, betrayed by his own son. Three regions, three honest, complementary angles on one real war, none of them duplicating the others.

### 15.2 The Establishment of the Stable Client Dynasty (post-63 BC, closed history by default)

Following Mithridates's own death, Rome backed the establishment of the real, lasting client-king line that would govern the Bosporan Kingdom for the remainder of this game's own range — the direct real transition from "a rebel's last refuge" to "Rome's most stable, longest-unconverted client relationship" (§1).

### 15.3 Rising Steppe Pressure (a gradual trend across the range's own later stretch, not a single date)

A third application of the gradual-trend Timeline Hook type this project's own Nubia and Arabia Felix documents already established (Starting Regions: Nubia §15.2, Starting Regions: Arabia Felix §15.2): real historical pressure from Sarmatian and, later, Gothic movements against the Bosporan Kingdom's own frontier genuinely increased across the later stretch of this game's range. A household starting early experiences §5's own frontier relationship at its most stable and cooperative; a household starting in the later decades experiences a genuinely harder, more contested version of the same relationship.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** the seventh region built entirely outside the framework's original candidate list, following Sicily, the Alpine Provinces, Armenia, Mesopotamia, Nubia, and Arabia Felix — and the first built around a genuine fourth Rome-relationship shape: a permanent, unconverted Client kingdom.
- **Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples:** §5 and §6 together are this document's own clearest illustration of reuse over invention — an existing Frontier relationship, simply reframed as a client kingdom's own diplomacy rather than Rome's.
- **Religions of the Known World:** §7 inherits the Bosporan Religious Blend entry directly.
- **Greek East, Anatolia:** §15.1 completes a three-region complementary telling of the Mithridatic Wars, alongside those two documents' own earlier contributions.
- **Nubia, Arabia Felix:** §15.3 is this project's third application of the gradual-trend Timeline Hook type, confirming it as a genuinely established, reusable pattern rather than a one-off or even a two-off.
- **Rival Houses:** three named, region-seated houses (§11), including this roster's clearest illustration yet of a household identity that's genuinely blended from the outset rather than choosing between two or three separate options.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14 and this region's own grain/fish/horse output are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether "Permanent Client" (§17) should generalize as a category beyond the Bosporan Kingdom specifically** — no other real culture on this roster shares its exact never-converts profile, but a future addition could plausibly reuse this document's own structural thinking if one emerges.
- **The household of Radamsades's own long-term relationship to the more Hellenic-royal household of Tiberius Julius Sauromates.** This document names both as real, legitimate flavors of this kingdom's own blended identity but doesn't specify whether they read as allies, rivals, or something in between by default.
- **Whether real, intermarried Sarmatian-Bosporan elite identity (§5, §9, §11) needs its own Cultural Drift treatment** distinct from Education & Culture's existing default model — this document names the real historical pattern but doesn't propose new mechanics to capture it.
- **This closes the current run of exceptional, outside-the-original-slate regions at direction's own instruction.** Whether and when this project returns to build further regions — Sicily-and-Alpine-Provinces-style straightforward additions, further Nubia-style independent kingdoms, or something else entirely — is a genuinely open question this document doesn't presume to answer.
