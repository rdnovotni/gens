# Glossary and Appendices

This chapter collects the recurring terms you'll see across every other part of the manual in one alphabetical reference, followed by a sense of just how much authored content sits behind the game, and a pointer to where to go if you want more depth than a player's manual is meant to provide.

## Glossary

**Affectio Maritalis** — the genuine, ongoing intent to be married that Roman law actually treated as the substance of a marriage, rather than any single ceremony or document. In practice, it's the felt, living state of a marriage in play — separate from whatever formal arrangements surround it, and something a household's own choices can strengthen or quietly erode over time.

**Autonomy Level** — how much independent initiative a companion, officer, or household member is allowed to take on your behalf without waiting for direct orders. A high-autonomy appointee acts on your household's interests as they read the situation; a low-autonomy one waits to be told, for better or worse.

**Clientela** — the patron-client relationship structure underlying much of Roman social and political life: a network of obligation and support running between a patron and their dependents that shapes everything from a morning greeting to a vote in the Curia.

**Cursus Honorum** — the traditional ladder of public offices a Roman political career climbs, rung by rung, toward the Senate and, ultimately, the consulship. How quickly and how far a household can climb it depends heavily on where that household actually starts.

**Dignitas** — public standing and prestige, the game's primary reputation statistic. Dignitas is what your household actually spends and risks when it pursues office, patronage, marriage alliances, or public honor.

**Divine Favor** — a household's standing with its patron deity and the wider gods, built through worship, sacrifice, and pious behavior, and spent (or lost) in the form of good or ill omens that color events touching the household.

**Familia** — the full household in the Roman sense: blood family, slaves, freedmen, and clients together under one roof and one authority, not merely a family tree.

**Fidelity Tier** — how deeply a given non-player character is actually simulated: a small number of people around your household are fully tracked "Living World Actors" with their own ongoing goals and decisions, while the wider population is modeled more lightly, in aggregate, so the game's attention scales with what actually matters to your story rather than trying to fully simulate everyone at once.

**Gens** — a Roman clan or family line, the game's own title and its basic unit of long-term progress across generations.

**Household Doctrine** — a standing ideological identity a household can adopt — the Old Blood's traditionalism, the Mercantile Dynasty's trade focus, the Military Aristocracy's martial bent, and others — that colors which policies fit naturally and which capstone achievement that identity ultimately builds toward.

**Legal Status** — where a person stands in Roman law: citizen, holder of Latin Rights, or Peregrine (a free non-citizen). Legal Status shapes what a character can do, own, and become, and it varies sharply from one starting region to another.

**Living World Actor** — see Fidelity Tier.

**Loyalty** — how reliably a household member, officer, or client will actually follow through on what you ask of them, distinct from their raw skill or their personal ambitions. A loyal but incompetent servant is a different problem than a brilliant but unreliable one.

**Manumission** — the formal act of freeing a slave, one of the most consequential and most permanent decisions a household head can make about another person's life.

**Pater/Materfamilias** — the legal and social head of the household: the character you actually play.

**Patria Potestas** — a household head's sweeping formal legal authority over descendants, extending to approving or forbidding marriages, disowning an heir, and, in the harshest historical circumstances the game is willing to portray honestly, life-and-death authority itself.

**Reputation Duality** — the mechanic tracking a frontier or recently-conquered household's standing with Rome and its standing with the local, non-Roman population as two genuinely separate, sometimes opposed, values. See the World Atlas chapter for how it varies from region to region.

**Rival House** — a competing gens pursuing land, office, marriage, and prestige on its own initiative, whether or not your household ever directly engages with it. Rival houses are generated both by hand, seeded into a region's own starting political landscape, and procedurally, to keep the wider political field alive across a long playthrough.

**Scheme** — a covert action taken against another person or household — sabotage, blackmail, seduction, assassination, or any of several other quiet moves — carrying real risk of failure or exposure alongside its potential reward.

**Vestal** — one of Rome's state hearth-priestesses, an institution centered specifically on Rome itself; a household's daughter being considered for Vestal service is a genuine mark of prestige that comes bundled with decades of real personal sacrifice.

**Vicus** — a village-scale settlement, the intermediate stage of growth between a single villa and a full town or city.

## The Scale of the World

*Gens* draws on an unusually large body of authored content behind the systems you'll actually touch in play. None of it is meant to be exhaustively memorized — most of it exists so that the world feels genuinely full rather than thinly painted — but it's worth knowing roughly how much is actually there:

| Category | Count |
|---|---|
| Starting Regions | 18 (6 launch, 5 promoted, 7 further individual regions) |
| Cultures | 35 named peoples, plus the full Roman pantheon of patron deities |
| Religions & Faiths | 27 named traditions |
| Building types | 94 |
| Court & companion positions | 55 |
| Tradeable goods | 144 |
| Named character traits | 219 |
| Named occupations | 119 |
| Named plants | 105 |
| Named creatures | 60 non-legendary, plus 17 confirmed legendary creatures |
| Named technologies & discoveries | 94, across six historical eras |
| Named historical figures | 63 |
| Dated historical events | 121, spanning 133 BC to AD 565 |

That content sits on top of 110 individual system design documents, totaling roughly 384,000 words — the working design corpus this manual is condensed from.

## Where to Go Deeper

Everything in this manual is a deliberate synthesis: a player-facing condensation of a much larger, much more detailed design corpus, written to get you playing and enjoying the game without requiring you to read the engineering behind it. If you want the full mechanical depth behind any system, region, culture, or number mentioned here, the [game design index](../design/README.md) is the complete map of all 110 underlying design documents. Because a corpus that large inevitably contains documents written at different times that describe the same shared concept slightly differently, the [Design Authority Registry](../gens-design-authority-registry.md) is the single reconciling source: it resolves those overlaps down to one authoritative document per concept, so if you ever find two design documents seeming to disagree, that registry — not this manual — is where the real answer lives.
