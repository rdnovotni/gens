# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Iberian Colony
*Final polish and balance pass. Fills in the Starting Regions framework's Region Profile schema (§4) for a single unified region that nonetheless carries real internal contrast — a demilitarized, senatorial south (Baetica) against a more recently pacified, garrisoned north (Tarraconensis) — and gives the framework's own "tapering" Reputation Duality call (Starting Regions §6) its first concrete mechanical shape, keyed to the real, dateable close of the Cantabrian Wars. This pass adds a worked example (§6.1) showing that mechanic's own tapering in practice, softens a claim that overstated Legal & Court's existing content, and makes explicit the one-flagship-Provincial-Seat-per-region convention this document shares with Gallic Frontier. Intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — A Region With Two Roman Provinces, One Colonial Character

Iberia carries a real internal contrast worth naming clearly before anything else: **Baetica**, the south, was administered as a *senatorial* province — governed by a proconsul answering to the Senate rather than the Emperor directly, and, remarkably for a non-Italian province, genuinely garrisoned by no standing legion at all by this game's own era. **Tarraconensis**, covering the east coast, the interior, and the real Cantabrian conquest zone in the north, remained an *imperial* province throughout — governed by the Emperor's own legate, and home to this region's real standing legionary presence.

Unlike Italy or Gaul, this document doesn't split that contrast into two selectable regions — Iberian and North African were already established as the launch roster's own division of this broader geography (Starting Regions §5.1), and a further Baetica/Tarraconensis split would multiply the roster further than this project currently wants. Instead, this document keeps that real internal contrast alive *within* one region, the same way Gallic Frontier kept Narbonensis and the Three Gauls together under one identity: the calm, wealthy, senatorial south and the more recently pacified, garrisoned north both belong here, and a player's own choices — where the Home Anchor sits (§12), which Gazetteer entries a household actually engages with — determine which side of that contrast a given playthrough leans into.

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

Genuinely rich hill and mountain terrain (the Sierra Morena, running along Baetica's own northern edge) carries real, substantial mineral deposits — the actual historical basis of this region's own signature Gold and Silver goods (§8), among the richest metal-bearing terrain in the entire ancient Mediterranean world. The Baetis river valley (the real ancient name for the Guadalquivir) cuts through genuinely fertile agricultural land, the actual historical basis for Baetica's own real fame as a grain and olive oil exporter (§8). Coastal terrain exists on two real, distinct fronts — a Mediterranean coast in the east and south, and a real Atlantic coast in the west — echoing Gallic Frontier's own dual-coast profile, though less central to this region's own identity than its interior mineral and agricultural wealth.

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Consistent with the core doc's own "strong trade and resource extraction... moderate risk, colonial-administration flavor," this region sits between the Italian Heartland's expensive maturity and the Gallic Frontier's raw cheapness: land here is genuinely more affordable than Italy's, but this region's own mining and agricultural wealth means it was never a bargain the way frontier Gaul is. Baetica specifically supports genuinely deep, liquid markets — a real, wealthy, thoroughly settled economy rather than a frontier one — while the Tarraconensis interior and the Cantabrian north carry a rawer, thinner-market character closer to Gallic Frontier's own.

**A real, deliberate weight worth naming directly, consistent with this project's own commitment to frank historical treatment of slavery:** Roman mining was genuinely brutal labor, and this region's own real historical mines were worked substantially by slaves and, in a real, specific legal practice, by condemned convicts under the sentence *damnatio ad metalla* — "condemnation to the mines," an actual, severe category of Roman criminal punishment, and a natural, historically grounded addition to Legal & Court's own sentencing categories if that document doesn't already include it. A household whose wealth runs through Gold Ore or Silver Ore production here is drawing that wealth from a real, historically documented site of some of the harshest labor conditions in the ancient world — Labor & Slavery's own discipline and labor-assignment mechanics should read as genuinely harsher and higher-stakes when applied to this region's own mining operations than to an ordinary agricultural estate elsewhere.

## 4. Political & Legal Texture — Senatorial Peace, Imperial Garrison

Baetica's own real senatorial-province status means a household seated there answers to different administrative machinery than one in Tarraconensis — a proconsul rather than an imperial legate — a genuine, concrete Legal & Court and Politics & Patronage administrative distinction worth keeping honest rather than treating "the Iberian colony" as one flat governance model. Baetica's own real political ceiling, worth stating directly and specifically because it upends the easy assumption that a "colony" necessarily means a lower prestige ceiling than Italy: this is a region with a genuine, real, historically attested path to the very top of Roman power. Cordoba-and-region families genuinely did reach the Senate, and, most strikingly, a real Gades-born man — Lucius Cornelius Balbus — is a real, historically documented case of a colonial-born individual reaching the consulship itself, the same real ceiling a Latium-born household spends its entire cursus honorum chasing (Politics & Patronage §6). This region's own rival houses (§11) are built to make that real ceiling feel present rather than theoretical.

Tarraconensis's own Legal Status mix runs rawer — genuinely closer to Gallic Frontier's own texture (§4 of that document), with citizen, Latin Rights, and Peregrine status all realistically present, especially in the more recently pacified Cantabrian north.

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — No External Neighbor, an Internal Line

A structural point worth stating clearly, since it's a genuine, deliberate contrast with Gallic Frontier: **Iberia has no standing external Frontier neighbor.** It's a peninsula, bounded by ocean on three sides and the Pyrenees on the fourth — once the Cantabrian Wars close the conquest (§6, §15), there is no further "beyond the border" people left to negotiate with, raid, or ally against Rome. Diplomacy with Non-Roman Peoples simply has nothing external to anchor to here, a genuine structural difference from every region that borders a real, ongoing frontier.

What this region has instead is an **internal military line**, per §1: Tarraconensis carries this region's own real standing legionary garrison (historically, Legio VII Gemina, based at the real fortress-town this document's Gazetteer names Legio — modern León, literally named for the legion once stationed there), while Baetica carries none at all. This gives Military & Combat a genuine recruitment-and-security texture that varies by which half of the region a household actually sits in, without needing an external enemy to justify it.

## 6. Reputation Duality — Tapering by Conquest Timeline

The framework document names Iberian Colony as a **tapering** Reputation Duality region rather than Gallic Frontier's **full** implementation (Starting Regions §6), and this document gives that taper a concrete mechanical shape keyed to the real, dateable Cantabrian Wars (29–19 BC):

- **Active Conquest** *(a playthrough starting before 19 BC)* — Reputation Duality functions exactly as it does in Gallic Frontier: standing with Rome and local standing with the region's own Iberian population are two genuinely independent, actively diverging axes.
- **Recently Pacified** *(a playthrough starting in the decades following 19 BC)* — the two axes remain tracked, but local standing's own swing is measurably smaller and slower-moving than in an active-conquest start; a household can still meaningfully win or lose local trust, but the ceiling and floor both sit closer to the center than they did during the war itself.
- **Long-Settled** *(a playthrough starting a full generation or more after 19 BC)* — Reputation Duality effectively converges to a single Dignitas value, functionally identical to the Italian Heartland's own "none" designation (Starting Regions §6), reflecting a population that has, by this point, substantially settled into ordinary Provincial life. This convergence is described here as gradual and substantial rather than absolute — Baetica in particular should read as further along this convergence than Tarraconensis's own more recently pacified north, consistent with §1's internal contrast.

This gives a future Start Modes or Scenario Starts pass a clean, three-position dial to read from, rather than a binary on/off switch that would flatten a genuinely gradual real historical process into a single toggle.

### 6.1 A Worked Example — The Same Choice, Two Different Weights

Consider a household pursuing an **Exploitative** Provincial Administration Posture (Policies & Edicts §2.10) alongside **Aggressive** mining production (§3). In an **Active Conquest** playthrough, that choice reads exactly as it would in Gallic Frontier (Starting Regions: Gallic Frontier §15): real, steady local-standing erosion, a real and immediate cost to standing with the household of Segontius (§11) and the wider Iberian population, set against real gains in Dignitas-with-Rome. In a **Long-Settled** playthrough, the identical policy choice produces almost no local-standing movement at all — the same extraction, the same mining conditions, but a population far enough from active conquest that it no longer reads the choice as a live political act one way or the other. The household's own behavior hasn't changed; what's changed is how much the region's own population is still watching. This is the concrete, felt difference between "tapering" and "full" Reputation Duality that §6's own three-position dial exists to produce.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults

Iberian religious tradition (per Cultures of the Known World) is a real, blended local practice, with the historically attested Lusitanian healing deity Endovelicus already named in Religions of the Known World as a specific, concrete rather than generically "Iberian paganism" detail. This region also carries a genuine, real Punic cultural legacy of its own — Gades and Carthago Nova (§10) were both real Phoenician/Carthaginian foundations before Rome ever arrived, giving Iberian Colony a residual Punic thread that deliberately echoes North African Colony's own, since Carthage's real historical reach spanned both sides of the strait alike. A small, real Greek colonial pocket exists too, distinct from Massilia's own larger Gallic-adjacent Hellenic presence — Emporion (§10), a genuine, if minor, Greek trading foundation on the northeast coast.

---

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

Gold and Silver (Sierra Morena and the real mines around Carthago Nova), Cinnabar (feeding Pigments), Esparto Grass (feeding Linen Works and the region's own signature Esparto-variant Sandals), and Tyrian Purple (Murex, shared with the Greek East's own coastal tradition) are this region's already-established signature goods (Resources & Goods §5/§7). Two further real, concrete details worth naming directly: **Baetican olive oil** was exported to Rome in genuinely staggering quantities — real enough that the broken amphorae that once carried it literally built Monte Testaccio, an artificial hill still standing in Rome today, made almost entirely of discarded Baetican oil containers. And **Gades' own Garum Sociorum** was a real, historically famous premium garum brand, widely regarded as the finest in the Roman world — the luxury-tier expression Resources & Goods' own "Iberian" Garum tag (§7 of that document) points to, distinct from and a real notch above Campania's own more workaday Pompeian production (Starting Regions: Italian Heartland §4.6) — both real, both true, simply different tiers of the same good.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, spanning this region's own real senatorial-to-native range.

- **Gens Annia** *(seated at Corduba)* — an old Baetican mining-and-olive-oil fortune with a genuine, live senatorial ambition, consciously measuring itself against the real historical precedent of Baetican families who actually reached Rome's highest offices (§4). The region's own clearest illustration that "colonial" doesn't mean "capped."
- **Gens Balbina** *(seated at Gades)* — a Punic-heritage trade family, its own name a deliberate echo of the real historical Balbus of Gades (§4, §10) without being that individual — built on the same real Garum Sociorum and maritime trade wealth that made his own rise possible in the first place.
- **Gens Terentia** *(seated at Legio)* — a garrison-connected military family in Tarraconensis's own more recently pacified north, structurally the same archetype as Gallic Frontier's Gens Fulvia, but here justified by §1's own internal north/south contrast rather than an external frontier.
- **The household of Segontius** *(seated near Numantia, in the Cantabrian-adjacent interior)* — deliberately given a native Celtiberian-rooted name rather than a Roman one, echoing Gallic Frontier's own household of Cintugnatus: a still-largely-Peregrine local family whose own favor is this region's single clearest illustration of §6's local-standing axis, especially sharp for a playthrough still in the Active Conquest or Recently Pacified window.

## 12. Home Anchor

**In the fertile Baetis valley, within real reach of the Sierra Morena's own mining wealth.** This anchors the household in Baetica's own prosperous, senatorial-province identity — the region's own default character, per the core doc's "moderate risk" framing — while keeping §3's own real, harder truth about mining labor close enough to feel present rather than abstract, the same way Campania's own Home Anchor keeps Vesuvius close rather than comfortable.

---

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Every one of Core §5.2's four Templated Background archetypes already has a primary claim elsewhere on the roster (Latium: impoverished patrician; Campania: jumped-up equestrian merchant; Gallic Frontier: veteran given a land grant and provincial notable). Rather than compete for primacy, Iberian Colony offers real, distinct **secondary** expressions of two of them: Gens Annia and Gens Balbina (§11) give **"jumped-up equestrian merchant"** a genuine mining-and-garum-wealth flavor, distinct from Campania's own shipping-and-trade version; and Balbus of Gades's own real historical precedent (§4) gives **"provincial notable"** an economic-power variant, distinct from Gallic Frontier's own native-aristocracy-straddling version (Gens Iulia Turona). Both are real, legitimate alternate readings of an archetype this document doesn't claim as its own primary home.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 The Numantine War (143–133 BC, closed history by default)

A real, historically significant final resistance in the Iberian interior, ending in the same year this game's own range opens — effectively closed history for any default-era playthrough, but the direct real grounding behind Numantia's own Gazetteer entry (§10) and its Dynasty Chronicle-flavor weight.

### 15.2 The Sertorian War (80–72 BC, era-conditional)

Genuinely distinct in character from every other region's own Timeline Hook so far: a real Roman civil-war-adjacent conflict fought substantially on Iberian soil, where a breakaway Roman general held and governed much of the peninsula independently for years before the conflict's real resolution. Era-conditional — only live for a playthrough starting early enough to reach it — and a rare case where this region's own drama comes from Rome fracturing against itself on Iberian ground, not from a native uprising or an imperial policy.

### 15.3 The Cantabrian Wars (29–19 BC)

The defining event of this entire document: the real, final conquest of the Iberian peninsula, and the direct mechanical hinge §6's own Conquest Timeline Position dial turns on. Unlike §15.1 and §15.2, this hook isn't purely flavor — it's the actual historical event this document's central regional mechanic is built around.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** fulfills the Region Profile schema (§4) for Iberian Colony, and gives that document's own "tapering" Reputation Duality call (§6) its first concrete three-position mechanical shape, with a worked example (§6.1) illustrating the taper directly.
- **Politics & Patronage:** §4's own Balbus-of-Gades precedent and Gens Annia's senatorial ambition give the cursus honorum (§6 of that document) a genuine, non-Italian path to its own highest tier.
- **Legal & Court:** §3's own *damnatio ad metalla* detail is a real, historically grounded sentencing category that document can adopt directly if it hasn't already; §4's senatorial-vs-imperial administrative split is a genuine case study for that system's own provincial-governance texture.
- **Labor & Slavery:** §3's mining-labor note is this document's own direct, frank extension of that system's discipline and labor-assignment mechanics into a genuinely harsher real historical context.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 specifies that document's existing Iberian tags (Gold, Silver, Cinnabar, Esparto Grass, Tyrian Purple, Garum) and resolves the apparent Garum tension with Campania's own claim by establishing both as real, coexisting, differently-tiered production centers.
- **Religion, Religions of the Known World:** Endovelicus (§7) is inherited directly rather than redefined; the region's own Punic thread deliberately parallels North African Colony's own.
- **Military & Combat:** §5's internal senatorial/imperial military line gives that system a genuine within-region security texture distinct from any external-frontier model.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), deliberately spanning from a still-Peregrine native household to a family with a real historical path to the consulship.
- **Events:** §15's three Timeline Hooks include this project's first "Rome fractures against itself" flashpoint (the Sertorian War), distinct in kind from every prior region's own native-revolt or imperial-policy hooks.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** Numantia's own real, tragic weight (§10, §15.1) gives that system a memorial-flavor location in the same register as the Italian Heartland's Alba Longa.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — the Conquest Timeline Position's own local-standing swing multiplier, every Distance Tier in §14, and mining labor's own severity modifiers are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether an earlier-era Start Mode ever actually opens the Sertorian War window**, the same open question the Gallic Frontier document raised for Vercingetorix's revolt — a real, live option only once a future era-range decision makes it reachable.
- **The household of Segontius's own long-term arc**, mirroring the same open question the Gallic Frontier document left for the household of Cintugnatus — deliberately left to a future Familia or Legal & Court pass rather than resolved here.
- **Whether Baetica's own "no standing legion" status should ever carry a distinct Frontier Security Posture default** (Policies & Edicts §2.12) — this document notes the real historical fact but doesn't specify whether Baetica-anchored households should face any mechanical nudge toward a specific Posture as a result.
- **Whether the Conquest Timeline Position's "Long-Settled" convergence should ever fully zero out**, or should always retain a small, permanent residual local-standing texture reflecting Iberia's own persistent Celtiberian/Lusitanian/Basque cultural distinctness (Cultures of the Known World §4) even centuries into settled Provincial life — this document leans toward the latter (§6's own "gradual and substantial rather than absolute" phrasing) but doesn't fully resolve it.
