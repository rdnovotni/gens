# GENS — System Design: Starting Regions — Britannia
*Final polish and balance pass. Moves Britannia from the framework's own candidate list (Starting Regions §5.2) into a fully realized seventh selectable region — the first extensible-slate region to receive its own full document. Fills in the Region Profile schema (§4) for a single unified region that the framework itself already flagged as "the most dramatically volatile frontier candidate" — and this document bears that out directly: Britannia is only the second launch-adjacent region, after Gallic Frontier, to run **full** Reputation Duality, and its own defining historical event, Boudicca's revolt (AD 60–61), is the first regional Timeline Hook on this entire project's roster that's genuinely live under a normal Pax Romana start rather than closed history by default. This pass renamed a rival house that read too close to Cunobelinus, a genuinely famous real pre-conquest British king (the historical basis for Shakespeare's *Cymbeline*), to the household of Togirix instead — safely following the same authentic Celtic *-rix* naming pattern without echoing one specific well-documented figure — disambiguated Isca Silurum from the real, separate Isca Dumnoniorum, and gave that renamed household a second Templated Background reading in the same pass. Intended as the finalized version of this document pending only the project-wide numeric balancing pass every design document in this project defers to the end.*

## 1. Scope & Identity — A Harder, Later Version of the Gallic Frontier's Thesis

The framework document's own extensible-slate note called this correctly before any of its specifics existed: Britannia reads as "a harder, later-arriving version of the Gallic Frontier's own thesis rather than a redundant copy of it" (Starting Regions §5.2). Everything in this document bears that framing out. Conquest starts later here (AD 43, three generations after Caesar's own Gallic Wars) and never actually finishes — Hadrian's Wall (§15.4) isn't a triumphant capstone the way the Cantabrian Wars close out Iberian Colony's own conquest arc; it's a physical, permanent admission that the northern third of the island was never going to be held. And where Gallic Frontier's own population settles into provincial life relatively durably once conquered, Britain's own settled south proves it hadn't actually settled at all: Boudicca's revolt (§15.2) erupts seventeen years *after* the initial conquest, in the supposedly pacified lowland heartland, not at some still-contested frontier edge.

This document keeps Britannia as a single selectable region, consistent with every other multi-province region on this roster, with its own internal contrast running along a real, familiar line: a thoroughly Romanized **Lowland Britain** (the south and east — villas, towns, the eventual provincial capital) against a heavily militarized **Wall Zone** in the north, permanently facing the unconquered Caledonian tribes beyond it.

---

## 2. Terrain & Feature Profile

A genuinely wetter, cooler terrain profile than anywhere else on the roster — real, extensive Forest cover, particularly in the pre-clearance lowlands, and a real, substantial Marsh presence in the east (the Fens region), giving this region a distinct combination Buildings' own terrain gates haven't emphasized together this heavily anywhere else: strong Timber access alongside genuine Reed Bed and Goat Pasture opportunities (Resources & Goods §4). Hill country in the west and southwest carries real mineral wealth — most significantly Cornish tin, the actual, most plausible historical identity behind the ancient "Cassiterides" (Tin Islands) Mediterranean traders sought for centuries before Rome ever set foot here. Coastal terrain is extensive but colder and rougher than any Mediterranean-facing region's own coast, and the Channel crossing itself (§14) carries a real, distinct weather-risk character Travel should read as meaningfully less predictable than an ordinary Mediterranean sea route.

---

## 3. Economic Package

*(Qualitative — numeric packages remain Start Modes' own territory, per the framework's §4.2.)* Land here is genuinely the cheapest on the entire roster, cheaper even than Gallic Frontier's own — this is the newest, rawest, least-developed province any household can start in, full stop. Markets exist at the handful of real towns (§10) and are thin everywhere else. What this region trades cheap land and thin markets for is real, substantial mineral wealth (tin, and real if secondary lead deposits) and a genuinely wide-open settlement frontier — Design Pillar #1's own "no dominant setting" promise taken about as far as this project's launch-and-extensible roster ever pushes it.

---

## 4. Political & Legal Texture

Legal Status here runs rawer than anywhere else on the roster except perhaps the earliest Gallic Frontier timeline — the overwhelming majority of the native population remains Peregrine for a real, substantial portion of this game's own range, with citizen status concentrated almost entirely in the handful of real colonia (§10) and among auxiliary veterans who've completed real, standard terms of service. Politics & Patronage's own cursus honorum is a genuinely distant prospect from here — harder even than Gallic Frontier's own already-difficult path, consistent with the core doc's own general "lower starting prestige, more social mobility" framing for frontier regions, pushed further.

---

## 5. Diplomatic & Military Exposure — Two Neighbors, Three Legions

Britannia carries not one but **two** distinct external cultural relationships, a genuine first on this roster:

- **Caledonian** — the permanently unconquered northern portion of the island itself, per Cultures of the Known World. This is the live, tense, adversarial relationship, anchored at the Wall (§10) and directly comparable in shape to Gallic Frontier's own Germanic relationship, though — per §1 — even more permanently unresolved, since the Rhine eventually functions as a genuinely stable administrative border in a way Hadrian's Wall, built specifically because the frontier *wouldn't* stabilize on its own, never quite manages to.
- **Hibernian** — Ireland, genuinely never conquered and, per Cultures of the Known World, never even seriously attempted beyond Agricola's own recorded consideration of it. This is a distant, low-intensity, essentially peaceful relationship — a real, rare Diplomacy or Travel destination rather than a live threat, giving a Britannia household access to a second, entirely different flavor of "unconquered neighbor" alongside Caledonia's own adversarial one.

Britain's own military footprint is genuinely exceptional for its size: **three full legions** stationed here for most of the Principate — II Augusta, VI Victrix, and XX Valeria Victrix, each with its own real fortress (§10) — a heavier garrison, relative to the province's own population and territory, than almost anywhere else on the roster. Crucially, and distinct from every other region's own military texture, these legions serve a genuine dual purpose: facing the Caledonian frontier to the north, and — after Boudicca (§15.2) — standing ready against the settled province's own population as well. No other region's garrison carries that second job as explicitly.

## 6. Reputation Duality — Full, and Slower to Settle Than Gaul's Own

This document designates Britannia a **full** Reputation Duality region, the same designation Gallic Frontier carries and the second (and, on the current roster, last) region to use it — a deliberate call this document makes directly, since the framework's own §6 table only classified the five launch regions and Britannia, as an extensible-slate addition, needed its own determination.

**Why full rather than tapering:** Iberian and North African Colony both taper because each has a real, dateable moment where the conquest genuinely closes (the Cantabrian Wars; Mauretania's AD 44 annexation) and the local population settles durably afterward. Britain has no equivalent closing moment within this game's own range — Hadrian's Wall (§15.4) doesn't close a conquest, it formalizes giving up on finishing one, and Boudicca's own revolt is direct, dateable proof that even the "settled" lowland population's own loyalty remained genuinely fragile well past the point Gallic Frontier's own population would typically read as pacified. A Britannia household's local standing is a live concern for a longer real stretch of the timeline than a Gallic Frontier household's own, even though both regions share the same underlying mechanic.

## 7. Religious & Cultural Defaults — The Druidic Heartland, Not Just a Province of It

Real ancient sources — Caesar's own *Gallic Wars* commentary among them — describe Britain not merely as another Druidic-tradition region alongside Gaul's own, but as the tradition's actual origin and most sacred heartland, with Gallic Druids reportedly traveling to Britain for the deepest levels of training. This gives Britannia's own religious suppression story (the same real Roman policy Gallic Frontier's own document already establishes, §6 of that document) a sharper, more concrete edge: the real AD 60 Roman assault on Mona (modern Anglesey), the actual druidic stronghold island, is this document's own named Gazetteer anchor (§10) for that suppression — and the real, striking historical detail that this assault happened in the very same year Boudicca's revolt erupted (§15.2) is more than coincidence worth noting: a province stripped of much of its garrison to strike at the religious heartland was a genuinely more vulnerable province at the exact moment its own local standing collapsed.

A further, real, concrete detail of a different character entirely: **Aquae Sulis** (§10), where the native goddess Sulis was genuinely syncretized with Minerva — a real, well-documented, and rather more peaceful illustration of this region's own religious complexity sitting alongside Mona's harsher suppression story.

Correspondence & Letters' own existing note deserves a direct callout here: that document already flags British culture (alongside Gallic and Germanic) as carrying a real, meaningfully reduced Correspondence effectiveness for anything touching druidic-adjacent leadership specifically — a constraint this document inherits rather than redefines, and one that reads with particular weight here given §7's own claim that Britain is the tradition's actual heartland rather than merely another province of it.

## 8. Regional Goods & Trade

**Tin** is this region's own real, primary claim, and a clarification worth making directly: Resources & Goods currently tags Tin to "Gallic/frontier" (§7 of that document), but the real historical trade picture is layered rather than contradictory — Cornish tin, from this region specifically, was the actual, most plausible source behind the ancient "Cassiterides" traders sought for centuries, while Gaul's own role was substantially that of the overland and coastal middleman moving British tin toward the Mediterranean rather than the primary extraction site itself. This document doesn't ask Resources & Goods to change its own tag — Gaul's trade-route role is real too — but names Britain as tin's actual point of origin, the same kind of layered-not-contradictory resolution this project's own documents have already applied to Garum (Iberian Colony vs. Campania) and Tyrian Purple (Iberian Colony vs. Greek East).

Beyond tin, this region's own economic identity leans on real secondary lead deposits, Furs/Pelts (shared with Gallic Frontier's own tag), and the same Reed Bed and Goat Pasture opportunities §2 already notes — a genuinely modest goods profile compared to almost every other region on the roster, consistent with this being the newest, least-developed province a household can start in.

## 11. Rival Seeding

Four houses, spanning this region's own real veteran-colonist-to-native range.

- **Gens Cornelia** *(seated at Camulodunum)* — a veteran-colonist family descended from the real original settlers Camulodunum was founded to house, rebuilding after Boudicca's own real destruction of the town — this region's own clearest illustration that even the supposedly safest, most established Roman settlement here was never actually safe (§1, §15.2).
- **Gens Vitellia** *(seated at Eboracum)* — a garrison-connected family tied to Legio VI Victrix's own long institutional presence, structurally the same archetype as Gallic Frontier's Gens Fulvia, Iberian Colony's Gens Terentia, North African Colony's Gens Petronia, and Greek East's Gens Vinicia — here justified by Britain's own genuinely exceptional three-legion garrison (§5).
- **The household of Togirix** *(seated near Verulamium)* — a native British aristocratic house with a real, plausible claim of descent from pre-conquest British nobility, carrying the real, attested Celtic *-rix* ("king") suffix Cultures of the Known World's own naming conventions already note for British chieftain names, and deliberately given a native name rather than a Roman one, echoing Gallic Frontier's household of Cintugnatus, Iberian Colony's household of Segontius, and North African Colony's household of Massuna — this region's own clearest illustration of §6's own local-standing axis, and, given §1's own emphasis on the lowland south's own fragile loyalty, a household whose own favor matters just as much in the "settled" heartland as at the Wall. Per §13, this household also gives **"provincial notable"** a fifth flavor — a pre-conquest native-nobility claim distinct from every other region's own reading of that same archetype.
- **Gens Agricolana** *(seated at the Wall Zone)* — a frontier military family, its own name a deliberate echo of the real historical Agricola's own Caledonian campaigns (§15.3) without claiming descent from any specific real individual, in the same spirit as Iberian Colony's Gens Balbina — built on real, ongoing service against the Caledonian frontier.

## 12. Home Anchor

**Near Camulodunum**, in the settled lowland southeast. Unlike Gallic Frontier's own choice to anchor at the harsher Rhine frontier for maximum felt tension, this document deliberately anchors the household in what should, by every reasonable measure, be the *safest* place in the entire province — a veteran colonia in the thoroughly pacified south — precisely because Camulodunum's own real history proves that safety was an illusion. This is this region's own version of Campania's Vesuvius-adjacent and North African Colony's Carthage-adjacent Home Anchor choices: leaning into the region's defining historical lesson rather than avoiding it, but inverted — the lesson here isn't "the danger is obviously nearby," it's "the danger was never actually as far away as it looked."

---

## 13. Templated Background Flavor

Every one of Core §5.2's four Templated Background archetypes already carries at least one prior claim elsewhere on the roster. Britannia offers two further readings rather than competing for primacy. Gens Cornelia (§11) gives **"veteran given a land grant"** a second flavor alongside Gallic Frontier's own primary claim — Camulodunum was genuinely, specifically founded as a veteran colonia, arguably an even purer illustration of the archetype's own premise than Gallic Frontier's Gens Fulvia, though this document doesn't contest Gaul's own primacy, established first and reinforced by that region's own broader veteran-colonist texture. The household of Togirix (§11) gives **"provincial notable"** a fifth flavor — a pre-conquest native-nobility claim distinct from Gallic Frontier's straddling aristocrat, Iberian Colony's economic-power variant, North African Colony's native royal-descent claim, and Greek East's cultural-prestige claim.

## 15. Historical Timeline Hooks

### 15.1 Claudius's Invasion (AD 43, closed history by default)

The real, foundational conquest event behind this entire region's own Roman presence — closed history for any default-era playthrough, but the essential backstory behind every Gazetteer entry and every rival house on this document's own roster.

### 15.2 The Assault on Mona and Boudicca's Revolt (AD 60–61, genuinely live under a default start)

The defining event of this entire document, and the first Timeline Hook on this project's roster that isn't closed history under a normal Pax Romana start — falling comfortably within the range most playthroughs would actually reach. Two real, connected events: the Roman assault on the druidic stronghold at Mona (§7, §10), and, erupting the same year, Boudicca's own revolt, sacking Camulodunum and Verulamium (§10) and threatening Roman control of the entire province before its real, eventual suppression. This document's own single highest-weight Divergence-eligible moment, and the direct, concrete proof behind §1's own central claim that this region's conquest was never as settled as it looked.

### 15.3 Agricola's Caledonian Campaigns (c. AD 77–84, era-conditional)

Already named in Cultures of the Known World's own Caledonian entry — real campaigns pushing into Caledonia and winning a real, disputed victory at Mons Graupius, without ever durably holding the territory. Era-conditional on how far into the timeline a given playthrough reaches, and the direct real grounding behind Gens Agricolana's own name (§11).

### 15.4 Hadrian's Wall (c. AD 122)

Unlike every other region's own "closing" hook — the Cantabrian Wars, Mauretania's annexation, both marking a conquest's real completion — this one marks the opposite: a physical, permanent admission that the northern frontier would never be finished. A genuinely distinct flavor of historical resolution, worth reading as this region's own honest answer to what "closing the frontier" actually looked like here.

## 16. Cross-System Integration

- **Starting Regions (framework):** promotes Britannia from the extensible slate (§5.2) to a fully realized seventh region, and makes this document's own determination that it carries full Reputation Duality (§6) — a call the framework's own launch-region table didn't make, since it only covered the original five.
- **Cultures of the Known World:** inherits British, Caledonian, and Hibernian culture entries directly; §5's dual external relationship (Caledonian adversarial, Hibernian distant) is this document's own concrete regional application of both.
- **Correspondence & Letters:** §7 directly inherits that document's own existing British/Gallic/Germanic druidic-correspondence penalty rather than redefining it.
- **Resources & Goods:** §8 resolves an apparent Tin tension the same way Garum and Tyrian Purple were resolved elsewhere — Britain as real point of origin, Gaul's existing tag as the real trade-route role, both true and coexisting.
- **Religion, Religions of the Known World:** Mona (§7, §10) gives this project's own Druidic-suppression theme its sharpest and most concrete illustration anywhere on the roster; Aquae Sulis gives it a real, more peaceful syncretism counterpoint.
- **Military & Combat:** §5's three-legion, dual-purpose garrison (frontier defense and internal control both) is a genuine fifth military-texture model, distinct from every other region's own single-purpose garrison logic.
- **Rival Houses:** four named, region-seated houses (§11), including this roster's first house whose own founding town (Camulodunum) was sacked within this document's own historical timeline.
- **Events:** §15.2 is this entire project's first regional Timeline Hook that's genuinely live rather than closed history under a default start — a real, meaningful escalation in stakes for a Britannia playthrough specifically.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** Camulodunum's own sacked-and-rebuilt history (§10, §11, §15.2) gives that system this document's own equivalent of Alba Longa's, Numantia's, Carthage's, and Corinth's memorial-flavor locations — arguably the most immediate and dangerous of the five, since it's the one still-live threat rather than settled history.

## 18. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — every Distance Tier in §14, the Channel crossing's own weather-risk severity, and this region's own tin/lead output are all left to a future balancing pass.
- **Whether Boudicca's revolt should carry special mechanical weight beyond an ordinary Timeline Hook**, given §15.2's own status as this project's first genuinely live-by-default regional hook — a future Events pass may want to treat this with more structural weight than a purely optional Divergence moment.
- **The household of Togirix's own long-term arc**, mirroring the same open question left for the households of Cintugnatus, Segontius, and Massuna — deliberately left to a future Familia or Legal & Court pass.
- **Whether the Hibernian relationship (§5) ever needs more mechanical depth than a rare Diplomacy/Travel destination**, given how genuinely peaceful and distant it is compared to every other external relationship on the roster — this document treats it as intentionally minor, but a future Diplomacy pass could reasonably expand it.
- **Two simultaneous external Frontier relationships' own interaction**, if any — this document treats Caledonian and Hibernian as fully independent (§5), but doesn't address whether a household's posture toward one should ever meaningfully affect the other.
