## 1. Scope & Role — Cooperation and Competition as Two Poles

Collegia & Guilds already models the cooperative end of this project's own commercial life — a trade guild's own collective bargaining, real historical solidarity among members of the same craft (that document's §5). Notable Businesses already models the competitive end — a named rival, real price undercutting, worker poaching (that document's §5). Neither document names the tension sitting directly between them: a household can be a loyal collegium member *and* a ruthless competitor against a fellow member of that same guild, and Roman economic life genuinely contained both instincts at once, unresolved. This document is where that tension gets real, explicit mechanical shape — the full ladder from ordinary rivalry to real economic ruin, the choice to collude rather than compete, and the one place Rome's own government actually did care about market manipulation: the grain supply.

---

## 2. The Competitive Escalation Ladder

Notable Businesses' own worked example (that document's §5.1) showed the opening moves — a price cut, a poached worker, a damaging rumor. This document extends that into a genuine four-rung ladder, each rung a real, distinct commitment rather than a bigger number:

1. **Ordinary Rivalry** — two Notable Businesses in the same trade, normal Market Dynamics pressure, no deliberate escalation. The default state.
2. **A Price War** — one business deliberately cuts prices below its own comfortable margin specifically to draw customers away from a named competitor, accepting reduced profit as the real cost of the attempt. Genuinely reversible — either side can simply stop.
3. **Predatory Pricing** — a price war sustained deliberately past the point of ordinary profitability, specifically intended to force the rival into Economy & Finance's own Insolvency (§9 of that document) rather than merely outcompete them for a season. This carries real risk for the aggressor too — sustained below-margin operation is a genuine drain on their own finances, a real gamble that the rival breaks first.
4. **Forced Consolidation** — the rival actually reaches Insolvency, triggering Land Ownership & Real Estate's own forced-sale mechanism (§5 of that document) — and the aggressor is now the single most natural, well-positioned buyer of the very business it just broke, per §7 below.

## 3. Breaking Ranks — Undercutting a Fellow Guild Member

The genuine tension this document exists to formalize: a Notable Business whose owner belongs to a trade Collegium (Collegia & Guilds §2) and whose §2 escalation ladder is being run *against a fellow member of that same guild* is doing something with real, distinct social weight beyond ordinary competition — undercutting an outside rival is simply business; undercutting a guild brother is a real breach of collegium solidarity, carrying its own Dignitas cost within that collegium's own membership (a real, separate reputational hit from Notable Businesses' own ordinary competitive consequences) and a genuine risk of the collegium's own Magister or fellow members responding collectively — social pressure, exclusion from the collegium's own mutual aid, or, in a severe case, an organized collective response mirroring the collegium's own darker political-disruption capacity (Collegia & Guilds §6), now turned against one of its own rather than an outside target.

---

## 4. Cartels and Market-Sharing Agreements

The real, cooperative alternative to §2's own competitive ladder: two or more Notable Businesses in the same trade, often but not always fellow collegium members, can agree to fix prices at a mutually comfortable level or divide a settlement's own customer base by District rather than compete for all of it — a genuine, historically plausible arrangement given how naturally collegium structures already lend themselves to exactly this kind of coordination. This carries a real tradeoff distinct from honest competition: both businesses enjoy more stable, predictable income, but the arrangement is inherently fragile — any participant's own Ambition or Greed (Traits) creates a real, standing temptation to secretly break the agreement and undercut the others while they remain unaware, converting a cartel into exactly the kind of betrayal §3 already describes, now aimed at co-conspirators rather than an honest rival.

---

## 5. Grain and the Real Roman Anxiety About Hoarding

The one place Rome's own government genuinely did concern itself with market manipulation, worth building in with real historical specificity rather than a generic "price gouging is illegal" rule: grain speculation and hoarding during a shortage was a real, deeply feared, and politically volatile act, tied directly to the Cura Annonae anxiety already established in this project's own Latium document (Starting Regions: Italian Heartland §3.2) and to Settlement Demographics' own Grain Dole (§6.3 of that document). A grain-trading Notable Business that deliberately withholds stock during a genuine shortage — betting on prices rising further rather than releasing supply — is engaging in a real, historically specific act Roman crowds and Roman law alike treated with genuine severity: a real risk of mob violence directly against the business and its owner (a Notable Household consequence distinct from an ordinary competitive rivalry), and a real, live Crime & Punishment exposure (that document's own Punishable Offense framework, §3) rather than an ordinary civil Market Dynamics outcome. This document treats grain hoarding as this project's own single most severe form of economic misconduct, deliberately outside the ordinary, survivable rungs of §2's ladder.

---

## 6. Market Entry and Saturation

A settlement's own trade capacity is real and finite, read directly from Settlement Demographics' own Employment Ratio and pop-group sizing rather than a new standalone market-size figure: a new Notable Business entering an already-crowded trade genuinely dilutes the available demand every existing business in that trade competes for, raising the odds that §2's own escalation ladder actually gets triggered somewhere in the field rather than staying purely theoretical. A settlement with real, growing population (Settlement Demographics' own growth trend) can absorb new entrants without necessarily forcing competition; a settlement with flat or declining population turns every new entrant into a genuine, felt threat to the businesses already there.

---

## 7. The Spoils of Victory

What actually happens once §2's own ladder resolves in someone's favor: the winner of a successful Forced Consolidation is this project's own single most natural buyer for the defeated rival's own now-available Property Record (Land Ownership & Real Estate §5), a real acquisition opportunity earned rather than simply offered, and gains real Reputation (Notable Businesses §4) and an improved position against Settlement Demographics' own trade-capacity ceiling (§6 above) by having genuinely reduced the field. A cartel's own successful, undiscovered long-term operation (§4) delivers no such dramatic single payoff, but real, steady, shared prosperity for as long as every participant keeps honoring the arrangement — the two paths' own genuinely different risk-and-reward shapes, matching Design Pillar #1's own "no dominant strategy" commitment directly.

## 8. Cross-System Integration

- **Notable Businesses:** this document's own direct extension of that document's §5 Named Competition — the four-rung ladder (§2) gives that document's own worked example a fuller structure to escalate through.
- **Collegia & Guilds:** §3's own breaking-ranks tension and §4's own cartel mechanic both sit directly on top of that document's own collective-bargaining and Magister/membership structure (§3, §5 of that document).
- **Economy & Finance:** Predatory Pricing and Forced Consolidation (§2) both resolve through that document's own existing Insolvency mechanic (§9 of that document); §6's market-capacity reading uses Settlement Demographics' Employment Ratio directly.
- **Land Ownership & Real Estate:** §7's own acquisition opportunity is that document's own forced-sale mechanism (§5 of that document), now given a concrete, earned trigger condition.
- **Starting Regions: Italian Heartland, Settlement Demographics:** §5's grain-hoarding content ties directly to the real Cura Annonae anxiety already established there and to that document's own Grain Dole mechanic.
- **Crime & Punishment:** grain hoarding (§5) is a real, severe Punishable Offense source, distinct from and more serious than an ordinary Market Dynamics outcome.
- **Traits:** Ambition and Greed are the direct mechanical drivers behind §4's own cartel-breaking temptation.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** a dramatic Forced Consolidation, a discovered cartel, or a real grain-hoarding scandal are all tiered material in their own right.

## 10. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — escalation thresholds between rungs, cartel discovery probability, and market saturation's own exact formula are all unsized.
- **Whether a cartel's own discovery should route through the Scandal system directly** (Scandal §4's own sourceType list) as a formal new source, the same honest addition Notable Businesses made for a business-specific Scandal — this document assumes yes but doesn't formally amend that list itself.
- **Grain hoarding's own mob-violence resolution mechanics.** §5 names the real historical risk directly but doesn't specify how that violence actually resolves — likely reading through Crime & Punishment's own Justice Spectrum (§2 of that document) as an extralegal event, but not explicitly stated here.
- **Cross-settlement price wars.** §2's ladder assumes both businesses sit in the same settlement; whether a price war can meaningfully occur between businesses in different settlements trading the same good isn't addressed.
- **Whether a successfully maintained cartel should generate its own positive Dynasty Chronicle material** the way a dramatic price war does, or whether its own quiet, undiscovered success is deliberately meant to leave no trace — this document leans toward the latter but doesn't state it explicitly.
