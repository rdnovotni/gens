# GENS — System Design: Collegia & Guilds (§6.29, new)
*The organizing structure sitting underneath both Rival Houses and the Curia, resolving an open question that document raised and never closed: "Collegia's actual mechanical depth... left for a dedicated pass." A collegium is Rome's own real answer to the question every other system in this project asks about the elite — how do people organize, pool resources, and pursue collective interest — applied to everyone the cursus honorum and the Curia's own citizenship requirements shut out. Real trade guilds, real burial societies, real neighborhood associations, and a real, live political tension: Rome found collegia genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous at the same time, and both halves of that are this document's own spine.*

## 1. Scope & Role

Politics & Patronage already built the Curia and the cursus honorum — both explicitly closed to Peregrine and Freedman status (Familia §2.5's own legal-status restrictions), and both, even for a citizen, a real climb. Notable Households already gave the settlement's ordinary population real, named faces. What's been missing is the organizing structure those same people actually used, historically, to pursue collective interest the formal political system never offered them: the **collegium** — a real, genuine Roman voluntary association, existing in trade, funerary, religious, and neighborhood forms, sitting alongside Rival Houses (the elite's own organizing structure) as this project's equivalent institution for everyone else.

This document resolves two prior open questions directly: Rival Houses' own deferred "Collegia's actual mechanical depth," and Land Ownership & Real Estate's lighter note that a Collegium can own property without further specification of what a Collegium actually *is*.

---

## 2. Types of Collegia

Four real, historically distinct categories, all coexisting:

- **Collegia Opificum — Trade Guilds.** Organized by craft: potters, weavers, shippers, bakers, and so on, drawn directly from Settlement Demographics' own Opifices and Negotiatores pop groups. A trade guild's own real function was collective bargaining and shared professional standing, not formal price-fixing in the modern sense — its members were competitors as often as collaborators, but shared a real interest in the trade's own reputation and its access to raw materials and buyers.
- **Collegia Funeraticia — Burial Societies.** Real, and genuinely important: a decent burial mattered enormously in Roman religious and social life, and an ordinary person of modest means joining a burial society specifically to guarantee one was a real, common practice (§8).
- **Cult-Specific Collegia.** Organized around a specific deity or foreign cult's own worship — a real, natural home for a household or Character's own foreign-cult affiliation (Religion §7) to exist as a shared, communal practice rather than a purely private one.
- **Collegia Compitalicia — Neighborhood Associations.** Organized around a local crossroads shrine (a *compitum*, sacred to the Lares Compitales) — a real, genuine neighborhood-level social structure, the closest real Roman analogue to a modern civic or block association.

A single Character or Notable Household can plausibly belong to more than one — a potter might hold trade-guild membership for professional standing and burial-society membership for the funerary guarantee, exactly the layered, overlapping affiliation real Romans actually held.

## 3. Membership & Officers

A collegium is a real, structured organization, not merely a shared label:

- **Magister** — the collegium's own head, typically elected or rotating, carrying real local standing within the collegium's own membership even where that standing means nothing to the formal Curia (§9).
- **Quinquennalis** — a real, specifically-attested special officer, tied to the census cycle, responsible for the collegium's own membership rolls and financial standing at that fixed interval.
- **Membership dues**, feeding a shared **Arca** — the collegium's own common treasury, funding mutual aid, the funerary guarantee (§8), and any shared property (§5).
- **A Schola** — a real term for the collegium's own meeting hall, a natural Land Ownership & Real Estate Property Record (that document's own Municipal or Collegium ownership type) where a collegium is established enough to hold one outright.

---

## 4. The Patron Relationship

A real, concrete, and genuinely valuable Politics & Patronage tie: a wealthy patron — often someone entirely outside the trade itself, frequently the player's own household — could sponsor a collegium, gaining real prestige and, more importantly, a real Clientela advantage no other mechanism in this project offers: **an entire bloc of grateful clients acquired in one relationship**, rather than built one Character at a time through Clientela's own ordinary roster-growth pathways (Politics & Patronage §4.1). A collegium patron's own name is displayed with real pride by the collegium itself — a genuine, mutual, and visible arrangement, not a quiet favor.

This is this document's own single clearest mechanical payoff: sponsoring a collegium is a real, efficient alternative path to political capital, trading a real one-time or ongoing cost (funding the Arca, funding a collegium feast or funerary guarantee shortfall) for Influence and Dignitas at a scale an individual Clientela relationship can't match.

---

## 5. Economic Function

A trade collegium's own collective standing genuinely affects Economy & Finance's own Market Dynamics — a well-organized, well-funded guild in a given trade can negotiate better terms collectively than its members could individually, reading as a modest, real thumb on that trade's own wage floor or price stability within the settlement. A collegium holding a Schola or shared workshop/warehouse property (§3, and Land Ownership & Real Estate §2's own Collegium ownership type) gives its members shared infrastructure none of them could individually afford — a real, concrete economic function distinct from its social and political ones.

---

## 6. Political Function — Voting Blocs and Their Darker Edge

A collegium's own membership is a real, organized bloc, and Rome's own real history records both halves of what that could mean. On the legitimate side: a collegium's endorsement is a real, visible show of support in a Curia election (Politics & Patronage §5.5), a genuine bloc of goodwill or opposition a candidate has to reckon with beyond individual Clientela relationships. On the real historical darker side, worth naming rather than glossing over given this project's own standing commitment to frank treatment of difficult material: organized collegium membership was real-historically used for intimidation and outright political violence during the late Republic specifically — organized crowds, sometimes armed, mobilized around collegium structures to disrupt a rival's election or assembly. This document names that real possibility directly as an available, extreme political tool: a household willing to use a collegium this way is running a real, high-risk Coercive Interaction at settlement scale, reading through Crime & Punishment's own Justice Spectrum and Justified/Unjust framework (that document's §2, §4) exactly as any other exercise of raw power would — a collegium-organized disruption with no real Punishable Offense behind it is every bit as "Unjust" as an individual imprisonment would be, just at a much larger, more visible scale.

---

## 7. Legal Status — Licitum and Illicit

A real, genuinely live regulatory tension, not a settled background fact: Rome viewed collegia with real, documented suspicion as potential vehicles for exactly the kind of organized disruption §6 names, and real history records both Julius Caesar and Augustus dissolving collegia they judged politically dangerous, while generally tolerating the oldest, most established, and most clearly non-political ones — funerary and religious associations especially. This document tracks a real **Collegium Licitum** (authorized, legally recognized) versus **Illicit** (unauthorized, operating at real risk) distinction directly:

- A **Licitum** collegium operates openly, can hold property and a Schola without concealment, and its patron relationship (§4) carries no risk of its own.
- An **Illicit** collegium — whether newly formed and not yet authorized, or a formerly licit one caught using §6's own darker political tool once too often — operates under real, standing legal exposure: a sitting magistrate (Politics & Patronage §5, or a provincial governor per §7 of that document) holds real authority to formally dissolve it, and any patron publicly associated with an Illicit collegium carries real Dignitas risk and potential Legal & Court exposure of their own.

---

## 8. The Funerary Function

Worth its own dedicated, emotionally honest treatment rather than folding into §2's category list alone: a decent burial mattered enormously to real Romans, and the Collegia Funeraticia's own real historical purpose — guaranteeing exactly that for members who couldn't otherwise be sure of it — is a genuine, humane function sitting alongside this document's more transactional economic and political content. A Notable Household's own member dying while in good standing with a burial society (Notable Households §4's own death/inheritance handling) resolves with dignity guaranteed regardless of that household's own Wealth tier — a real, concrete mechanical expression of what the collegium actually promised its members, and a natural Dynasty Chronicle-eligible beat where the player's own household is the sponsoring patron behind that guarantee.

---

## 9. A Second Prestige Ladder

The single most important social-mobility point this document makes: for a Freedman or Peregrine-status individual categorically excluded from the Curia and the cursus honorum (Familia §2.5, Politics & Patronage §5.1), **collegium leadership was a real, genuine, respected achievement in its own right** — Magister or Quinquennalis carried real local standing and self-respect precisely because the formal political ladder was closed to exactly the people most likely to hold those titles. This document treats a Notable Household's own head becoming a collegium Magister with the same narrative weight Politics & Patronage gives a citizen's own Decurion seat — a different ladder, not a lesser one, and this project's own clearest illustration that the cursus honorum was never the only real path to a life that mattered.

## 10. Cross-System Integration

- **Rival Houses:** resolves that document's own explicit open question directly — a Collegium is now a real, structured entity a rival gens can patronize, compete over patronage of, or be publicly embarrassed by if one it sponsors turns Illicit (§7).
- **Politics & Patronage:** §4's patron relationship is a genuine, efficient alternative to Clientela's own individual roster-growth; §6's endorsement mechanic feeds directly into that document's own contested-election machinery (§5.5); §7's dissolution authority is a real, concrete exercise of a magistrate's or governor's own standing power.
- **Land Ownership & Real Estate:** the Collegium ownership type (§2 of that document) is fully specified here — a Schola or shared workshop/warehouse is this document's own concrete Property Record.
- **Settlement Demographics:** trade collegia draw directly from the Opifices and Negotiatores pop groups; a collegium's own membership size scales with those groups' own settlement-level totals.
- **Notable Households:** a household's own head is this document's natural Magister or Quinquennalis candidate; a household's own burial-society membership (§8) ties directly to that document's own death/inheritance handling (§4 of that document).
- **Crime & Punishment:** §6's darker political-violence option reads through that document's own Justice Spectrum and Justified/Unjust framework directly, rather than a parallel consequence system.
- **Religion:** a cult-specific collegium (§2) is the natural communal home for a foreign-cult affiliation (§7 of that document) that a private household consecration alone doesn't fully capture.
- **Legal & Court:** a formal Illicit-collegium dissolution case, or a patron's own exposure from association with one, is a real, concrete new case source.
- **Economy & Finance:** §5's trade-guild collective bargaining is a real, modest input into that document's own Market Dynamics wage/price stability.
- **Dynasty Chronicle:** a collegium sponsorship, a Magister's own achievement, or a dramatic Illicit dissolution are all real, tiered material.

## 12. Open Questions

- **All numeric sizing**, per this project's standing convention — Arca balance mechanics, dues rates, the collective-bargaining effect on Market Dynamics, and dissolution-risk thresholds are all unsized.
- **Multiple simultaneous memberships.** §2 notes a Character or Notable Household can plausibly belong to more than one collegium type at once, but doesn't specify any cap or interaction effect between overlapping memberships.
- **Rival House collegium competition.** §10 notes a rival gens can compete for patronage of the same collegium, but the actual resolution mechanic for two houses both seeking to sponsor one collegium isn't specified.
- **Quinquennalis's own actual census-cycle trigger.** §3 names the real office and its census-tied function but doesn't specify how the game's own monthly tick maps onto the real, much longer census interval.
- **Whether a collegium can ever be player-founded from scratch** rather than always pre-existing and joined/sponsored — this document assumes collegia already exist in a settlement by the time the player engages with them, but doesn't address founding an entirely new one.
